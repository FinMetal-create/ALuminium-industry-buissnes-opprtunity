## 1. Executive summary

### The issue that outweighs everything else in this paper

**Indirect cost compensation (ICC) is worth €682–852/t to us in 2031. On 300 kt that is €205–256m a year.**
- The low end uses 75% aid intensity and a flat EUA at the sourced Dec-27 price of €88.7. The high end uses 80% and +4%/yr.
- In 2034 the range is €682–959/t, or **€205–288m a year**. The low case is flat, so its low end does not rise.
- The growth portfolio in this paper earns **€16.2m/yr** of EBITDA in the base case.
- **ICC is therefore 13–16 times the entire growth portfolio.**

**My answer to the Board's obvious question is yes: ICC defence and the no-ICC contingency plan are the paper.** Growth is the appendix. It is funded because each case is gated, cheap before FID, and, for the three that matter, robust if the potlines close.

**Why this holds whatever EEX shows**
- **Without ICC the smelter needs commodity power at €64/MWh or less to break even in cash.** That is true in 2031, and the figure is €65 in 2034. Each €1/MWh above it costs €4.35m a year. This needs no forward reading.
- **The loss disappears only if Cal-31 settles at €64 or below.** That is €9 below the planning curve.
- **The only sourced German forward in this paper is Cal-27, at €140.2/MWh.** It is more than twice the break-even.
- **Every component build of firmed renewable baseload sits above €64.** On the planning curve they cost €73–85/MWh. The €72.8 low end uses a **pan-European hybrid PPA index**, not a German price; the German onshore-wind route is €84.6; an independent build gives €74–93 (Section 5.1). No German industrial PPA price is disclosed, so this comparison rests on builds, not on deals.
- **Only nuclear, Nordic hydro or state support could get below €64.** None is signed.
- **The EEX attachment changes how large the loss is.** It changes whether there is a loss only if Cal-31 is at or below €64. In that case the paper returns to the CEO before circulation.

**What we do about it.** These are decisions, not studies.
1. **The CEO personally owns the ICC-to-2040 campaign, starting now.** It is run jointly with European Aluminium. Budget: **€1.0m over 12 months**. This is one line inside the €2.0m policy, trade-defence and legal budget (Section 8). A precedent exists: in Dec 2025 the Commission widened ICC eligibility ([EU Reporter](https://www.eureporter.co/environment/2025/12/30/commission-amends-ets-state-aid-guidelines-to-tackle-carbon-leakage-for-more-energy-intensive-industries/)).
2. **The no-ICC contingency plan comes to the Board at M9 (July 2027).** It comes *before* we sign 2031–40 power contracts at M12. The options, thresholds and what each needs are in Section 7.4.
3. **Every power contract running past 2030 carries an ICC-lapse clause.** If ICC is not renewed by 31 Dec 2029, we must be able to resell volume or step it down by at least 50%. Otherwise a fixed-price contract becomes a take-or-pay liability on a loss-making smelter.
4. **New capital rule (ICC cliff).** No potroom capex whose post-tax payback runs past 31 Dec 2030 goes ahead before ICC is decided.
   - The PTM retrofit (€12.0m, post-tax payback about 2035) moves to Tier B.
   - Casthouse, scrap and rolling-mill investments are not affected, because they survive a potline closure.
   - There is one safety override (Section 6(b)).
5. **No growth case and no power contract is sized to offset ICC, because none can.**
6. **Funding.** Growth capital is released only through gates and is funded from the 2026–27 spot window. At spot, the realised price is €748/t above normalised (range €733–764), about €224m/yr on 300 kt before hedges [CSO calc]. Growth capital is not funded with debt.

### What the evidence says

**EU supply is short and shrinking.**
- EU-27 primary output was **1.2 Mt in 2025**, down from 2.9 Mt in 2005 ([Mysteel/AL Circle, Jun 2026](https://www.mysteel.net/news/5130043-europes-aluminium-future-demand-rises-primary-output-falls-can-recycling-bridge-the-supply-gap)).
- EU consumption was **13.5 Mt in 2025** ([Bloomberg via SunSirs, Feb 2026](https://www.sunsirs.com/uk/detail_news-30292.html)).
- Primary self-sufficiency is therefore **about 9%** [CSO calc].

**Demand keeps growing.**
- Global demand rises from 86.2 Mt (2020) to **119.5 Mt (2030)** ([CRU for IAI, 2022](https://international-aluminium.org/report-reveals-global-aluminium-demand-to-reach-new-highs-after-covid/)).
- European demand reaches **16 Mt in 2030** and 18 Mt in 2050 ([European Aluminium CAAP, via EU Circular Economy Platform](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/fr/node/10102)). CAAP was published before the energy crisis, so treat it as an upper bound.

**Scrap is the scarce input.**
- The EU exported a record **1.27 Mt of scrap in 2025** ([EUWID, Mar 2026](https://www.euwid-recycling.de/news/maerkte/eu-aluschrottexporte-2025-auf-rekordhoch-270326/)).
- Restrictions on shipments to non-OECD countries apply from 2027 ([European Aluminium, 7 Sep 2026](https://european-aluminium.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/2026-09-07-European-Aluminium-letter-concerning-the-change-in-approach-to-the-urgent-scrap-leakage-problem.pdf)). Our case does not depend on them.

**Power and ICC decide our competitiveness.**
- Every €10/MWh moves cash cost by **€145/t**, or €43.5m/yr [CSO calc: 14.5 MWh/t].
- Without ICC, cash break-even is **€64/MWh**.
- We found **no disclosed €/MWh price for any industrial firmed-baseload contract** in Germany or the EU. The platform is valued as a hedge. The RFP (day 75) produces the price evidence.

**CBAM helps our P&L only a little.**
- On domestic sales the net benefit is **+€22–29/t a year (2027–34)**. The marginal importer (about 1.8 tCO₂/t) is only slightly dirtier than we are (1.55 t).
- Exports carry our full ETS cost with no rebate: **€18/t in 2027, rising to €181/t in 2034** (Section 7.1).

**China has overcapacity, but the surplus leaves as semis, not metal** (Section 7.2).
- China's capacity cap is **45 Mt**. Output was **45.02 Mt in 2025** and is running at **46.6–46.9 Mt for 2026**, about 104% of the cap [CSO calc from NBS via Mining Weekly].
- China is a net *importer* of primary metal: about 45 kt exported and 172 kt imported in Sep 2026 (SMM).
- The surplus leaves as **5.5 Mt of semis exports (2025)**.
- EU imports of Chinese semis:
  - Before the duties, the Commission found 309,853 t of extrusions, 201,928 t of flat-rolled products and 46,595 t of converter foil (all 2019, sourced).
  - For 2025 I estimate **0.19–0.26 Mt (central 0.22 Mt)** from Comtrade values. One sourced 2025 tonnage line (CN 7604 10 90) and a physical cross-check against China's own export tonnages support it (Section 7.2).
  - That implies falls of **at least 39–49%** for flat-rolled and **about 85–92%** for extrusions.
  - No decision depends on the precise figure. Comext replaces it by 28 Oct (attachment 4).
- The China–EU *metal* gap is **€429/t** (range €413–444). All of it is the European premium: SHFE excluding VAT sits US$87/t *above* LME.
- **Converter-foil duties (Reg. 2021/2170) expire on 9 Dec 2026 unless an expiry review is initiated before then.**
  - The fact that decides the outcome is already fixed: either a request was lodged by about 9 Sep 2026 or it was not.
  - It becomes public only at initiation or at expiry. Initiation is normally published in the last 2–6 weeks before expiry, so about 1 Nov to 8 Dec. A notice of expiry can only appear on or after 9 Dec.
  - A blank Official Journal (OJ C) today proves nothing either way. One written answer from European Aluminium settles it (attachment 2).
  - Until then we act as if the duties will lapse (decision 5).
  - On foil CBAM defaults, the price pressure in the gap is **€62–262/t in 2027** and **€0–189/t in 2028**.

**Copper-to-aluminium substitution**
- For equal conductance, 0.49 kg of aluminium replaces 1 kg of copper [CSO calc: 61% IACS; densities 2.70 vs 8.96 g/cm³].
- So aluminium is cheaper on metal whenever the Cu/Al price ratio is above about 2.0x.
- At a ratio of 4.9x, aluminium's metal cost per unit of conductance is about **90% lower**. Installed-cost savings are smaller, because aluminium needs about 1.6x the cross-section.

**What this means for us**
1. **Power and ICC set our competitiveness to 2035, not cell technology.**
2. **CBAM gives primary metal weak protection.**
   - It leaves out indirect emissions.
   - On grid-priced power, ICC leaves **€198/t (2027) to €232/t (2031)** of the carbon in the power price uncovered [CSO calc: 0.72 t/MWh × EUA × 14.5 MWh, less ICC].
   - ICC is paid on a benchmark whoever supplies our power, so no PPA changes it.
3. **Potroom automation must pay back before the ICC cliff.** Casthouse automation must pay back within 5 years, because inert anodes could remove the anode-change task.

**Part A: Power platform (no capex)**

**What we sign**
- 10–15-year contracts for a further 20% of load from 2029, rising to 70% of load from 2031.
- **Price gate:** a weighted firmed price of **no more than €3/MWh above the EEX Phelix DE baseload strip** for the same delivery years on the signing date.
  - For years beyond the last liquid EEX contract, we use that last liquid year.
  - This caps the insurance cost at €3 × 3.05 TWh = **€9.1m/yr** (€30/t).
  - The gate is set relative to the strip on the day we sign, so it holds whatever today's settlements show.
- Every contract running past 2030 carries an ICC-lapse clause: either we may resell volume at market, or contract volume steps down by at least 50% if ICC is not renewed by 31 Dec 2029.
- **No contract is signed before the M9 contingency plan.** The plan sets how much volume the smelter can carry without ICC.
- **Fallback if the gate cannot be met:** if less than 30% of 2029+ load clears the gate by M6, the remaining volume is hedged with EEX baseload futures for the liquid delivery years. The expected cost is also €0.

**Why version 3's €62.5/MWh and €59m/yr stay withdrawn**

- That index is a **pan-European pay-as-produced price**, not a German baseload price.

**Converter-foil timing under Art. 11(2) of [Reg. (EU) 2016/1036](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/1036/oj)**
- A request had to be lodged at least 3 months before expiry, so by about **9 Sep 2026**. That date has passed, and the fact is fixed.
- Initiation must come by **8 Dec 2026**. Duties stay in force during a review. A review normally takes 12 months and at most 15 months.
- Public evidence appears only at initiation, normally in the last 2–6 weeks before expiry (about 1 Nov to 8 Dec), or as a notice of expiry on or after 9 Dec.
- **Whether a request was lodged is not confirmed in any source I could reach.** A blank OJ C on 21 Oct proves nothing either way.
