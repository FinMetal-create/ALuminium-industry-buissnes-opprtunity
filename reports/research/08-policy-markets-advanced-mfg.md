### 1. Policy context: what actually changes our P&L from 2026

**CBAM definitive phase (since 1 Jan 2026).** CBAM now carries a financial obligation ([Reg. (EU) 2023/956](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/956/oj)). The 2025 Omnibus simplification ([Reg. (EU) 2025/2083](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2025/2083/oj), verify number) changed four things:
- Importers bringing in less than **50 t/year** of CBAM goods are exempt. That removes about 90% of importers but keeps about 99% of embedded emissions in scope.
- Only authorised declarants may import above the threshold.
- Certificate sales start in **Feb 2027** for 2026 imports. 2026 certificates are priced at the quarterly-average EUA price, and weekly pricing starts in 2027.
- The first declaration and surrender is due by **30 Sep 2027** ([EC CBAM page](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/carbon-border-adjustment-mechanism_en)).

ETS free allocation is cut in step with CBAM ([Directive 2023/959](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2023/959/oj)). Producers lose 2.5% of it in 2026, 10% in 2028, 48.5% in 2030 and 100% in 2034.

**The critical design flaw for aluminium:** CBAM covers only **direct** emissions for aluminium: anode consumption, PFCs and process heat. Indirect emissions from electricity are excluded. Yet about 75-85% of a coal-powered smelter's footprint (12-16 tCO2e/t in China) comes from power, against ~1.5-2.5 tCO2e/t of direct emissions. CBAM therefore offers EU smelters much less protection than the headline suggests.

Worked example **(est.)** for imported P1020 with 2.0 tCO2e/t direct emissions, a 1.464 t benchmark and EUAs at €75-90:

| Year | Approx. CBAM cost |
|---|---|
| 2026 | ~€40/t |
| 2030 | ~€100/t |
| 2034 | ~€180/t |

Using default values adds a mark-up (10% in 2026 rising to 30% in 2028) and raises the cost further. Scrap carries **zero** embedded emissions under CBAM, which structurally favours recycled-content imports.

In **December 2025** the Commission proposed three further measures (verify final status):
- extending CBAM downstream to about 180 steel- and aluminium-intensive products;
- anti-circumvention rules;
- a temporary mechanism to cover carbon-leakage risk on EU **exports**.

Whether indirect emissions are added to the scope for aluminium is the single largest policy swing factor for EU primary production. European Aluminium is lobbying for it ([european-aluminium.eu](https://european-aluminium.eu/)).

**EU ETS.** EUAs have traded around **€65-85/t** in 2025-26 (verify). As free allocation phases out, our own direct emissions become a rising cost. With ~1.6 tCO2e/t direct, losing 48.5% of free allocation by 2030 costs about **€55-65/t of primary metal (est.)**. Indirect-cost compensation continues under the ETS state-aid guidelines.

**Critical Raw Materials Act** ([Reg. (EU) 2024/1252](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1252/oj)). Bauxite, alumina and aluminium are on both the strategic and critical lists, as are **gallium and scandium**. The 2030 benchmarks are 10% extraction, 40% processing and 25% recycling, with no more than 65% from any single third country. Strategic-project status brings:
- faster permitting (27 months for extraction, 15 months otherwise);
- access to EIB/RESourceEU financing and offtake matching.

**Clean Industrial Deal (Feb 2025) and CISAF state-aid framework (June 2025)** ([CID](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/eu-competitiveness/clean-industrial-deal_en), [CISAF](https://competition-policy.ec.europa.eu/state-aid/clean-industrial-deal-state-aid-framework_en)). CISAF lets member states subsidise up to ~50% of energy-intensive users' power cost for a limited period, on condition that the user invests in decarbonisation. The Affordable Energy Action Plan pushes PPAs and CfDs.

The **Industrial Accelerator Act** (IAA, originally the Industrial Decarbonisation Accelerator Act) adds "Made in Europe" and low-carbon requirements to public procurement and lead markets, using a voluntary low-carbon product label starting with steel, then aluminium. **Final content and timing should be verified.** The direction is clear, though: demand-side pull for **certified low-carbon, EU-made aluminium** in construction, vehicles and grid tenders.

**Russia.** The 16th sanctions package (Feb 2025) bans Russian primary aluminium imports after a phase-out ending around early 2026. This removes roughly 0.3-0.5 Mt/yr of supply from the EU (est.) and tightens the Rotterdam premium.

**US Section 232.** The tariff went to 25% with no exemptions on 12 Mar 2025, then to **50%** on 4 Jun 2025 ([White House proclamation](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/06/adjusting-imports-of-aluminum-and-steel-into-the-united-states/)). About **400+ derivative HTS codes** were added in Aug 2025. The EU-US framework (Aug 2025) did not lift aluminium tariffs; quota talks were ongoing (verify). As a result:
- direct EU semis exports to the US are largely uneconomic;
- the **US Midwest premium** has been at record levels above ~60 c/lb (~$1,300+/t) since mid-2025 (verify);
- some metal and scrap that would have gone to the US is being redirected to Europe.

**Energy.** Large EU industrial consumers pay roughly **€70-110/MWh** on recent forwards (est., verify). A competitive smelter needs about €35-45/MWh. Each **€10/MWh is ~€140-150/t** of aluminium at 14-15 MWh/t. This, not CBAM, is the decisive variable for EU primary production.

### 2. Market context

- **China at its 45 Mt cap.** Output was about 43-44 Mt in 2024 and about 44-45 Mt in 2025, against roughly 72-74 Mt globally ([IAI](https://international-aluminium.org/statistics/primary-aluminium-production/)). Growth is moving to **Indonesia** (Chinese-owned, coal-based; 2-5 Mt pipeline by 2030, est.), the Gulf and India. Chinese domestic supply has stopped growing, which supports LME prices. The flip side is a structural surplus of Chinese **semis and products**, which is exactly what the CBAM downstream extension and anti-circumvention rules target.
- **Prices (verify all).**
  - LME cash: roughly **$2,500-3,000/t** across 2025-26, firming on supply losses such as the Mozal care-and-maintenance around March 2026.
  - **Rotterdam duty-paid P1020 premium:** roughly **$250-450/t**.
  - **Midwest premium:** record highs, as above.
  - **Low-carbon differential** in Europe (Fastmarkets-type assessment): still thin at roughly **$10-40/t**. The real "green premium" is captured bilaterally on branded, recycled-content products (Hydro CIRCAL/REDUXA, Novelis, Speira, Constellium) and in automotive and packaging contracts with Scope 3 targets.
- **EU structural position.** Since 2021, EU27 primary output has fallen to about **2 Mt** (est.), or about 4 Mt including Norway and Iceland. The EU imports **>50%** of its primary needs (est.). Scrap exports are a hot policy topic: the EU exports about **1+ Mt/yr** of aluminium scrap (est.), and the Commission has been weighing export measures (verify).

### 5. So what for our company

1. **Be the "CBAM-proof, IAA-qualified" supplier.** CBAM offers only limited protection for primary metal, but it plus the IAA and the downstream extension create a **compliance and certification moat** for EU-made, low-carbon, high-recycled-content semis. We should:
   - get ASI/EPD/product-passport certification on every product;
   - offer customers CBAM-ready emissions data;
   - price a green premium of €50-150/t bilaterally (est.) instead of waiting for the market differential.
2. **Lobby hard, with numbers, on two points.** First, include indirect emissions in CBAM's aluminium scope. Second, secure CISAF-backed power at €40-50/MWh. Without the power deal, any primary capacity we restart or keep is at risk. Each €10/MWh is worth ~€15m/yr per 100 kt of capacity (est.).
3. **Treat the US as a "build inside the wall" market, not an export market.** At a 50% tariff plus the Midwest premium, US-based recycling or finishing assets earn the premium; exports from the EU do not.
4. **Monetise our alumina and bauxite-residue position for gallium and scandium** under CRMA strategic-project status. This is the only advanced-materials play with policy money, scarcity pricing and a direct link to our assets.
5. **Keep AM, WAAM, foam, Al-air and Al-H2 as an options portfolio** (≤€10-20m over 5 years): licensing, JVs and minority stakes. Do not build plants yet.
6. **Hedge the scrap-policy outcome.** If the EU restricts scrap exports, domestic scrap gets cheaper and our recyclers win. Prepare remelt and sorting capacity now.
