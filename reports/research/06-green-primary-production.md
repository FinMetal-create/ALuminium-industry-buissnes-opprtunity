## Green Primary Production Technologies

*Research swarm, specialist area: next-generation primary aluminium. Status as of 21 Oct 2026. Where a number is my own calculation or estimate, it is marked **[est.]**. Everything else is cited inline.*

### 1. Executive summary

- **Zero-carbon smelting is coming, but not at scale before the mid-2030s.** ELYSIS started its first commercial-size inert-anode cell (450 kA) at Alma, Québec in Nov 2025, and Alcoa still targets first production by 2027 ([Rio Tinto](https://www.riotinto.com/en/news/releases/2025/elysis-achieves-breakthrough-with-commercial-size-cell); [Alcoa 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1675149/000119312526077167/aa-20251231.htm)). Hydro's HalZero only finished building its test facility in Dec 2025 and plans an industrial pilot "towards the end of the decade" ([Hydro](https://www.hydro.com/en/media/on-the-agenda/halzero-zero-emission-electrolysis-from-hydro/)). Carbon capture for smelter off-gas is at low TRL. Brownfield retrofit at scale is a 2032–2040 story.
- **Power is the decisive variable, not process chemistry.** A smelter uses about 14–15 MWh/t ([IAI via AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/europes-aluminium-sector-hit-by-rising-costs-lower-output-and-weak-demand-118732)). Electricity is 30–45% of production cost ([European Aluminium Action Plan, Jan 2026](https://european-aluminium.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/15-01-26-An-Action-Plan-for-European-Aluminium.pdf)). The money in 2026–2030 comes from flexible operation and long-term power contracts. Novel cells come later.
- **2026 changed the supply picture.** Iranian strikes on EGA Al Taweelah and Alba (28 Mar 2026) and the effective closure of Hormuz could remove 3–3.5 Mt of 2026 output (Wood Mackenzie, via [Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/lme-aluminium-nears-four-year-peak-after-iran-attacks-on-gulf-smelters-2026-03-30)). EGA expects up to 12 months to restore output ([AGBI](https://agbi.com/manufacturing/2026/04/ega-expects-12-months-to-restore-output-after-iran-attack)). Mozal went on care and maintenance on 15 Mar 2026 ([South32/Engineering News](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/south32-places-mozal-on-care-and-maintenance-2026-03-16)). CBAM entered its definitive phase on 1 Jan 2026. The Rotterdam duty-paid premium hit records near US$600/t in spring and was still about US$574/t (COMEX) on 1 Oct ([AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/eu-canada-and-asia-aluminium-prices-why-3-386-t-benchmark-can-cost-5-792-t-read-how-regional-premiums-reshaped-markets-120919)). That makes 2026–2028 the best window in a decade to restart or secure EU primary capacity, provided the power is locked in for 10+ years.

**Interpretation.** The IAI 1.5°C pathway needs global primary intensity to fall from 16.1 tCO2e/t (2021) to 11.5 by 2030, 4.2 by 2035 and 2.2 by 2040 ([IAI](https://international-aluminium.org/iai-models-1-5-degree-decarbonisation-scenario-in-a-bid-to-drive-greater-emissions-reductions/)). An EU smelter on low-carbon power is already at about 1.6–1.8 tCO2/t direct and about 4 t cradle-to-gate. Below about 2 t, the remaining gap is anode carbon (about 1.5 t) plus alumina refining (about 1–2 t per tonne of aluminium [est.]). Those two items are exactly what inert anodes and refinery electrification address.

### 3. Inert-anode economics: when does it pay? [est.]

| Item (per t Al) | Carbon anode (today) | Inert anode, retrofit |
|---|---|---|
| Anode cost | about US$140 (old data), realistic US$250–350 incl. 2026 coke/pitch [est.] | about US$128 (RSC, dated) |
| Extra electricity | 0 | +2.5–3.0 MWh, i.e. **+€150–180 at €60/MWh** |
| Direct CO2 | about 1.5–1.7 t; cost rises as ETS free allocation phases out with CBAM (2026–2034) | about 0 (plus O2 by-product) |
| CO2 cost at €75–125/t with no free allocation | **€115–210** | 0 |
| Anode plant capex | about US$160m (greenfield) | US$10–15m |

**Conclusion [est.]:** Before 2030, inert anodes are roughly cost-neutral against carbon anodes in a retrofit. After about 2032, as free allocation falls, they win by about €100–250/t. Two further gains are possible: avoided anode-plant reinvestment, and a "zero-direct-emission" product premium. The vertical-electrode new-cell design narrows the energy penalty to about 0.4 kWh/kg (RSC). The main technical risks are anode life and metal purity at 450 kA, on which there is no public data yet.

### 4. EU smelter closures and restart economics

- **Capacity lost:** about 1.18 Mt closed or curtailed since 2021 ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/110724-european-aluminum-industry-readies-for-cbam)). About 800 kt was still offline in late 2025 ([Hellenic Shipping News](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/?p=1115828)). Speira Rheinwerk has been fully curtailed since 2023.
- **Restarts in 2026:**
  - **Alcoa San Ciprián** (228 kt, Spain): restart completed 7 Apr 2026 after the April 2025 blackout delay ([Alcoa 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001675149/000119312526197447/aa-20260331.htm)). Expected 2025 loss was US$90–110m ([Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/alcoa-sees-up-to-110m-loss-as-spain-aluminium-smelter-restart-delayed-2025-07-14)).
  - **Hydro Slovalco** (75 kt of 175 kt): €100m restart, Q4 2026. Conditional on EU approval of Slovak indirect cost compensation and backed by a 10-yr state hydro contract ([Hydro](https://www.hydro.com/en/global/media/news/2026/slovalco-to-restart-75000-tonnes-of-curtailed-aluminium-capacity-supplying-a-critical-raw-material-for-europe/)). That is **about €1,330 per annual tonne of restart capex [est.]**.
- **Policy support:** The EU Clean Industrial Deal State Aid Framework (CISAF, adopted 25 Jun 2025, runs to 2030) lets member states cut wholesale power prices for energy-intensive users down to a floor of **€50/MWh** for up to 3 years, conditional on decarbonisation reinvestment. Germany's scheme is €3.8bn ([PwC](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/green-light-for-electricity-price-relief-for-energy-intensive-users); [ESG News](https://esgnews.com/eu-adopts-new-state-aid-framework-to-accelerate-clean-industrial-development-through-2030)). European Aluminium is lobbying to extend indirect cost compensation beyond 2030 and to set up Contracts for Difference for low-carbon aluminium.
- **Power benchmark:** Competitiveness needs a 10–20-yr contract around US$40/MWh. Data centres are bidding US$115+/MWh for the same firm power ([oenergetice](https://oenergetice.cz/en/commodity-markets/slovakia-restores-part-of-aluminium-production-electricity-price-will-be-decisive)).

**Restart cash-cost sketch [est., EUR/USD about 1.16]:**

| Item (per t Al) | At €50/MWh | At €90/MWh |
|---|---|---|
| Alumina (1.92 t × about US$370) | about €610 | about €610 |
| Net carbon anode | about €250 | about €250 |
| Power (14.5 MWh) | about €725 | about €1,305 |
| Labour, maintenance, other | about €400 | about €400 |
| **Total** | **about €2,000** | **about €2,560** |

Revenue on 12 Oct 2026 was LME US$3,055 plus about US$500 premium, roughly €3,050/t. **At today's prices both cases make money.** At a "normalised" US$2,500 LME plus US$250 premium (about €2,370), only the €50/MWh smelter survives. A restart is a sound decision only with a 10+ year power contract at or below €50–55/MWh (hydro, nuclear, CISAF-supported). The current price spike should be treated as a payback accelerator, not as the base case.

### 5. Low-carbon brands and premiums

- **Hydro:** REDUXA ≤4.0 (and a 3.0 grade) kg CO2e/kg. CIRCAL 75R cut from 2.3 to 1.9, CIRCAL 100R <0.5 ([AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/norways-low-carbon-aluminium-gets-greener-from-hydro-reduxa-to-alcoa-ecolum-120816)).
- **Alcoa:** EcoLum <4.0 t CO2e/t cradle-to-gate, EcoSource low-carbon alumina, EcoDura ≥50% recycled ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220602005074/en/Alcoa-Assisting-in-the-Development-of-Renewable-Energy-Infrastructure-With-Sales-of-Low-Carbon-EcoLum-to-Hellenic-Cables)).
- **Premium reality check:** Fastmarkets' Europe low-carbon P1020A differential (≤4 t Scope 1+2) was only **US$0–10/t**, with value-added products at **US$20–30/t** (last public data, circa late 2024) ([Fastmarkets](https://fastmarkets.com/insights/fastmarkets-low-carbon-aluminium-differentials-to-bring-transparency-to-emerging-trend)). Premiums are paid mainly where buyers face regulatory or contract pressure ([Eurometal](https://eurometal.net/?p=40634)).
- **CBAM:** importers pay about €15–30/t in 2026 for a 1.6–1.8 tCO2e/t smelter. This rises steeply to 2034 as the CBAM factor phases in ([AL Circle interview](https://www.alcircle.com/news/interview/there-is-real-tightness-of-aluminium-in-europe-but-it-is-a-supply-shock-rather-than-a-cbam-driven-deficit-121425)).

The lesson for a low-carbon brand: it is a ticket to win long-term offtakes, prepayments and co-investment from automotive, packaging and cable OEMs, not a large per-tonne price premium.

### 6. Power procurement is the core competence

Hydro signed three long-term PPAs in 2026 for 2031–2040 delivery: Eviny 0.5 TWh/y, Statkraft 876 GWh/y, plus 0.9 TWh/y in April. These replace contracts expiring end-2030 ([SMM](https://news.metal.com/en/newscontent/104021624-hydro-secures-5-twh-renewable-power-deal-for-low-carbon-aluminium-in-norway); [AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/hydro-locks-in-renewable-power-supply-through-2040-with-statkraft-to-drive-low-carbon-aluminium-growth-in-norway-120893)). EDF's 10-yr nuclear contract replaced ARENH at Dunkerque. **The 2030–2031 contract cliff is a sector-wide event.** Every EU smelter needs to re-contract roughly 2–4 TWh per 200 kt within the next four years, while competing with data centres.

### 7. So what for our company

1. **Do now (2026–2028), low regret:** retrofit 1–2 potlines for ±20–25% power modulation (Trimet/EnPot heat-exchanger route, about €30–40m per line). Then monetise it: aFRR/mFRR and interruptibility revenue, cheaper "shaped" PPAs that follow solar and wind profiles, and a stronger case for CISAF and indirect cost compensation support. Flexibility is what lets a smelter compete with data centres for renewable power.
2. **Lock power before the 2030 cliff:** pursue 10–15-yr hybrid contracts (wind/solar PPA plus nuclear contract plus CISAF floor) and consider a minority equity stake in generation. Without power at or below €55/MWh, no green technology investment makes sense.
3. **Opportunistic restart or M&A of curtailed EU capacity:** about 800 kt is offline. Restart capex is about €1,300/t (Slovalco benchmark). Do it only with long-term power in hand. Today's US$500–600/t premium and the Gulf outage could repay a restart in about 2–3 years [est.].
4. **Secure a seat at the inert-anode table:** negotiate with ELYSIS for an EU licence or host-site position for a 2030–2032 retrofit line. Combine this with EU Innovation Fund grants and an OEM offtake/prepay. Our next anode-plant reinvestment decision should be explicitly tested against the inert-anode timeline.
5. **Low-carbon alumina:** our Scope 3 alumina footprint is the next bottleneck for ≤2 t products. Options are offtake from refineries going electric (Alunorte-type e-boilers) or a JV on e-boiler/MVR retrofit at an EU or Atlantic refinery.
6. **Don't bet on:** HalZero-type chloride routes (post-2035, Hydro-proprietary), hydrogen calcination (hydrogen cost too high), or SMRs before 2035.
