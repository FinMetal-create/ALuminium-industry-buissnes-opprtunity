### 1. Why this matters now

Scrap is becoming the scarce input in European aluminium. Energy is no longer the only constraint. Three forces meet between 2026 and 2030:

1. **A carbon price on the alternative.** CBAM entered its definitive phase in January 2026. Recycled metal carries about **0.5 t CO2e/t**, against roughly **16 t CO2e/t** for the global average of primary metal (coal-heavy Chinese, Indian and Gulf grids) **[est., IAI benchmark]**. EU primary is about 6-7 t **[est.]**. Each tonne of post-consumer scrap kept in Europe therefore avoids about 15 t of CO2 against the imported primary it replaces.
2. **OEM demand for low-carbon metal.** Automotive, packaging and façade customers now buy against product carbon footprints. Examples are Hydro CIRCAL (≥75% post-consumer scrap) and Novelis and Constellium closed-loop sheet. Low-carbon and recycled-content premiums exist but move with the market **[est.: EUR 50-300/t over standard P1020 billet/sheet, product-dependent]**.
3. **Scrap is leaving Europe.** European Aluminium reports that EU scrap exports hit a record **1.27 Mt in 2025, about 50% above 2019**, and rose another **3% year-to-date to April 2026** ([AlCircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/eu-delays-aluminium-scrap-export-curbs-till-september-will-india-seize-the-opportunity-120070); [Engineering News / Reuters](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/eu-aluminium-scrap-export-curbs-delayed-until-september-group-and-sources-say-2026-06-26)). FACE estimates the outflow at about 1.2 Mt/yr, or **about 25% of Europe's total recycling capacity**, which implies roughly 4.8 Mt of EU recycling capacity ([AlCircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/europes-aluminium-scrap-policies-strengthen-recycling-but-leave-export-challenge-unresolved-119804)). India is the largest buyer, at about **383 kt**, followed by Thailand and Pakistan. Exports to the US also rose sharply in 2025 after the US tariff changes ([AlCircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/europe-eyes-retaliation-over-scrap-leakage-to-us-115253)).

Over the long term, European Aluminium's Circular Aluminium Action Plan expects that **about 50% of European aluminium demand could be met from post-consumer recycling by 2050**. That would cut up to **39 Mt CO2/yr** ([EU Circular Economy Platform](https://circulareconomy.europa.eu/platform/en/knowledge/circular-aluminium-action-plan-strategy-achieving-aluminiums-full-potential-circular-economy-2030)). End-of-life scrap volumes in Europe should rise by about **2-3% a year to 2040 [est.]**. Products made in the 2000s-2010s are now reaching end of life: cars at 15-20 years, buildings at 40-60 years, packaging in under one year.

### 2. Policy: the scrap export fight

- **November 2025:** Commissioner Šefčovič announced a measure on scrap exports. A 15% export duty was floated ([AlCircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/eu-proposes-15-tax-on-aluminium-scrap-exports-to-secure-recycled-metal-supply-120114); [Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2025-11-18/eu-plans-curb-exports-aluminum-scrap)).
- **Mid-2026:** The measure slipped from Q2 to September 2026. The duty was then **dropped**. The Commission is instead preparing a **delegated act under the Waste Shipment Regulation that restricts waste and scrap exports to non-OECD countries, applying from 2027**. Current and prospective FTA partners would be exempted, which could include India. The restriction could cover up to **about 950 kt (about 75%) of exports** before those exemptions ([AlCircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/eu-aluminium-scrap-export-measure-delayed-as-commission-shifts-from-duty-to-non-oecd-restrictions-121153); [Hellenic Shipping News](https://www.hellenicshippingnews.com/european-aluminium-irked-by-delay-replacement-of-scrap-export-duty/)).
- **Industry is split.** European Aluminium, representing producers and remelters, wants strong curbs. Recycling Europe, representing scrap traders and shredders, opposes them.
- **Other rules pushing toward circularity [est., from regulatory knowledge]:**
  - The **PPWR** requires deposit-return schemes for single-use metal beverage containers, with a 90% separate-collection target by 2029. This adds UBC volume in markets that do not yet have deposit schemes.
  - The **Battery Regulation** introduces a digital battery passport from February 2027.
  - The **ESPR** brings Digital Product Passports.
  - The **ELV Regulation** revision brings dismantling, recycled-content and vehicle-passport rules. Aluminium-specific content targets are still under debate.

**Read-across:** even a diluted restriction is likely to push some hundreds of kt of scrap back into the EU market from 2027 onward. That would widen scrap discounts for EU melters. Do not plan on it, though. The FTA exemptions may leave the Indian route open, and that is the biggest single flow.

### 3. Where the value sits in the scrap chain

| Step | Typical economics **[est.]** | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Collection / merchant trading | EUR 20-60/t gross margin | Fragmented and relationship-driven, so a good buy-and-build target |
| Shredding plus sensor sorting (eddy current, XRT, XRF, LIBS, AI vision) | Upgrades mixed scrap (Zorba/Twitch) by EUR 150-400/t | This is where the "wrought vs cast" value is unlocked |
| Remelt / refining to cast alloys (rotary or tilting furnaces) | Conversion cost EUR 200-350/t; margin highly cyclical | Squeezed in 2025 because Asian buyers outbid EU refiners for scrap |
| Remelt to wrought (sheet ingot or billet with high post-consumer content) | Total margin EUR 300-700/t above the scrap cost, including the green premium | Needs clean, sorted, alloy-specific scrap |
| Dross / salt-slag recovery | Recovers 40-70% of the metal in dross; avoids landfill fees | Zero-waste and permit value |

**Recycled vs primary:**
- Recycling uses about 5% of the energy of primary (about 0.7 MWh/t vs about 14-15 MWh/t) **[est., IAI benchmark]**.
- Capex per tonne of recycling capacity is about EUR 400-1,000/t, against more than EUR 5,000-7,000/t for a greenfield smelter **[est.]**.
- At EU power prices of EUR 60-90/MWh, a tonne of recycled metal can earn **EUR 300-600/t more contribution** than marginal EU primary, provided scrap can be bought at a 10-20% discount to LME plus premium **[est.]**.
- The binding constraint is access to scrap, not plant capacity. FACE's figure implies EU recycling assets are underused while scrap is being exported.

### 4. Technology: sorting, robotics and alloy-to-alloy recycling

- **Sensor-based sorting** is now commercial. Hydro runs **HySort** (LIBS-based) at its Alumetal plant in Nowa Sól, Poland, from 2025 ([search summary of Hydro/Alumetal coverage](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/norsk-hydro-hydro-moves-to-finalize-integration-of-recycling-company-alumetal-ce7d5bdfd881f42d)). Speira says it operates "Europe's most modern UBC sorting plant" ([AlCircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/speira-commits-40-million-to-expand-rheinwerk-aluminium-recycling-capacity-aiming-for-significant-carbon-reduction-111265)). Vendors include TOMRA, Steinert and Sesotec, plus US AI-vision and robotic pickers such as Sortera and AMP **[est.]**.
- **Robotics in disassembly.** Robotic dismantling of EV battery packs and of cars before shredding raises the purity of wrought-alloy streams, such as 6xxx crash structures and battery trays. This is where the CEO's robotics interest meets recycling: robotic cells for battery-pack opening and alloy-specific pick-and-place sorting.
- **The "cast scrap sink" problem (2030-2040).** Today, much mixed post-consumer scrap is downcycled into high-silicon cast alloys for ICE engine blocks and gearboxes. As BEVs displace ICE, that sink shrinks while mixed scrap volumes grow. The result could be a surplus of hard-to-use scrap and price dislocation **[est., academic consensus, e.g. Modaresi & Müller]**. Players that can sort scrap by alloy, refine it, or design alloys that tolerate scrap will capture the spread.
- **Mega/giga-casting** of structural parts with recyclable, scrap-tolerant alloys is a new outlet for secondary metal. BEVs use about 200-250 kg of aluminium per vehicle, against about 180 kg for an ICE car, with battery enclosures at 60-100 kg **[est., Ducker-type benchmarks]**.

### 6. Competitive landscape (Europe)

- **Hydro:** Doubled post-consumer scrap use from about 250 kt to more than 500 kt/yr by 2025. Acquired Alumetal (Poland and Hungary, cast alloys). Torija (Spain) is a greenfield plant recycling up to 70 kt of scrap into 120 kt of extrusion ingot, starting in 2026. Székesfehérvár (Hungary) has 90 kt of capacity. Wrexham (UK) is a remelt plant of about 103 kt ([AlCircle](https://alcircle.com/news/hydro-hosts-groundbreaking-ceremony-for-its-recycling-plant-in-torija-spain-113560); [Aluminium Today](https://aluminiumtoday.com/news/hydro-opens-new-aluminium-recycling-plant-in-hungary)).
- **Novelis:** Average recycled content about 60% **[est.]**. Expanding UBC capacity in the UK.
- **Speira:** Says it aims to become Europe's leading recycler. Rheinwerk expansion under way ([AlCircle](https://alcircle.com/news/speira-strategically-turning-europe-into-an-aluminium-recycling-hub-investing-millions-of-dollars-in-new-capacities-111108)).
- **Constellium:** Announced 60 kt/yr of extra European recycling capacity in 2021 ([AlCircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/constellium-to-invest-in-adding-60-000-tonnes-per-annum-aluminium-recycling-capacity-in-europe-62446)). Runs closed loops with automotive and packaging customers.
- **Secondary specialists:** Raffmetal, Trimet, Real Alloy Europe and Befesa (salt slag) **[est.]**.

The integrated majors are already moving. A mid-to-large player has to compete for scrap, not only for customers.

### 8. So what for our company

1. **Treat scrap security as strategic, on a par with power purchase agreements.** Lock in scrap through closed-loop contracts, ownership of collection, and tolling deals. Do this before the 2027 export rules change the market. If the restriction comes through, early movers with sorting capacity capture the wider discount. If it is diluted, owning supply is the hedge.
2. **Move up the sorting curve.** The money is in turning mixed scrap into alloy-specific feedstock, which is a technology and robotics problem. That also protects us against the post-ICE surplus of cast-alloy scrap.
3. **Sell carbon, not just metal.** Use CBAM and OEM targets for Scope 3 emissions to sell certified high-recycled-content products with digital passports, at a premium.
4. **Use our primary smelting base as an advantage.** Primary metal is needed to sweeten and dilute recycled melts to meet wrought specifications. An integrated player can blend more post-consumer scrap than a pure remelter.
5. **Lobby jointly with European Aluminium** for an export restriction that actually bites, with as few FTA exemptions as possible. Plan the business case so that it works without one.
