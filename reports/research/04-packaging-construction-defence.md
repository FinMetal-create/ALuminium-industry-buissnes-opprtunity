## Packaging, Construction, Aerospace & Defence

*Research cut-off: 21 Oct 2026. Where I could not find a published figure I give my own estimate and mark it **[EST]**. Several primary sites (european-aluminium.eu, constellium.com, alcircle.com, argusmedia.com) could not be reached directly during this research. Their figures are taken from search-result extracts and syndicated copies, so check them against the originals before using them in board material.*

### 1. Executive summary

These four segments split into two groups:

- **Packaging (cans, foil) and building & construction** make up most of European aluminium tonnage, but margins are thin (roughly USD 300-400/t EBITDA at a rolling mill). Both are being reshaped by regulation: PPWR, deposit-return schemes, EPBD whole-life carbon rules and CBAM.
- **Aerospace and defence** are small in tonnage but earn 4-6x the margin per tonne. Since 2025 they also carry a political argument for security of supply, backed by money (EU defence spending of EUR 418 bn in 2025) and by "buy-European" rules (SAFE requires 65% EU/EEA/Ukraine content).

2026 has made the security-of-supply case much stronger. The Iran war and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz took Gulf smelters offline or curtailed them (Qatalum at 648 kt nameplate, Alba lines 1-3, damage at EGA). LME aluminium hit USD 3,418/t in March 2026, the highest since April 2022 ([ING via FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/news/aluminium-middle-east-outages-tighten-supply-ing-202603311400), [Hydro/Qatalum release](https://www.inderes.dk/en/releases/norsk-hydro-qatalum-maintaining-scaled-down-aluminium-production-1)). The Rotterdam duty-paid premium went to a record of about USD 595/t in May 2026, driven by CBAM (live since 1 Jan 2026), the Grundartangi outage and Hormuz freight and insurance costs ([AL Circle/IMA](https://ima-api.org/eu-canada-and-asia-aluminium-prices-why-3386-t-benchmark-can-cost-5-792-t-read-how-regional-premiums-reshaped-markets/)). Project Blue now expects a global deficit of about 900 kt in 2026 and longer lead times for aerospace components ([Mining Weekly, Sep 2026](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/conflict-driven-supply-constraints-impair-aerospace-manufacturing-stability-2026-09-11)). **EU-based, traceable, low-carbon metal is now a reason customers buy, not just a marketing claim.**

### 2. Packaging: regulation-driven, scrap-advantaged, high utilisation

**Demand.** Beverage cans keep gaining share in Europe. Ardagh Metal Packaging grew European shipments by about 2% in 2025, guides 3-4% long-term growth, and says the industry runs at "high 90s" utilisation with some can sizes "particularly tight". It is adding capacity in Spain and the UK ([AMP Q4-25 presentation](https://ir.ardaghmetalpackaging.com/_assets/_8d473d680ba296f51770501a125b950a/ardaghmetalpackaging/db/1085/15640/presentation/Ardagh+Metal+Packaging+S.A.+Q4+2025+Earnings+Presentation+260226.pdf), [AMP earnings call](https://www.aol.com/articles/ardagh-metal-packaging-ambp-earnings-152150379.html)). Europe drinks about 93 bn canned servings a year (market-research estimate, [GII](https://www.giiresearch.com/report/moi2121935-europe-metal-cans-market-share-analysis-industry.html)). Per capita, Europe still uses far fewer cans than North America, which leaves structural room to grow.

**Recycling and regulation.**
- The European can recycling rate reached a record **76.3% in 2023**. Volumes placed on the market rose 4% and recycled tonnes rose 7% ([Metal Packager, Feb 2026](https://metalpackager.com/2026/02/aluminium-beverage-can-recycling-reaches-record-76-3-across-europe/)). The 2022 figure was 74.6%, or about 580 kt recycled ([Packaging Europe](https://www.packagingeurope.com/report-states-record-580000-tonnes-of-aluminium-recycled-from-beverage-cans.md)). That implies roughly 0.8 Mt of can aluminium placed on the EU+ market each year **[EST]**.
- Countries with deposit-return schemes reach about 92%, and Germany and Finland about 99% ([Packaging Europe/CIAL](https://www.packagingeurope.com/italy-reaches-928-recycling-rate-for-aluminium-beverage-cans/)).
- **PPWR** sets aluminium packaging recycling targets of 50% (2025) and 60% (2030). It makes deposit-return schemes for metal beverage containers mandatory by **1 Jan 2029**, with a 90% collection target, and requires all packaging to be recyclable by 2030 ([Reloop](https://www.reloopplatform.org/ppwr/)).
- The risk is that PPWR reuse targets from 2030 may not exempt cans ([Brauwelt](https://brauwelt.com/en/topics/packaging/648994-new-eu-packaging-regulation-–-possibilities-and-challenges-for-packaging-waste)).
- The upside is that rolling deposit-return schemes out to the remaining roughly 25% of the market that lacks them would release an estimate of **+120-150 kt/yr of clean used-beverage-can (UBC) scrap** in the EU by 2030 **[EST]**: 0.8 Mt × (90% − 76%), plus market growth.

**Plastic substitution.** Bain expects substrate substitution to accelerate as PPWR deadlines approach ([Bain 2026](https://www.bain.com/insights/battle-of-the-substrates-paper-and-packaging-report-2026/)). Foil grows steadily, but thinner gauges eat into tonnage growth, and post-consumer recycled content in foil stays low ([EAFA interview](https://www.alcircle.com/news/interview/al-circle-x-goksal-gungor-from-eafa-roller-group-packaging-will-continue-to-be-the-primary-driver-of-demand-for-aluminium-foil-in-europe-116642)).

**Competitor moves.** Speira commissioned a **EUR 40 m, about 60 kt/yr** UBC recycling furnace at Rheinwerk (Grevenbroich) in October 2026, with Red Bull as anchor customer, plus storage for 6,500 t of can scrap ([Metal Packager](https://metalpackager.com/2025/09/speira-circular-can-business/), [AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/60kt-a-year-red-bull-backs-speiras-40m-aluminium-recycling-furnace-at-rheinwerk-121519)). That works out to roughly **EUR 650/t of annual capacity**, a useful benchmark.

**Scrap policy.** EU scrap exports hit a record **1.27 Mt in 2025**, about 50% above 2019. The Commission has dropped the planned export duty and is preparing a restriction on non-OECD exports under the Waste Shipment Regulation, expected to apply from 2027 ([AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/eu-aluminium-scrap-export-measure-delayed-as-commission-shifts-from-duty-to-non-oecd-restrictions-121153), [Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/eu-aluminium-scrap-export-curbs-delayed-until-september-group-and-sources-say-2026-06-26)). That should push more scrap into EU remelters.

**Margin reality.** Constellium's Packaging & Automotive Rolled segment made USD 353 m adjusted EBITDA on 1,086 kt in 2025, about **USD 325/t** ([Constellium FY25](https://constellium.com/news/constellium-reports-strong-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results)). The money in packaging comes from **scrap spread and utilisation**, not from product premium.

### 3. Building & construction: largest extrusion outlet, cyclical trough, carbon rules coming

**Volumes are flat to slightly down.** European extrusion demand is about 3.48 Mt (2025), rising to 3.56 Mt (2026), with a CAGR of about 2.4% to 2035. **Construction, at about 1.52 Mt, is the largest outlet but is forecast at −1.3% in 2026**, followed by about 1.3-1.6% CAGR to 2035 ([AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/europes-aluminium-extrusion-demand-set-to-grow-at-2-42-cagr-through-2035-which-end-use-sectors-will-drive-it-120950), [Aluminyum Yapi](https://www.aluminyumyapi.com/en/news/europes-aluminium-extrusion-demand-set-to-grow-at-242-cagr-through-2035_533)).

**Overcapacity is producing distressed assets.** Hydro announced five European extrusion plant closures in November 2025 and plans to close Lucé (France) in 2026 ([Yieh](https://yieh.com/en/News/norsk-hydro-plans-to-close-french-extrusion-plant-in-2026/159462)). Buyers can expect cheap press assets and customer books.

**Carbon rules are the structural change.**
- The recast EPBD requires a **life-cycle GWP calculation from 1 Jan 2028 for new buildings over 1,000 m², and for all new buildings from 1 Jan 2030**.
- Member States must submit roadmaps for national GWP limit values by 1 Jan 2027. The common calculation method is set in Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/52 ([CIF](https://cif.ie/2026/02/12/epbd-current-state-of-play/), [Boverket](https://www.boverket.se/globalassets/publikationer/dokument/2026/regulation-on-life-cycle-gwp-and-limit-values-for-the-climate-impact-of-buildings-part-1.pdf)).

This makes verified EPDs and low-carbon billet a specification requirement. Today's benchmarks are Hydro REDUXA at ≤4.0 kg CO2e/kg and CIRCAL (≥75% post-consumer) at ≤2.3 kg CO2e/kg ([Hydro](https://hydro.com/en/aluminium/industries/building--construction/building-profiles)). Facades and windows are where aluminium faces competition from PVC and timber on embodied carbon, so a product at **≤2 kg CO2e/kg billet** is the defensive moat.

**Renovation.** The renovation wave (EPBD zero-emission building stock by 2050) supports replacement windows and façade retrofit more than new-build. That favours system houses and fabricators over commodity billet **[EST]**.

### 4. Aerospace: premium margins and a multi-year ramp

**Build rates are rising.**
- Airbus delivered 351 aircraft in H1 2026 and targets about **870 deliveries in 2026** and **more than 1,100 a year by 2029**.
- A320 rate 70-75 is due by end-2027, then stable at 75. A220 is going to 13/month (2028) and A350 to 12/month (2028). The backlog is over 9,000 aircraft ([Airbus H1-26](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-07-airbus-reports-half-year-h1-2026-results), [AFM](https://afm.aero/airbus-targets-more-than-1100-deliveries-by-2029)).
- Airbus forecasts more than 42,000 new large aircraft over 2024-2044. CRU expects **North American plus European aerospace rolled-products demand to grow 8.5% a year over 2025-2030** ([Constellium 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1563411/000156341126000057/cstm-20251231.htm)).

**Margins are 5-6x packaging.**
- Constellium A&T made USD 339 m EBITDA on 207 kt in 2025, about **USD 1,640/t**.
- In Q2 2026 it made USD 135 m on 65 kt, about **USD 2,080/t**, with shipments up 21% year on year ([Constellium Q2-26](https://finviz.com/news/373875/constellium-reports-strong-second-quarter-and-first-half-2026-results-including-record-segment-adjusted-ebitda-raises-full-year-2026-guidance), [Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/constellium-q2-earnings-call-highlights)).
- Constellium is qualifying its third Airware (Al-Li) casthouse at Issoire, with ramp-up in 2027.

**Al-Li** is a market of about USD 2.3-2.4 bn (2025) growing at 6-7% a year, mostly 2xxx-series. Constellium holds about 20% of it (market-research, low confidence: [DataM](https://www.datamintelligence.com/research-report/aluminum-lithium-alloys-market)).

**Barriers to entry are very high.** Qualification takes 3-7 years, thick-plate stretching and heat-treat assets are needed, and the main suppliers (Constellium, Novelis/ex-Aleris Koblenz, AMAG, Arconic, Kaiser) hold long-term agreements with the OEMs.

**Aerospace is also a scrap opportunity.** With a buy-to-fly ratio of about 8-10:1 on machined plate, roughly 80-90% of purchased plate becomes high-value, alloy-segregated swarf and chips **[EST, industry rule of thumb]**. Closed-loop recovery of that scrap is a realistic entry point for a non-incumbent.

**Space** is small in tonnes but strategic. Ariane 6 uses AA2195 Al-Li cryogenic tanks built by MT Aerospace (about 10% of the launcher), with Constellium material support ([ESA](https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Space_Transportation/Ariane/Ariane_6_made_in_Germany), [ASM](https://asm.confex.com/asm/aero17/webprogram/Paper43328.html)). IRIS² and the new-space launchers add demand for 2219/2195/7xxx plate, forgings and AM powders, probably **<5 kt/yr in the EU through 2030 [EST]**, at very high EUR/kg.

### 5. Defence: money, mandates and "made in Europe"

**The spending surge is real.**
- **EU-27 defence spending was EUR 418 bn in 2025** (+20%, 2.2% of GDP). It is projected at EUR 454 bn in 2026 and up to EUR 547 bn by 2029.
- **Equipment procurement was EUR 115 bn in 2025** ([EDA Defence Data 2025-26](https://eda.europa.eu/news-and-events/news/2026/07/16/eu-defence-spending---418-billion-in-2025--projected-to--454-billion-in-2026)).
- NATO's Hague target is 3.5% of GDP core defence plus 1.5% related spending by 2035 ([OSW](https://www.osw.waw.pl/en/publikacje/osw-commentary/2025-06-26/nato-summit-hague-trumps-return-and-a-two-component-5-gdp)).
- ReArm Europe/Readiness 2030 targets about EUR 800 bn, including **SAFE at EUR 150 bn**. Poland and Romania (EUR 16.7 bn) have the largest SAFE allocations ([AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/romania-boosts-defence-industry-with-2-5b-from-eu-safe-payment-120960)).

**SAFE content rule.** At least **65% of component value must be EU/EEA/Ukraine**, and nothing may come from hostile third countries ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/publication/security-action-for-europe-legal-architecture-implementation-and-private-capital-s-opening), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Action_for_Europe)). For semis this effectively favours European mills. NATO listed aluminium among its 12 defence-critical raw materials (Dec 2024). European Aluminium's July 2025 paper asks for the value chain to be treated as an EU security priority ([European Aluminium](https://european-aluminium.eu/news_events/position-paper-the-strategic-role-of-aluminium-in-europes-aerospace-and-defence-sectors/)).

**Where aluminium goes in defence:**
- **Armour plate**: 5083-H131 for fragmentation; 7039/7020/7017 for armour-piercing threats with less spall ([Total Materia](https://www.totalmateria.com/articles/application-to-aluminum-armor-systems/), [Constellium](https://www.constellium.com/defense)). AMAG markets AL4 Defense plate ([Alu Report](https://www.alureport.com/en/issues/issue-01-26/article/amag-al4-r-defense-kopie)).
- **Military trucks and IFV sub-components**: Salzburger Aluminium Group supplies Rheinmetall's HX2/HX3 and Lynx ([Industriemagazin](https://industriemagazin.at/ruestung/wie-die-salzburger-aluminium-group-ihr-defence-geschaeft-ausbaut/)).
- **Naval superstructures and patrol craft**: 5083/5383 marine plate, e.g. Speira VIA Maris. Elval is positioning for the EDA's European Combat Vessel family ([Speira](https://experience.speira.com/speira-via-maris/), [ElvalHalcor](https://www.elvalhalcor.com/newsletter-articles/elval-positions-aluminium-solutions-at-the-heart-of-europe-s-next-generation-nav/)).
- **Other uses**: missile and rocket-motor cases, air-defence launchers, bridging, shelters and containers, aircraft plate.

**Drones are a big story but small tonnage.** Ukraine built more than 4 M drones in 2025 and expects about 7 M in 2026, and the EU wants "millions per year" by 2030 ([Free Malaysia Today/Reuters](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/world/2026/03/12/from-kyiv-to-uk-ukrainian-drone-production-spans-europe), [DroneXL](https://dronexl.co/2025/07/05/eu-millions-of-drones-by-2030/)). At 0.2-1 kg Al per FPV or loitering drone that is only about **1-7 kt/yr [EST]**. Larger UAVs, motors and heat sinks add some volume.

**Sizing [EST].** I could not find a published tonnage for European defence aluminium. My working estimate is **150-250 kt/yr of semis today**, which is well under 1% of equipment spend by value. If metal intensity holds, that grows to **300-450 kt/yr by 2030-2035**. Plate and hard-alloy extrusions earn aerospace-like premiums: an estimated USD 1,000-2,500/t EBITDA, based on the Constellium A&T margin, which includes defence. The US already writes large multi-year plate contracts, e.g. Reliance/AMI's up-to-USD 654 m IDIQ for Lockheed/F-35 plate processing ([Reliance](https://s206.q4cdn.com/385653838/files/doc_news/Reliance-Inc--Subsidiary-AMI-Metals-Inc--Awarded-Significant-Government-Infrastructure-and-Defense-Projects-2026.pdf)). Europe has no equivalent framework yet. That gap is the opening.

### 7. So what for our company

1. **Shift the mix toward plate and hard alloys.** Every 10 kt moved from can or commodity sheet (about USD 325/t) into defence or aerospace plate (USD 1,500-2,000/t) adds about **USD 12-17 m EBITDA [EST]**. Start with defence armour and marine plate. It qualifies faster (12-24 months against national and NATO standards) than Airbus or Boeing material (3-7 years), and SAFE's 65% rule protects it.
2. **Sell security of supply as a product.** After Hormuz and CBAM, OEMs and governments will pay for **guaranteed EU-origin metal**: take-or-pay frameworks, strategic stock held on our sites, and traceable smelter-to-semi supply with carbon below 4 kg CO2e/kg. Treat this as a pitch to KNDS, Rheinmetall, BAE Hägglunds, Patria, Naval Group, Fincantieri, TKMS and Airbus Defence & Space.
3. **Win the scrap race now.** Deposit-return schemes everywhere by 2029 and non-OECD export curbs from 2027 will release clean UBC and aerospace scrap. Whoever holds remelt capacity and long-term brand and deposit-scheme contracts gets the scrap spread.
4. **Use construction defensively.** Don't add capacity. Buy distressed extrusion presses cheaply, and make EPD-certified billet at ≤2 kg CO2e/kg ready for 2028.
5. **Partner on aerospace rather than build it.** Supplying low-carbon remelt and primary ingot to incumbents, and closing the loop on aero swarf, gives exposure to 8.5% growth without a decade of qualification.

**Main risks:**
- A ceasefire or Gulf restart that normalises premiums.
- PPWR reuse rules hitting cans.
- A defence procurement bottleneck, since most buying is still national (collaborative buying is only 24% of equipment spend).
- EU power prices.
- US tariff and US-domestic sourcing rules, such as DFARS specialty-metal clauses.
