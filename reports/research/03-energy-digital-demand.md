## Energy Transition, Grids & Data Centres

*Research swarm, specialist area: energy transition, grids and digital infrastructure. Date: 21 Oct 2026. Numbers marked **[est.]** are my own calculations or assumptions, not published figures.*

### 1. Executive summary

- **The grid is the biggest and most reliable new demand pool for aluminium in Europe, bigger than solar or data centres.** The EU's 2023 Grid Action Plan put grid investment needs at **€584bn by 2030** ([EC](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/publications/eu-action-plan-grids_en)). The December 2025 **EU Grids Package (COM(2025) 1005)** raised the 2040 figure to about **€1.2tn**, of which **€730bn is for distribution** ([HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/energy/2025-posts/the-eu-grids-package-a-blueprint-for-europes-future-energy-infrastructure); [Elia](https://www.eliagroup.eu/press/2025/12/20251212_european-commitment-to-grids)). Eurelectric's *Grids for Speed* study finds the EU+Norway distribution grid must grow from **~10m km to 16.8m km of conductor by 2050**. That means roughly **262,000 km of new conductor a year** and **twice as many transformers** ([Grids for Speed](https://www.tos.lth.se/fileadmin/tos/docs/Grids-for-Speed_Report.pdf); [Eurelectric deck](https://powersummit2024.eurelectric.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Technologies-supporting-grids-for-speed.pdf)).
- **Switching from copper to aluminium is now a structural trend, not a cyclical one.** In mid-October 2026, LME copper traded at about **$14,400/t** and aluminium at about **$2,940/t**, a **copper-to-aluminium price ratio of about 4.9x** ([S&P Global, 15 Oct 2026](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/pricing-benchmarks/our-methodology/corrections/20261015)). Industry starts to switch at a ratio of 3.5–4.0x. JPMorgan expects aluminium to displace **~2% of global copper demand in 2026, rising to ~6% by 2030** ([Reuters via TimesLive](https://www.timeslive.co.za/motoring/2026-06-30-ferrari-and-bmw-follow-tesla-and-chinese-brands-in-shift-to-cheaper-aluminium-wiring)).
- **Data centres are a high-value niche, not a volume driver.** Wood Mackenzie expects aluminium used *inside* data centres to **peak at 0.6–0.9 Mt/yr globally** before declining in the late 2030s. Counting the grid and power systems around each site lifts total metals demand **3–4x** ([WoodMac](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/wood-mackenzie-data-centre-metals-demand-isnt-an-asset-story.-its-a-grid-story); [Engineering News](https://engineeringnews.co.za/article/grid-infrastructure-requirements-reshape-base-metals-demand-outlook-to-2040-woodmac-2026-06-17)).
- **Solar and batteries add volume but face heavy Chinese competition.** EU solar installations fell to **65.1 GW in 2025** and are forecast below 62 GW in 2026 ([SolarPower Europe](https://solarpowereurope.org/press-releases/new-report-eu-hits-2025-solar-target-but-market-contraction-puts-2030-goal-at-risk)). Almost all of the aluminium in those panels arrives already built into imported Chinese modules. Battery storage is growing fastest: Europe installed **36 GWh in 2025** and is forecast to install **138 GWh/yr by 2030** ([SolarPower Europe](https://www.solarpowereurope.org/press-releases/new-report-european-battery-storage-surges-in-2025-as-utility-scale-drives-next-growth-phase)).
- **Supply security has become a selling point in its own right.** The 2026 Strait of Hormuz crisis and the Iranian strikes on EGA's Al Taweelah smelter and on Alba pushed LME aluminium to **$3,545/t**. They also lifted the Rotterdam duty-paid premium to **$300–340/t** ([Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/strait-of-hormuz-closure-fuels-european-us-aluminium-supply-concerns/); [Breakwave](https://www.breakwaveadvisors.com/insights/2026/4/2/gulf-aluminium-supply-shock)). The Gulf supplies about **20–30% of European aluminium imports**. EU primary output is only about **1.2 Mt** (2025) ([Mysteel](https://www.mysteel.net/news/5130043-europes-aluminium-future-demand-rises-primary-output-falls-can-recycling-bridge-the-supply-gap)). Grid operators (TSOs and DSOs) and cable makers will pay for certified, local, low-carbon metal on a reliable delivery schedule.

### 2. Demand by segment

#### 2.1 Grids: overhead lines, cables, transformers

- **Global baseline.** Transmission and distribution lines used about **4.9 Mt of aluminium in 2020**, projected to reach **5.7–7.2 Mt by 2040** ([ING](https://think.ing.com/downloads/pdf/article/more-renewables-mean-more-metals)). The IEA expects grid-driven aluminium demand of **~21 Mt/yr** under announced pledges, or **~27 Mt/yr** in its net-zero scenario, for 2041–2050. This covers all grid uses and **80m km of lines to be added or refurbished globally by 2040** ([IEA via Oregon Group](https://theoregongroup.com/commodities/copper/copper-and-aluminium-demand-to-double-for-electric-grid-expansion/)). CRU/IAI put total electrical-sector aluminium at **10.4 Mt (2020) rising to 15.6 Mt (2030)** ([Investment Monitor](https://www.investmentmonitor.ai/sectors/energy/aluminium-production-energy-transition-challenges)).
- **EU distribution tonnage [est.].** Low- and medium-voltage aluminium cable or overhead line weighs about 0.8–1.5 t of aluminium per km. At 262,000 km/yr, distribution conductors alone need about **0.2–0.4 Mt Al/yr**. That figure excludes transformers, substations, busbars and transmission.
- **EU transmission tonnage [est.].** A 400 kV double-circuit overhead line with triple-bundle aluminium conductor (ACSR) carries about **30–40 t Al/km**. HVDC cables with aluminium conductors are also proven: Nexans used aluminium for the 423 km deep-water section of the MONITA interconnector ([Offshore Energy](https://offshore-energy.biz/?p=244212)).
- **Cable makers are already shifting.** Aluminium makes up about **40% of Prysmian's input metal by weight**, up 3 points in five years. In July 2026 Nexans signed a **five-year offtake of ~85 kt of wire rod (about 17 kt/yr) with Hydro**, at a verified footprint **below 4.0 kg CO₂/kg**, supplied from expanded rod capacity at Karmøy ([Nexans PR](https://www.nexans.com/app/uploads/2026/07/2026-07-06-pr-nexans-and-hydro-secure-long-term-low-carbon-aluminium-supply-agreement.pdf); [Energy Global](https://www.energyglobal.com/other-renewables/07072026/nexans-and-hydro-secure-long-term-low-carbon-aluminium-supply-agreement/)). Prysmian buys low-carbon metal from Rio Tinto in Canada ([CRU AMU](https://www.aluminummarketupdate.crugroup.com/decarbonisation/rio-tinto-prysmian-aluminum-rod-deal-highlights-tight-north-american-redraw-rod-market/)).
- **Reconductoring.** Replacing conductors on existing towers with high-temperature, low-sag (HTLS) aluminium conductors can roughly **double the capacity of an existing line**. Nexans has installed **2,700 km of HTLS conductor in Europe** ([Nexans](https://www.nexans.de/en/business/power-transmission-distribution/overhead-power-grids/Advanced-Conductors.html)). These conductors use high-value aluminium alloys and trapezoidal strand wire.
- **Transformers.** Lead times for large units now exceed **24 months** and approach **36–48 months** for specialised units. The IEA reports transformer prices up **~75% in real terms** since 2019 ([CWIEME](https://berlin.cwiemeevents.com/articles/transformer-lead-times-critical-grid-delivery); [IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/building-the-future-transmission-grid/executive-summary)). In distribution and small-to-medium power transformers, aluminium windings show **no significant performance gap** against copper. ABB cites **more than 1,500 power transformers above 10 MVA** with aluminium windings ([IM Mining](https://im-mining.com/?p=37723)). At today's prices, REO estimates that aluminium windings save about **63% of winding-material cost** ([Drives & Controls, Aug 2026](https://drivesncontrols.com/aluminium-an-attractive-alternative-to-copper-windings/)). This creates demand for **precision winding strip and foil (1xxx alloys)**, a rolling product the company can supply.

#### 2.2 Copper-to-aluminium substitution (cross-cutting)

- Aluminium has **61% of copper's conductivity**. A conductor with the same resistance needs about **1.6x the cross-section** but weighs only about **half as much** ([Reuters via TimesLive](https://www.timeslive.co.za/motoring/2026-06-30-ferrari-and-bmw-follow-tesla-and-chinese-brands-in-shift-to-cheaper-aluminium-wiring)).
- **Substitution tonnage [est.].** Global refined copper demand is about 27–28 Mt. If 6% of it is replaced by 2030, that is about 1.6–1.7 Mt of copper, which translates to roughly **0.8 Mt of extra aluminium a year** at about 0.5 kg Al per kg Cu. Europe's share is about 10–15%, or **~80–120 kt Al/yr** by 2030.
- **Where substitution happens:** busbars and busways, transformer windings, LV/MV power cable, EV charging cables, HVAC coils (Daikin cites "switching from copper to aluminium" in its 2025 annual report), and automotive high-voltage cables (Tesla, BMW, Ferrari).
- **Barriers:** joint design (oxide films, galvanic corrosion with copper), standards and utility specifications, and installers' habits. Selling engineering support alongside the metal is what makes customers switch.

#### 2.3 Solar PV

- Aluminium intensity is about **5.5–5.8 kt/GW for module frames**, plus **~13 kt/GW for rooftop rails and mountings**. Wood Mackenzie's all-in figure is about **21 t/MW** ([Australian Aluminium Council](https://aluminium.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/230719-Aluminum-response-Wired-for-change-Solar-PV-System-Waste.pdf); [WoodMac](https://woodmac.com/news/opinion/harnessing-the-sun-how-rising-solar-power-demand-impacts-base-metals)). WoodMac's global solar aluminium forecast for 2040 is **4.6 Mt** in the base case and **~10 Mt** under faster transition.
- **EU tonnage [est.].** At 60–68 GW/yr, EU solar uses about **0.35–0.4 Mt/yr in frames** and **0.6–1.0 Mt/yr all-in**. Nearly all of the frame metal arrives inside imported modules. Utility-scale projects now make up **more than 50%** of EU installations ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2025/12/11/eu-installs-65-1-gw-of-solar-in-2025/)), and those mostly use steel trackers.
- **The opening:** the Net-Zero Industry Act (NZIA) requires member states to apply **non-price "resilience/sustainability" criteria to at least 30% of auctioned volume, or 6 GW/yr, from 30 Dec 2025** ([SolarPower Europe](https://www.solarpowereurope.org/advocacy/position-papers/recommendations-on-nzia-s-implementing-act-on-renewable-auctions)). That gives a small premium niche for EU low-carbon frames and mounting.

#### 2.4 Wind, heat pumps, hydrogen

- **Wind:** Vestas' material disclosures put aluminium at 1.0–1.5% of turbine mass, which works out to about **1.1–1.9 t Al/MW [est.]** ([Vestas](https://www.vestas.com/content/dam/vestas-com/global/en/sustainability/environment/2023_04_Material-Use-Brochure_Vestas.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)). Including aluminium-conductor array cables, EU wind demand is in the tens of kt a year. Wind is a modest market overall.
- **Heat pumps / HVAC:** all-aluminium microchannel heat exchangers cut refrigerant charge by up to 40%, according to Hydro (a vendor claim; [Hydro](https://hydro.com/en/aluminium/industries/hvacr/micro-channel-heat-exchangers)). This is a growing market for extruded multi-port tube and brazing sheet. My estimate is about 5–15 kg Al per unit **[est.]** across the EU's ~2.5–3m units a year, or roughly 15–45 kt/yr.
- **Hydrogen:** electrolysers and hydrogen equipment need little aluminium. Rule it out as a primary opportunity.

#### 2.5 Battery energy storage and battery foil

- Europe's battery storage fleet passed **100 GWh** in 2025. Annual installations are forecast at **>50 GWh in 2026 and 138 GWh in 2030**, and the EU-27 fleet at about **470 GWh by 2030** ([SolarPower Europe](https://www.solarpowereurope.org/press-releases/new-report-european-battery-storage-surges-in-2025-as-utility-scale-drives-next-growth-phase)).
- **Tonnage [est.].** I found no published per-MWh figure. Prismatic lithium iron phosphate (LFP) cells use aluminium cans and aluminium cathode foil, which I estimate at about 0.6–0.8 t Al/MWh at cell level. Busbars, cooling plates and racks add 0.1–0.3 t, for a total of about **0.8–1.1 t Al/MWh**. At 138 GWh/yr, that is **~110–150 kt/yr in Europe by 2030**, though most of it arrives inside imported Chinese cells. Opportunities that are open to the company: **cooling plates, busbars, enclosure and rack extrusions**, and **battery foil** (Speira already competes here; [Speira](https://www.speira.com/markets-products/battery-systems/battery-foil/)).

#### 2.6 AI data centres

- **Capacity:** European data centre load is projected to rise from **18.7 GW (2024) to ~36 GW (2030)** ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2025/7/european-datacenter-power-demand-to-double-by-2030-straining-grids-91488155)). The EU AI Continent Action Plan targets **tripling capacity in 5–7 years** with €200bn of support. Global data-centre-driven power additions are expected to peak at **30–33 GW/yr in the early 2030s** (WoodMac).
- **Aluminium inside the building:** about **55% goes to cooling** and **25% to racking and enclosures**. Demand grows 8–10%/yr into the early 2030s, then declines 2–3%/yr. CRU puts combined copper and aluminium demand from data centres at **850–900 kt in 2026, rising above 1,500 kt by 2030**, with aluminium taking share as intensity falls ([CRU](https://prep.crugroup.com/en/communities/market-news/2026/blog-2/); [CRU AMU on fin stock](https://www.aluminummarketupdate.crugroup.com/global-trade/data-center-cooling-puts-fin-stock-in-focus/)).
- **Cold plates are contested.** Chip-level cold plates are mostly copper, according to CRU. Aluminium wins in **manifolds, coolant distribution units (CDUs), rear-door and dry-cooler heat exchangers (fin stock), multi-port extrusion flat-tube cold plates, busways and racks** ([Eaton](https://www.eaton.com.cn/cn/zh-cn/catalog/thermal-management-solutions/liquid-cold-plates.html)). Mixing copper and aluminium in one coolant loop causes galvanic corrosion, which pushes system designers to choose one metal throughout. The data-centre busbar market is worth about **$1.4–2.4bn (2026)**, and aluminium is its fastest-growing material ([Stratview](https://www.stratviewresearch.com/4683/data-center-busbars-market.html); [MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/data-center-busbar-market-31883863.html)).
- **Europe-specific [est.].** About 3 GW/yr of EU additions at roughly 15–25 t Al/MW inside the building gives **45–75 kt/yr**. Adding the grid and power systems around each site (3–4x) gives **~150–250 kt/yr of aluminium demand linked to data centres**.

### 4. Policy and market backdrop for the company

- **CBAM** has been in its definitive (paying) phase since 1 Jan 2026 and covers aluminium. A measure to restrict scrap exports to non-OECD countries is being prepared (Sept 2026) after **record exports of 1.27 Mt in 2025** ([Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/eu-aluminium-scrap-export-curbs-delayed-until-september-group-and-sources-say-2026-06-26); [Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/eu-moves-to-restrict-aluminium-scrap-exports)). If it is adopted, more scrap stays in Europe and the cost of making recycled electrical-grade metal falls.
- **Supply security:** the Hormuz crisis showed the risk of relying on imported ingot. Grid operators and cable makers now place value on certified EU supply under multi-year contracts.

### 5. So what for our company

1. **Move from selling metal to selling electrical-grade products.** Wire rod, conductor alloys, winding strip and busbars earn a value-added premium over P1020 metal *[est. €300–800/t]*. Demand is backed by regulated grid spending (€1.2tn), which makes it the least cyclical exposure available to us.
2. **Capture copper-to-aluminium substitution by offering engineering, not just metal.** Set up a "Cu→Al conversion" team that designs joints, qualifies products with transformer, switchgear and busway makers, and takes products through DSO specifications. At a 4.9x price ratio, the customer's savings fund the switch.
3. **Use our low-carbon, local supply as a lever with grid operators.** The Nexans–Hydro deal (<4 kg CO₂/kg, five years) is the template. We should offer the same to Prysmian, NKT, Nexans and the large DSOs and TSOs (E.ON, Enel, TenneT, Amprion, RTE), with both the CO₂ footprint and the delivery schedule contractually guaranteed.
4. **Keep electrical scrap in a closed loop.** Aluminium recovered from cables and lines (often pure 1xxx alloy) is ideal feed for electrical conductor rod. Any scrap export curb improves our cost of feed.
5. **Make targeted entries into data-centre thermal and power products, but do not bet the company on them.** Volumes are modest (under 0.1 Mt/yr in the EU) but margins are high. The quickest route in is to acquire or partner with a brazing or manifold specialist.
6. **Avoid commodity solar frames and battery cells.** Chinese cost leadership is overwhelming. Bid selectively for NZIA resilience-auction volumes and battery storage cooling plates and enclosures only.
