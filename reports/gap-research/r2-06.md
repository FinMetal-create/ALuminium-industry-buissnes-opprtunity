**Calculations [E]:**
- **Utilisation at end-2025:** 44.608 / 46.209 = **96.5%** of existing capacity.
- **2026 run-rate:**
  - Year to date annualised: 35.03 Mt × 365/273 = **46.8 Mt**.
  - September daily rate: 130.3 kt × 365 = **47.6 Mt**.
- **Full-year 2026 estimate:** 35.03 Mt plus Q4 at 3.85–3.95 Mt a month gives **46.6–46.9 Mt**. That is **104% of the 45 Mt cap**, and slightly above SMM's nameplate of 46.29 Mt, which points to capacity creep from technology upgrades.
- **What holding to the cap would take:** Breakwave estimates monthly output would have to fall by about 0.5 Mt ([Breakwave](https://www.breakwaveadvisors.com/insights/2026/9/18/guinea-powers-chinas-aluminium-record-but-a-cap-looms)).

**Conclusion:** China's primary overcapacity is **capped but binding at roughly 1.5–2 Mt above the cap**. The surplus is not leaving China as metal. It leaves as **semi-finished and finished goods**.

**After the duties (2025): an estimate from values only, not verified against Comext [E].** UN Comtrade values for 2025 (via [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/european-union/imports/china/aluminum)) are:
- 7606: $433m
- 7607: $303m
- 7604: $128m
- All of HS 76: $4.51bn

Assumed unit values are the 2025 LME average of about $2,600/t plus conversion premiums. That gives:
- **7606:** $433m / $3,600–4,200 per t = **103–120 kt**. This is about −40% to −50% against the 2019 flat-rolled level.
- **7607:** $303m / $4,000–5,000 per t = **61–76 kt**.
- **7604:** $128m / $4,000–5,000 per t = **26–32 kt**. This is about −85% to −90% against the 2019 extrusions level, but that comparison excludes tubes (7608).
- **7601:** **under 30 kt** [E]. China is a net importer of primary metal.

**Total exposure across 7601, 7604, 7606 and 7607 is about 0.19–0.26 Mt in 2025 (central estimate about 0.22 Mt).**

**Where the earlier 1.1–1.3 Mt figure came from, and why to delete it.** It matches $4.51bn / about $3,800 per t = 1.19 Mt. That calculation divides the **whole of HS 76** by a semis price. HS 76 is mostly finished articles (7610 structures, 7615 household goods, 7616 other articles), not semis. Replace it with **about 0.22 Mt of semis (range 0.19–0.26 Mt) until the Comext tonnages are in.**

**The claim that flows "collapsed".** Do not keep it as written. The defensible wording is:
- Flat-rolled imports are down an estimated 40–50%.
- Extrusion profile imports are down an estimated 85–90% against pre-duty levels.

Both are estimates and need Comext confirmation.

## So what for our company
1. **Size countermeasures to about 0.2 Mt a year of Chinese semis, not 1.2 Mt.** The larger exposure is Chinese demand redirected to third countries (Chinese sheet exports up 13.5% in 2026) and finished articles. Watch for circumvention through Thailand, Vietnam and Malaysia.
2. **Converter foil carries the most risk.** About 47 kt and a 24% share were at stake before the duties. If they lapse in December 2026, expect Chinese converter foil volumes and prices to rebound within 6–12 months [E]. Lobby on this, or prepare a fresh complaint.
3. **Support the flat-rolled and extrusion expiry reviews with company data**, because the reviews' dumping and injury assessment periods are running now. Pair the case with CBAM, whose definitive phase started in 2026, to argue on carbon intensity.

Two items remain open: the Comext tonnages for 2022–2025, and confirmation of the converter-foil review status. Both need a follow-up session with access to Eurostat and EUR-Lex.
