Both expiries fall inside our 90-day window, which ends about **19 Jan 2027**.

**Why an empty Sep–Oct 2026 OJ C search tells us little.** DG TRADE usually publishes expiry-review notices of initiation in the **last 1–4 weeks before expiry**. For these measures that means late Nov to early Dec 2026, and mid-Dec for FRP. Finding no notice before 21 Oct is consistent with a request already lodged and pending. It does not prove the measure will lapse. The fact we need is whether European Aluminium (the original complainant) lodged requests by about 9 and 23 Sep 2026. That is binary, and only the complainant or the DG TRADE case team can confirm it before initiation.

**My view on the likelihood of a request: 80–90% [own estimate].** Several things support it:
- The US Section 232 tariff on aluminium, including foil (CN 7607), doubled to **50% in June 2025**.
- The US has AD/CVD orders on Chinese foil, and a 2024 case added foil from five more origins.
- Together these divert Chinese foil towards the EU, which is the textbook evidence for "likelihood of recurrence".
- One factor against: China removed its **13% export VAT rebate on aluminium foil and semis from 1 Dec 2024**. Exporters will argue this narrows dumping margins. That is a defence, not a reason not to file.

## 2. Section 7.2 decision rule (as I reconstruct it)

The memo text was not in the repository. The tree below is my reconstruction, and it should be checked against the original wording.

- **A. Review initiated, or confirmed to be pending, before expiry:** do **not** file a new complaint. Register as an interested party within the notice deadline, normally 15 days for sampling data and 37 days for submissions. Supply injury and production data through European Aluminium. Use the resources saved to prepare an **Art. 13 anti-circumvention request** if third-country transhipment or slight product modification shows up in the data. Candidate origins to check are Thailand, Korea and Vietnam (not established). Candidate scope edges are 0.2 mm+ gauges and battery or capacitor foil.
- **B. No request lodged by about 9 Sep, so the measure lapses on 9 Dec 2026:** **file a new Art. 5 complaint by day 90.** The duty gap would open about 10 days after today+50, so 8–12 weeks of preparation is the critical path. A complaint needs:
  - standing: supporting producers above 25% of EU output and above 50% of those expressing a view;
  - a 12-month investigation period of dumping and injury data, most likely Q4-2025 to Q3-2026;
  - a likely 7–8 months from initiation to provisional duties.
- **C. Unknown at day 7:** treat it as B and start building the complaint dossier. The cost is roughly €0.3–0.6m in legal and economic fees [est.]. That is small next to the exposure in Section 4.

The FRP measure follows the same tree, with a 23 Sep request date and a 23 Dec expiry.

## 3. Restating foil exposure with foil-specific CBAM defaults

CN 7607 foil is in CBAM Annex I. Only direct emissions count. Chinese exporters without verified data will mostly be priced at **default values plus a mark-up**: 10% in 2026, 20% in 2027 and 30% from 2028, per the 2025 simplification. I could not fetch the country-specific default for Chinese 7607 from the Dec-2025 definitive default-value act.

**Assumptions [est.]:**
- Default direct intensity for Chinese foil: **2.3 tCO2e/t**, range 1.8–3.0. This is smelter anode/PFC at about 1.6–2.0, plus casting, rolling and annealing fuel at about 0.3–0.6.
- EUA price: €80 in 2026–27, €85 in 2028, €90 in 2030, €100 in 2034.
- The payable share follows the ETS free-allocation phase-out: 2.5%, 5%, 10%, 48.5% and 100% in 2026, 2027, 2028, 2030 and 2034.

| Year | Calculation | CBAM €/t foil (central) | Range (1.8–3.0 t) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 | 2.3 × 1.10 × 80 × 2.5% | **€5** | €4–7 |
| 2027 | 2.3 × 1.20 × 80 × 5% | **€11** | €9–14 |
| 2028 | 2.3 × 1.30 × 85 × 10% | **€25** | €20–33 |
| 2030 | 2.3 × 1.30 × 90 × 48.5% | **€131** | €102–170 |
| 2034 | 2.3 × 1.30 × 100 × 100% | **€299** | €234–390 |

**What the AD duty is worth for comparison [est.]:**
- Chinese converter foil lands at about €3,400–4,000/t CIF (LME plus a conversion premium).
- At 15–49%, the duty adds **€510–1,960/t**. The volume-weighted figure is about **€700/t**, because most volume pays the low cooperating rates.
- CBAM therefore replaces about **1% of AD protection in 2026–27**, about **19% in 2030** and about **43% in 2034**. CBAM is not a substitute for AD inside the 2026–2030 horizon.

## 4. Exposure if the foil measure lapses [illustrative estimates]

- **Market size:** EU converter-foil consumption is about 750–900 kt/yr. China's share was about 15–20% before the duties and is about 3–6% now [est. from recollection of the original case].
- **Return volume:** if duties lapse, I assume about 50–100 kt/yr of Chinese foil comes back within 12–18 months. That is plausible given that the US market is closed to it.
- **Price impact:** erosion of the EU foil conversion premium of about **€150–400/t**.
- **Our exposure, using an assumed 40–80 kt/yr of our own converter foil:** EBITDA at risk is **€6–32m/yr**, central about €15m (60 kt × €250/t).
- **CBAM offset at the same volume:** about €0.3–0.9m in 2027 and about €8m by 2030.
- **FRP measure:** adds a separate exposure of the same kind on sheet. It is not sized here.
