**Why the curve slopes down (backwardation):** the market expects more renewables, LNG supply growth and new gas or H2-ready plants from the Kraftwerksstrategie. The long end is not a price you can trade in size. A 10-year, 300 MW baseload hedge would move it by several €/MWh.

## 2. Published industrial and PPA price points [E: from memory, verify]

**German Industriestrompreis (CISAF section 4.5, coalition decision late 2025)**
- Target price of €50/MWh, for 2026–2028 only.
- Covers at most 50% of annual consumption.
- The aid may not exceed 50% of the wholesale price, and the price may not be pushed below €50.
- At least about 50% of the aid has to be reinvested in decarbonisation or flexibility.
- Eligibility follows the sector list for German ICC (Strompreiskompensation). Rules on stacking it with ICC need to be checked.
- Value at a Cal-27/28 price of €85: 0.5 × min(42.5, 35) = **€17.5/MWh averaged over all consumption, or about €245/t**.
- **It does not exist in 2031.** It is a bridge for 2026–28, not a lever for the 2031 margin.

**French nuclear: EDF CAPN contracts and the post-ARENH framework**
- The 2023 agreement between the French state and EDF targets an average of about €70/MWh. Revenue is clawed back at 50% above about €78/MWh and at 90% above about €110/MWh.
- CAPN contracts are 10–15-year allocations of nuclear output and need an upfront capex payment.
- The Aluminium Dunkerque CAPN (our research pack, report 06) has **no published price**. My estimate for an effective price including the upfront payment is **€60–70/MWh** [E].

**German PPAs (Pexapark and LevelTen indices, 2025 levels)** [E]
- Solar, pay-as-produced: about €40–50/MWh.
- Onshore wind, pay-as-produced: about €60–70/MWh.
- LevelTen P25 for Germany in 2025: roughly solar €50–55 and wind €65–75.

None of these is a baseload price. They have to be firmed (section 3).

## 3. Building a firmed German baseload price for 2031–2040 [E]

| Component | €/MWh |
|---|---|
| Hybrid pay-as-produced portfolio (60% wind / 40% solar by energy) | 56–62 |
| Shaping to baseload (capture rates: wind 80–85%, solar 55–65%) | +12–20 |
| Volume and balancing risk, plus residual top-up bought at the forward | +5–8 |
| Credit and collateral premium for a 10–15-year tenor | +1–3 |
| **Firmed green baseload, before non-commodity charges** | **74–93 (central ~82)** |
| Non-commodity costs for a smelter: grid fee reduced under §19 StromNEV, electricity tax at €0.5/MWh, capped levies | +3–8 (central 5) |

**Conclusion:** a firmed German renewable baseload PPA (central €82) costs more than the Cal-31 forward (€73) and more than the €76 break-even. **The memo's gate of ≤€50–55/MWh cannot be met by German wind and solar alone.** It needs at least one of:
- Nordic hydro or a French CAPN at €60–70,
- monetised smelter flexibility (worth €5–15/MWh [E]),
- extension of ICC after 2030,
- a CISAF or CfD floor.

## 4. Rebuilt section 7.1 margin table (€/t; revenue €2,370/t, 14.0 MWh/t, non-power cost €1,300/t)

**ICC formula:** 0.75 aid intensity × 0.72 tCO2/MWh (Germany's emission factor) × the EUA carbon price × 14.256 MWh/t (the EU benchmark for primary aluminium) [benchmark value: verify].

| Scenario | All-in power price (€/MWh) | ICC (€/t) | Cash cost (€/t) | Margin (€/t) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2028: 50% at €60 + 50% at Cal-28 €82 + €5 | 76.0 | 616 (EUA €80) | 1,748 | **+622** |
| 2028: same power, no ICC | 76.0 | 0 | 2,364 | +6 |
| **2031 base: hedge expired, Cal-31 €73 + €5, ICC lapsed** | 78.0 | 0 | 2,392 | **−22** |
| 2031: 50% at €60 still running, ICC lapsed | 71.5 | 0 | 2,301 | +69 |
| 2031: ICC extended (EUA €90, emission factor 0.72) | 78.0 | 693 | 1,699 | +671 |
| 2031: ICC extended with emission factor cut to 0.55 | 78.0 | 529 | 1,863 | +507 |
| 2031: firmed German PPA at €82 all-in, no ICC | 82.0 | 0 | 2,448 | −78 |
| 2031: memo gate met at €55 | 55.0 | 0 | 2,070 | +300 |
| 2031 high (Cal-31 €88) / low (Cal-31 €62), no ICC | 93 / 67 | 0 | 2,602 / 2,238 | −232 / +132 |

**Implications**
1. **The ICC lapse is the dominant risk, and the memo understates it.** At EUA €70–90, ICC is worth €540–690/t, or **€108–139m a year for 200 kt**. The memo's −€77m works out to €385/t on 200 kt. That only holds if EUA is about €50, or the emission factor falls to about 0.55 with EUA around €65, or output is about 125–140 kt. **The memo needs to state its EUA, emission-factor and output assumptions.**
2. Without ICC, the 2031 margin is **about €0/t ± €230/t** for every €12/MWh swing in the forward. Each €1/MWh moves the margin by €14/t, or €2.8m a year per 200 kt.
3. **The power-platform gate stays correct:** above about €76/MWh all-in, the smelter needs ICC or a CISAF/CfD floor to survive.
