## 2. Hub model: assumptions (all mine)

- **Size:** a 100 kt/yr Zorba-in hub. Utilisation ramps 60% (2029) → 85% (2030) → 90% (2031–43).
- **Capex:** €40m, the middle of the memo's €40–80m for two hubs. Phased 40% in 2027 and 60% in 2028. FID in Q2 2027.
- **Output mix per input tonne:**
  - 60% sorted wrought, priced at Zorba + S
  - 35% cast fraction, priced at Zorba − 2 points
  - 5% non-metallics and fines, valued at zero
- **Prices:** LME **€2,200/t**, about US$2,530, close to the memo's US$2,500 normalised price. Zorba at 75% of LME. Prices are flat and in real terms.
- **Operating costs:** variable €35/t of input, plus €4m/yr fixed (about 40 FTE plus overhead).
- **Sustaining capex:** 2.5% of capex per year (€1m), plus a €4m LIBS/XRT overhaul in 2036.
- **Depreciation:** initial capex straight-line over 10 years; sustaining capex over 5 years.
- **Tax:** 25%, with loss carry-forward.
- **Working capital:** 35 days of revenue (scrap yard plus in-process stock, plus receivables, less payables). It is about **€16m at full run-rate, 40% of capex**, and is released in 2043.
- **Hurdle:** the 10% IRR is unlevered and real. In nominal terms that is about 12%.

Per input tonne, margin = LME × (0.60·S − 0.05·P_Zorba − 0.007) − opex.

## 4. Why the 13-point gate fails

**IRR against the spread (base case):**

| Spread S (points) | 13 | 16 | 18 | 20 | 22 | 25 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IRR | **−15.5%** | −3.0% | 2.4% | 6.6% | 10.3% | 15.4% | 23.0% |

- **A 13-point spread gives EBITDA of about −€0.5m/yr.** At €2,200/t LME, 13 points on 60% of the tonnage is €172/t. After the 5% sorting loss (€83/t), the cast-fraction discount (€15/t) and opex (€79/t), nothing is left.
- **I can only get 13% IRR at S = 13 by stacking optimistic inputs:**
  - 75% wrought yield and 3% loss
  - variable opex of €25/t
  - capex of €30m
  - working capital of 20 days

  I assume the memo's 8.6% and 11.5% came from assumptions like these, or left out working capital and tax. Neither number can be reproduced on a standard structure.

**Spread needed for 10% IRR (points of LME):**

| Wrought yield \ LME | €1,900 | €2,200 | €2,600 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | 29.0 | 26.6 | 24.2 |
| 60% | 23.9 | **21.8** | 19.8 |
| 70% | 20.2 | 18.4 | 16.7 |

## 5. So what for our company

1. **Reset the FID gate to at least 22 points of LME** on the sorted wrought fraction, which is **about €480/t at €2,200/t LME**. Make it conditional on a **demonstrated wrought yield of at least 60%** and **sorting loss of at most 5%** in a 3–6-month toll trial. The gate scales with yield: **18 points if the trial proves 70% yield, 27 points at 50%.** An equivalent test is hub EBITDA of at least €113/t of input at 90% utilisation.
2. **A merchant-only hub, selling sorted 6xxx on the open market, does not clear the hurdle.** The market spread is about 8–15 points against the 22 needed. The investment case only works on **captive substitution**: sorted 6xxx replacing P1020 in our own casthouse.
   - Normalised value of that replacement: LME + US$250 premium is about 110% of LME. Less about 3% extra melt loss and about 1.5 points of handling, it is worth about 105% of LME.
   - Against Zorba at 75%, that is a **spread of about 30 points and an IRR of about 23%**.
   - The board paper must therefore use a **transfer price for sorted metal into our casthouse** and show the standalone merchant case alongside it.
3. **Working capital (about €16m) is a second capex line.** Cut it with:
   - consignment or tolling feed from JV shredders (the Hydro/PADNOS model)
   - inventory financing
   - LME hedging of scrap stock

   Moving from 35 to 20 days lowers the gate by about 0.7 points.
4. **Before FID, buy 24 months of Fastmarkets/Argus/EUWID history** for Zorba, Twitch, taint/tabor and 6063, and replace the section 1 ranges with real numbers. If the realised Zorba discount widens by 5 points after the 2027 export restriction, the captive spread rises one-for-one. The case must still pass with no widening.
