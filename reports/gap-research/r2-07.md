**Bottom-up market estimate [E]:**
- **Potroom mobile equipment.** About 220 potlines worldwide, with about 7 pot-tending machines (PTMs) or cranes per line, gives about 1,500 units. At a 22-year life, about 70 units are replaced each year. At EUR 4–7m per unit that is EUR 0.3–0.5bn/yr. Automation retrofits, spares and service roughly match that, so the segment totals **EUR 0.6–1.0bn/yr**.
- **Other smelter technology packages** (fume treatment centres, anode handling, rodding, bake-furnace control) for sustaining capex. Assume sustaining capex of USD 100–150/t across 73 Mt, with 20–30% of it going to equipment and automation. That gives **EUR 1.4–3.0bn/yr**.
- **Casthouse equipment** (DC casting, furnaces, homogenisers, melt treatment) for about 110 Mt/yr of primary and recycled cast metal: **EUR 1.5–2.5bn/yr**, plus about EUR 1bn of consumables such as filters and refractories.
- **Total:** about **EUR 3.5–6.5bn/yr** worldwide. Roughly half sits in China and is served by Chinese suppliers (NFC, GAMI, SAMI). The share open to a Western or EU vendor is therefore about **EUR 1.8–3.2bn/yr**.
- **Europe** (EU, Norway, Iceland, UK; about 4 Mt operating primary plus about 200 remelt and casthouse sites): **EUR 250–450m/yr**.
- **Vendor revenue [K/E].** Fives group is about EUR 2.3–2.5bn in total; its aluminium businesses (ECL, Solios) are an estimated 8–12% of that, or EUR 200–300m. Wagstaff is an estimated USD 150–250m and Hycast an estimated NOK 0.3–0.6bn. Western casthouse vendors together come to about EUR 0.8–1.2bn. Fives revenue can be checked at https://www.fivesgroup.com/finance.

**European premium pool estimate [E].** Assumptions:
- EU27+UK aluminium demand is about 12 Mt/yr.
- 20–35% of it is bought against a certified low-CO2 or high-recycled specification, which is 2.4–4.2 Mt.

| Case | Volume | Premium | Pool |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low (market differential only) | 2.4 Mt | USD 10/t | **~EUR 22m/yr** |
| Mid (blended market and bilateral) | 3.3 Mt | EUR 40/t | **~EUR 130m/yr** |
| High (branded bilateral, e.g. CIRCAL-type) | 4.2 Mt | EUR 100/t | **~EUR 420m/yr** |

**Structural anchor for 2030–2035 [E].** CBAM free allocation reaches zero in 2034. A coal-based import with about 1.5–2 t of direct CO2e per tonne would then carry €120–160/t of CBAM cost at an ETS price of €80/t. That is a defensible ceiling for the low-carbon premium on primary metal, and the pool would rise to about **EUR 0.4–0.7bn/yr**. Today the premium is thin; the money is in long-term offtakes.

## 3. Opportunity #2: German balancing reserve (FCR + aFRR)

I could not retrieve the official cost series. It is published in the Bundesnetzagentur/Bundeskartellamt Monitoringbericht, chapter "Regelreserve/Systemdienstleistungen" (https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Fachthemen/ElektrizitaetundGas/Monitoringberichte/start.html). Auction results are at https://www.regelleistung.net/apps/datacenter/tenders/. From memory [K], capacity costs spiked in 2022 during the gas crisis (about EUR 1bn+ for all products) and have fallen since as batteries entered the market.

**Bottom-up estimate for 2024–25 capacity costs [E]:**

| Product | Assumed German demand | Assumed average capacity price | Annual cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| FCR | ~580 MW (German share of about 3 GW in the regional FCR cooperation) | EUR 10–15/MW/h | 580 × 8,760 × 10–15 = **EUR 50–75m** |
| aFRR positive | ~2,000 MW | EUR 8–15/MW/h | **EUR 140–260m** |
| aFRR negative | ~1,900 MW | EUR 5–10/MW/h | **EUR 85–165m** |
| **FCR + aFRR** | | | **~EUR 275–500m/yr** in capacity payments, plus volatile energy payments |
| mFRR (for reference) | | | EUR 20–100m |

**What a smelter can capture [E].**
- A 200 kt smelter draws about 300 MW. At ±25% flex it could prequalify about 50 MW of aFRR (5-minute activation through rectifier control).
- Capacity revenue: 50 MW × 8,760 h × EUR 6–10/MW/h = **EUR 2.6–4.4m/yr**, plus energy revenue. That is about 1–1.5% of the market, consistent with our EUR 3–8m/yr estimate.
- **Risk:** large German battery fleets (several GW added in 2024–26) are pushing FCR and aFRR prices down. Model the window as worth 50% less by 2030.

## 5. So what for our company

1. **#6 is a real but niche market.** Europe is about EUR 250–450m/yr. A Hycast-style unit would need 5–10% of it, which is EUR 15–40m/yr in revenue. That makes it a partnership with or minority stake in AlumatIQ/Fives/REEL, not a stand-alone business.
2. **#7 is an option on CBAM, not a cash pool today.** Price contracts against today's thin premium of USD 0–30/t, and write in CBAM-indexed escalators that lift the premium toward €100–160/t by 2034.
3. **#2 is real but shrinking.** The market is about EUR 0.3–0.5bn/yr and batteries are eroding prices. Use the flexibility mainly to win shaped PPAs (power contracts that follow wind and solar output) and CISAF eligibility, and treat balancing revenue as a bonus.
