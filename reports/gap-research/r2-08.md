## 1. Marine and armour plate prices (€/t, delivered EU)

**What the public record holds.** TED (https://ted.europa.eu) and UK Find-a-Tender (https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk) awards for aluminium plate are usually framework ceilings with no tonnage, or they are hidden inside vehicle or hull contracts. As far as I know, no award lets you derive a clean €/t figure. ElvalHalcor, AMAG and Kaiser do not publish plate prices by alloy. The usable public anchors are conversion revenue (the price over metal) and revenue per tonne:
- **Kaiser** reports conversion revenue per pound by end market: Aero/HS about $2.3–2.8/lb (about $5,000–6,200/t); general-engineering plate, rod and bar about $1.3–1.5/lb (about $2,900–3,300/t) [R] (10-K at https://investors.kaiseraluminum.com).
- **Constellium A&T** revenue of about $9,500–10,500/t implies conversion of about $6,500–7,000/t for an aerospace-heavy mix [R]. Its 2025 shipments were 207 kt [repo].

**Build-up.** Delivered price = LME + Rotterdam duty-paid premium + conversion premium. Normalised metal (memo basis: LME $2,500 + $250 premium, at 1.15 USD/EUR) is about **€2,390/t**. At 2026 spot (LME about $3,000–3,400 plus a premium of about $500–595 [repo]), metal is about **€3,000–3,400/t**.

| Product | Conversion premium €/t [est.] | Delivered €/t, normalised | Delivered €/t, 2026 spot |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5083-H111/H116/H321 marine plate | 1,100–1,700 | 3,500–4,100 | 4,100–5,100 |
| 5383 / branded high-strength marine plate | 1,500–2,200 | 3,900–4,600 | 4,500–5,600 |
| 5083-H131 armour (ballistic-certified lots) | 1,800–2,800 | 4,200–5,200 | 4,800–6,200 |
| 7020-T651 / 7017 armour | 3,000–4,500 | 5,400–6,900 | 6,000–7,900 |

How the conversion ranges were set:
- **Marine plate** sits above Kaiser's general-engineering level, because of classification-society approval and corrosion testing.
- **7xxx armour plate** sits between Kaiser's general-engineering plate and Kaiser Aero/HS. Below Aero/HS because the approval scheme is lighter than aerospace; above general engineering because it adds heat treatment, stretching, ballistic lot testing and small batches.

**Implication.** The 2026 spike adds about €600–1,000/t of metal that passes straight through to the customer. Base cases should use the normalised column.

## 3. Brownfield heat-treatment and stretcher capex

No public €/t comparable isolates a plate heat-treatment line. The nearest public anchors are older full-mill expansions:
- **Alcoa Davenport** thick-plate expansion: about $300m, around 2012–14 [R].
- **Kaiser Trentwood** heat-treat plate programme: about $150m, completed by 2010 [R].

Neither discloses capacity cleanly. A bottom-up estimate from equipment-vendor price ranges (Ebner, Otto Junker, SMS, Fives, Danieli) is below [est.]:

| Item | € m |
|---|---|
| Plate solution heat-treatment furnace and quench (roller hearth or vertical) | 15–30 |
| Plate stretcher, 3,000–6,000 t | 10–25 |
| Ageing furnaces (2–3) | 4–10 |
| Immersion ultrasonic inspection, saws, handling | 5–10 |
| Building, foundations, utilities, ballistic test access | 6–15 |
| **Total for a 20–30 kt/yr line** | **40–90 (about €1,500–3,000 per tonne of annual capacity)** |

- An armour-only line with thinner plate and a smaller stretcher could sit near the low end, about **€40–55m**.
- The memo's **€30–60m placeholder is light**. Use **€60m as the base case and €45–75m as the range**.
- For comparison, the Speira UBC melt furnace cost about €650 per tonne of annual capacity [repo]. Plate heat treatment costs 2–4 times as much per tonne.

## 4. European scrap discounts (2026) and the 15-point spread

I cannot check the 2026 Fastmarkets prints. Fastmarkets' European aluminium scrap prices are behind a subscription, and the clean benchmarks for taint/tabor and zorba are US or export grades. The typical 2024–26 European bands, as a percentage of LME, are [R/est.]:

| Grade | % of LME |
|---|---|
| Sorted 6063/6xxx extrusion scrap | 85–92 |
| Clean 5xxx / old sheet (taint/tabor) | 78–86 |
| Zorba 90–95% (export, Rotterdam / CIF India) | 70–80 |

This gives two spreads:
- **Zorba to sorted 6xxx: about 8–18 points, centred near 12.** The 15-point figure is plausible but sits at the top of the band. Strong Indian buying and the scrap-retention debate compressed the spread in 2025–26.
- **Taint/tabor to sorted 5xxx/6xxx: about 4–10 points.**
- The spread against LME plus duty-paid premium is wider, because scrap does not carry the CBAM-inflated premium.

**Value per point.** Model hub: 125 kt input, 80% upgraded, €60/t sorting cost, LME at €2,174/t. Each point of spread is worth about **€2.2m/yr of EBITDA**.

**Recommendation:** use **12 points as the base case and 8–18 points as the range**, and buy a Fastmarkets or Argus Europe scrap subscription before FID.

## 5. IRR sensitivities (my model, not the memo's)

Common assumptions: 15-year life, 25% tax, 2% sustaining capex, straight-line depreciation.

**Case #1:** 125 kt hub; ramp 50% in year 1, 80% in year 2, then 100%.

| Spread (points) | EBITDA €m/yr | IRR at €40m capex | IRR at €60m capex |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 9.9 | 14% | 7% |
| 10 | 14.2 | 22% | 13% |
| 12 | 18.6 | 29% | 19% |
| 15 | 25.1 | 38% | 26% |

The memo's 19.0% IRR is supported at **12 points and €60m capex**. Spread and capex together set the IRR; the recommended range is 8–18 points.

**Case #3:** 25 kt; build spread over 2 years; ramp 20%, 50%, 80%, then 100%.

| Incremental margin €/t | EBITDA €m/yr | IRR at €45m | IRR at €60m | IRR at €75m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 250 | 6.2 | 3% | 0% | <0% |
| 400 | 10.0 | 10% | 6% | 3% |
| 600 | 15.0 | 16% | 11% | 8% |

The memo's 11.3% IRR needs **about €600/t of incremental margin at €60m capex**. That is above the evidenced range of €250–500/t. The case only works if any of the following holds:
- The mix shifts to at least 50% 7xxx armour.
- A take-or-pay framework includes a capacity fee.
- Capex can be held near €45m.

**For the FIDs:**
- **Case #1:** defensible now at 12 points and €60m capex.
- **Case #3:** defer FID until three things exist: a ballistic-mix offtake letter, an engineering capex quote, and verified AMAG, ElvalHalcor and Kaiser plate economics.
