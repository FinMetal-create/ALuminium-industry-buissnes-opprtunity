## 4. Recomputed numbers

**Realised P1020 price, Rotterdam duty-paid equivalent:**
- **Spot: ($2,997 + $567.5) / 1.1215 = €3,178/t.** The range is €3,163–3,224/t across the premium range and the LME prints from 13 to 20 Oct.
- **Normalised (memo basis, $2,500 + $250): €2,452/t.** The memo's €2,370 used an FX rate of about 1.16, which is stale. I recommend a premium floor of $350/t. That is roughly 3% duty (~$75–90) plus logistics and financing ($100–150) plus a rising CBAM cost [est.], and gives **€2,541/t**.
- Against the memo's cash cost of €2,560/t at €90/MWh, the margin is **+€618/t at spot** and **−€19 to −€108/t on a normalised basis**. The memo must use the normalised figure for FID and the spot figure only for 2026–27 cash.

**China–EU price gap:** $3,564.5 − $3,084 = **$481/t (€429/t)**. The range is $471–530/t.
- SHFE excluding VAT sits **$87/t above LME**.
- So the whole gap is the European premium (duty, CBAM and tightness), not cheaper Chinese exchange metal. If the premium normalises to $350/t, the gap shrinks to about $260/t.

**The €6.3m error.** It is reproduced exactly by **30 MW × 8,760 h × €24/MW/h = €6.31m** [my reconstruction]. That calculation has four faults:
- it uses the 2025 marginal price instead of the price actually received;
- it assumes 100% of hours, where 70% availability is realistic;
- it applies no decay as batteries saturate the market;
- it ignores the negative-aFRR leg.

Corrected for the same 30 MW of positive aFRR only, the revenue is **€1.7m in 2026 and €0.9m from 2030**.

**Rebuilt flex case, one ~200 kt potline.** The line draws about 331 MW, with ±25% modulation ≈ 83 MW. I assume 60 MW is prequalified in both directions, offered for 6,132 hours a year [est.].

| €m/yr | 2025 prices | 2026 prices | 2030+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| aFRR capacity | 10.7 | 6.1 | 3.3 |
| Wholesale shaping: ~100 GWh/yr energy-neutral shift × ~€30/MWh net [est.] | 3.0 | 3.0 | 3.0 |
| Opex and pot-life penalty | −1.0 | −1.0 | −1.0 |
| **Flex EBITDA** | 12.7 | 8.1 | **5.3** |

**IRR.** Capex is €36m, using Trimet's virtual-battery retrofit as the benchmark. It is spent over 2027–28, with 15 years of operation from 2029. aFRR revenue declines from 2029 to the 2030 level.
- **Base case: IRR 11.6%, NPV at 12% = −€0.7m.**
- aFRR only: IRR about 0%, NPV −€19m. This case does not pass.
- Shaping worth €5m: IRR 17.5%.
- 2026 aFRR prices held flat: IRR 20.1%. This is not credible.

**Flex envelope resized to the FID rule.** I assume the rule is IRR ≥12% on normalised prices, as set out in the memo [assumption].
- The capex that breaks even at 12% is **€35m per line in the base case**, €16m if the case rests on aFRR alone, and €48m with €5m of shaping.
- **Recommendation:** FID on **potline 1 only, with capex capped at €35m all-in**. Closing the €1m gap to €36m needs a CISAF/IPCEI grant of about 3% or a supplier price.
- Potline 2 becomes a **gated option, also capped at €35m**. It is released only if potline 1 shows at least €5m/yr flex EBITDA over 12 months.
- This replaces the memo's €30–80m for 1–2 lines with **€35m firm plus a €35m contingent option**.
- The board story becomes "a power-contract and shaping asset with aFRR as a declining kicker". It should not be presented as a balancing-revenue business. The memo's €3–8m/yr per 200 kt should read **€5–8m/yr base case, falling toward €2–3m if the asset only earns aFRR**.
