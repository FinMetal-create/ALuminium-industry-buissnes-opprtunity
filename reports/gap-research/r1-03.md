Example calculation for the 2035 humanoid base case: 6.5M units × 20 kg = 130 kt.

**What the numbers mean:** In the base case, robotics is about 0.2–0.3% of global aluminium demand in 2035. It is a small but premium niche: HPDC castings, precision extrusions for actuators and linear axes, and 6xxx/7xxx forgings. China builds 59% of industrial robots [IFR](https://ifr.org/img/worldrobotics/Executive_Summary_WR_2026_-_Industrial_Robots.pdf) and leads the humanoid supply chain. A realistic EU-addressable share is 10–15% **[est]**, about **23–35 kt/yr by 2035** in the base case. Humanoids are the swing factor, between 17 and 280 kt in 2035, and Mg/PEEK substitution is the main downside risk.

## Part B: Own-plant automation

### B1. Evidence base

- **Anode change** is the riskiest potroom task: hot metal, live busbars and fluoride. A smelter changes "several hundred" anodes a day. Each change takes more than 10 steps by a crane driver plus a floor helper. Rio Tinto's trials failed on the "most value-added tasks" ([ICSOBA 2022](https://icsoba.org/assets/files/publications/2022/Papers/AL10%20-%20Aluminium%20Electrolysis%20Cells-%20Anode%20Changing%20Automation%20Challenges.pdf)).
- **Fives ECL** has a staged path: a single-man PTM removes the floor helper, and an automatic PTM removes the driver as well ([ICSOBA 2015](https://icsoba.org/assets/files/publications/2015/Papers/AL09%20-%20Embedded%20service%20robot%20-%20towards%20an%20automated%20efficient%20and%20green%20smelter.pdf)). **EGA** replaced 14 Jebel Ali cranes with automated Fives cranes, all running by early 2025. No contract value was published ([alcircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/ega-moves-toward-total-automation-by-replacing-existing-cranes-in-jebel-ali-80910)). Qatalum runs 14 PTMs with 48 dedicated operators at over 95% efficiency ([Qatalum](https://www.qatalum.com/Media/News/Pages/Innovations-in-Pot-Tending-at-the-Qatalum-Smelter.aspx)).
- **AlumatIQ** (Mosjøen, Trondheim, Mo i Rana test centre; SINTEF project) automates the full cycle: covers, crust breaking, anode change and covering ([SINTEF](https://www.sintef.no/en/projects/2020/alumatiqanodeskifterobot/), [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/alumatiq)). No public deployment or financial data.
- **REEL Alesa** sells PTMs and furnace tending assemblies ([REEL](https://www.reelinternational.com/?p=84)). **Dynamic Concept/EPIQ** offer the ARFT robot (battery-powered skimming, cleaning and stirring), with a Gulf commercial deal in May 2026 ([alcircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/epiq-and-dynamic-concept-partner-to-deliver-advanced-solutions-across-the-gulf-118544)). **RiA** reports that automated charging and skimming saves 1.5 t Al, 2.4 t saltcake and 27 t CO₂e per 500 t produced (0.3% metal loss) and cuts cycle time by up to 50% ([alcircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/maximise-productivity-and-safety-of-your-casthouse-with-ria-charge-and-skim-at-the-earliest-and-safest-time-every-time-104304)).
- **SMS HybrEx** extrusion presses: up to 55% less energy and up to 20% more productivity (vendor claim) ([SMS](https://www.sms-group.com/plants/light-metal-extrusion-presses/hybrexr-extrusion-presses)).
- **Staffing benchmark:** Tiwai Point ran about 800–900 workers for roughly 330–350 kt, about 416 t per person, which is ~250 FTE per 100 kt ([alcircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/tiwai-pt-aluminium-smelter-workers-set-new-productivity-record-15212)).

No vendor publishes capex per cell, FTE removed or EUR/t savings. The table below is my model for a reference 300 kt EU smelter and casthouse.

### B2. Business case for the reference plant **[est]**

Assumptions: fully loaded labour cost EUR 80k per FTE, aluminium at EUR 2,300–2,400/t, power at EUR 120/MWh, CO₂ at EUR 75/t.

| Lever | Capex | FTE removed | Savings | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single-man PTM retrofit (8 PTMs) | EUR 1.5M × 8 = 12M | ~30 floor helpers | 2.4M/yr = **EUR 8/t** | ~5 yrs |
| Automatic PTM (AlumatIQ / Fives) | EUR 8M × 8 = 64M; only EUR 2–3M per unit extra if done at PTM end of life | +20 net (drivers move to the control room) | Labour EUR 13/t, plus 0.2% current-efficiency gain (600 t ≈ EUR 4.6/t) = **~EUR 18/t** (5.4M/yr) | 12 yrs standalone; **3–4.5 yrs on the incremental capex at replacement** |
| Furnace charge/skim robots (6 furnaces) | ~EUR 7.5M | 10–15 | 3 kg Al/t (EUR 7) + CO₂ EUR 4 + saltcake EUR 0.6 + labour EUR 3.3 = **~EUR 15/t** | ~1.7 yrs |
| HybrEx press (per 10 kt/yr line) | EUR 15–25M | 0–2 | 110 kWh/t ≈ EUR 13/t, plus up to EUR 50/t fixed-cost absorption if the extra 20% output sells | Only worth it at press replacement |

At the reference smelter: 750 FTE × 1,700 h ≈ 1.28M hours a year. At a smelter rate of 1.9 that is about 2.4 lost-time accidents a year. Removing about 50 floor FTE takes out ~85k of the highest-exposure hours **[est]**. The value is licence-to-operate, recruitment and avoided fatality risk rather than EUR/t.

## Part C: Adopt, co-develop or sell as a service

The two benchmarks point to different models:

- **Hycast (Hydro):** proprietary technology proven in Hydro's own plants, then sold as complete casthouse solutions. Examples are a EUR 9.7M Alu Ibérica contract and a NOK 200M contract with Innovation Group (China), backed by a support team and a full-scale reference centre ([Hydro](https://www.hydro.com/en/media/news/2021/hycast-signs-contract-to-deliver-state-of-the-art-casthouse-technology/), [alcircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/hydro-s-hycast-to-supply-casthouse-technology-to-spanish-aluminium-company-alu-iberica-59085)).
- **TOMRA:** equipment plus recurring service, leasing and long-term rental (financed by Eksfin and DNB), with uptime- and throughput-based contracts ([Eksfin](https://www.eksfin.no/en/cases/miljoteknologi/tomra-and-the-norwegian-deposit-return-model-rnter-australia-backed-by-eksfin-financing/)). The market is moving the same way: IFR reports robot-as-a-service fleets +31% and logistics robot-as-a-service +42% in 2024 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251007481584/en)).

| Use case | Decision | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Anode change / automatic PTM | **Co-develop** with AlumatIQ, plus a Fives/REEL machine; take IP and royalty rights | Vendors failed on the hardest tasks; our process data is the moat |
| Furnace charge/skim, pot basement cleaning, yoke repair | **Adopt** (Dynamic Concept/EPIQ, RiA) | Mature, paybacks under 2 years, no differentiation |
| Extrusion presses (HybrEx) | **Adopt** at replacement | Capex cycle decision |
| Scrap sensor-sorting | **Adopt TOMRA kit, co-develop alloy recipes** | The alloy recipes are our edge |
| Packaged potroom automation for third-party smelters (EU and Middle East) | **Sell as a service**, Hycast-style, with TOMRA-style uptime contracts | Start only after 2 internal reference potlines, about 2029 |

## So what for our company

1. **Robotics materials:** target 20–35 kt/yr of premium EU sales by 2035, in HPDC alloys, actuator and linear-axis extrusions and 7xxx forgings. Pursue framework supply deals with EU robot makers (Neura, 1X, Universal Robots, KUKA) and hedge against Mg/PEEK substitution with a Mg-competitive thin-wall casting alloy.
2. **Own plants:** about EUR 85M of capex in total: PTM retrofit EUR 12M, automatic PTMs EUR 64M (timed to the PTM replacement cycle), furnace robots EUR 7.5M. Together they deliver **~EUR 40/t** (~EUR 12M/yr) and remove about 65–80 FTE from hazardous jobs.
