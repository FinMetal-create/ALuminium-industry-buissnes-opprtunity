# Gap task (round 1)

> Benchmark the top-3 business cases against real comparable projects and market prices. (1) Scrap sorting: published capex and throughput for Hydro/PADNOS Alusort, Hydro Hueckelhoven, Novelis Nachterstedt, Speira, and TOMRA/Steinert LIBS installations; Fastmarkets/Argus EU scrap discounts (Twitch, Zorba, taint/tabor) vs P1020 to set the EUR/t spread. (2) Flex smelter: Trimet Essen actual capex, modulation and revenue disclosures; 2025-26 German, French and Nordic aFRR/mFRR capacity and energy prices, to compute EUR/yr flex revenue and a real payback. (3) Defence plate: recent plate-mill and heat-treatment investments (Constellium Issoire/Ravenswood, AMAG, Novelis, Hydro, Kaiser) for EUR/t capex; NATO/national armour-plate qualification timelines; any published prime supplier frameworks (Rheinmetall, KNDS, Naval Group). Use these to produce revenue at maturity, EBITDA %, IRR, named targets and 12-month milestones.

# Benchmarks for the top-3 business cases: scrap sorting, flex smelter, defence plate (as of 2026-10-21)

Current Fastmarkets/Argus European scrap discounts and full-year regelleistung.net auction averages sit behind paywalls or could not be retrieved; where they are missing I mark the figure **[EST]**. "Hydro Hückelhoven" was not found in any source. Hydro's German sorting asset is Dormagen.

**Prices and spread**

- **LME:** cash about $3,134/t on 6 Oct 2026 ([AlCircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/lme-aluminium-offer-price-rises-to-3-134-5-t-as-stocks-remain-unchanged-at-240-375t-121456)).
- **P1020 premium:** Rotterdam duty-paid $317.5/t in Dec 2025; from 2026 the series includes CBAM costs ([SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/103467009/Announcement-on-the-European-P1020A-aluminium-premium-price-[SMM-Price-Announcement])). All-in primary is therefore about €2,970/t.
- **Clean scrap:** clean 6063 extrusion scrap averaged LME **+€48/t** in 2026 ([Fastmarkets slides](https://www.fastmarkets.com/uploads/2026/03/Fastmarkets-aluminium-scrap-on-demand-webinar-slides.pdf)).
- **UBC:** US UBC (Argus) was $2,603/t in Sep 2026, about 84% of LME ([AlCircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/ubc-scrap-prices-in-q3-2026-values-rise-in-august-and-september-after-july-decline-121581)). EU UBC is quoted at 65–75% of LME, but from a weak source.
- **Taint/Tabor:** Fastmarkets has assessed it as % of LME since Nov 2025 ([MB-AL-0426](https://www.fastmarkets.com/commodity-prices/MB-AL-0426)), but the level is paywalled.
- **Spread I am using [EST]:** mixed Zorba/Taint at about 70–80% of LME, alloy-sorted wrought fractions at about 90–100%. That gives an uplift of 10–20 LME points, or **€270–530/t**, with a base case of €400/t.
- **Policy tailwind:** EU scrap exports were a record 1.27 Mt in 2025. The Commission has dropped the export-duty proposal in favour of non-OECD shipment restrictions from 2027 ([AlCircle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/eu-aluminium-scrap-export-measure-delayed-as-commission-shifts-from-duty-to-non-oecd-restrictions-121153)). That should widen EU scrap discounts.

**Business case: 100 kt/yr hub, 3–4 LIBS/XRT lines**

- **Capex:** €50m, i.e. €500/t, in line with Wrexham.
- **Gross value:** 90 kt sorted output × €400/t = €36m.
- **Costs:** sorting opex €60/t × 100 kt = €6m, plus about €7.5m for rejects and logistics.
- **EBITDA at maturity:** about **€22.5m**. Measured on the transfer value of sorted scrap (about €220m), that is roughly 10%.
- **IRR:** **25%** in the base case, 11% if the spread falls to 8 points, 40% at a 20-point spread. Payback is about 5 years.

**Counterparties.** Equipment from TOMRA or Steinert. Feedstock contracts with shredders, on the Sims–Hydro post-consumer supply contract model. Acquisition or JV targets: Gerhard Lang Recycling and STEMIN (illustrative; whether either would sell is not validated).

**12-month milestones.** M3: two LIBS trials on our own scrap mix. M6: two feedstock MoUs of at least 30 kt each. M9: site permit filed. M12: final investment decision, with first line live by M24.

## 2. Flex smelter: corrected payback

**Trimet benchmark**

- **Capex:** €36m, self-funded, for 120 cells in Essen hall 1 ([Light Metal Age](https://www.lightmetalage.com/news/industry-news/smelting/trimet-starts-trial-operation-of-its-virtual-battery/)).
- **Flexibility:** ±25% for up to 48 hours, and zero load for up to 1 hour.
- **Storage:** 1,120–2,000 MWh ([Eurometaux](https://eurometaux.eu/metals-with-ambition/turning-aluminium-production-into-a-virtual-battery)).
- **Implied power:** 1,120–2,000 MWh ÷ 48 h = **±23–42 MW**, so **€0.9–1.6m per MW** for a first-of-a-kind plant.
- **Revenue:** never disclosed. There is no post-2019 update on rolling it out to all four Trimet smelters.

**Prices**

- **Germany aFRR capacity:** 2025 averaged about 15 €/MW/h upward and 16 downward; 2024 was 16 and 13 ([oenergetice](https://oenergetice.cz/en/czech-energy-sector/afrr-balancing-capacity-prices-in-alpaca-minima-converge-german-daily-market-drives-peaks)). The 12–16h downward block averaged 48 €/MW/h ([Pexapark](https://pexapark.com/blog/high-downward-afrr-prices-can-drive-additional-income-for-solar-assets-in-germany-pexapark-analysis/)). Upward prices in 2026 are about 15, **35% below 2025**, because batteries are pushing prices down ([Modo](https://modoenergy.com/research/de/germany-afrr-data-explained-ko-analysis)).
- **France:** aFRR cleared at 16 upward and 12 downward in Jan 2026. The capacity mechanism for 2026-27 cleared at **€28,183/MW** ([Modo](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/france-afrr-optimisation-capacity-saturation-bess-asymmetry), [EDF](https://www.edf.fr/entreprises/le-mag/le-mag-entreprises/decryptage-du-marche-de-l-energie/mieux-comprendre-le-mecanisme-de-capacite-en-3-questions-cles)).
- **Nordic/Baltic:** Fingrid's reserve procurement cost was about €200m in 2024. Estonia's 2025 averages were aFRR downward 50.9 and mFRR upward 31.0 ([Fingrid](https://www.fingrid.fi/globalassets/dokumentit/en/electricity-market/reserves/reservimarkkinapaiva-2025-eng.pdf)).

**Calculation for a 50 MW symmetric block**

- **aFRR capacity:** 50 MW × (15 + 16) €/MW/h × 8,760 h × 60% award rate = **€8.1m/yr**. At 2026 prices it is €6.3m.
- **Day-ahead shifting:** plus €1–2m.
- **Costs:** minus €1–2m for current-efficiency loss and cell wear.
- **Net:** about **€7m/yr**, range €4–12m.

**Payback, corrected**

| Capex basis | Capex | Simple payback | IRR |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trimet first-of-a-kind cost, €1.6m/MW | €80m | 11 yr | about 4% |
| Nth-of-a-kind, €1.0m/MW [EST] | €50m | 7 yr | **7%** (range 0–16%) |
| Nth-of-a-kind, €0.7m/MW | €35m | 5 yr | 12% |

The memo's earlier payback is not supportable. On ancillary revenue alone this does **not** clear a 10% hurdle.

**The real driver is grid fees.** The German Bundesnetzagentur (federal grid regulator) plans to replace the §19 StromNEV baseload grid-fee discount (≥7,000 h/yr, constant load) with flexibility incentives by end-2028 at the latest ([Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2025/09/diskussionspapier-der-bundesnetzagentur-zur-reform-der-netzentgelte)). If that reform makes flexing the condition for keeping a grid-fee discount worth about €10m/yr [EST], IRR rises to **23%**.

**Recommendation.** Build it as a regulatory hedge, staged on one potline.

**Counterparties.** EnPot (heat-exchanger licence), Amprion or TenneT, RTE, Fingrid or Statnett, and a flexibility aggregator.

**12-month milestones.** M3: thermal model of one potline. M6: aFRR prequalification filing. M9: term sheet for the EnPot licence. M12: grid-fee ruling secured, then FID on 25–50 MW.

**Margins.** Constellium's Aerospace & Transportation segment made $339m EBITDA on 207 kt in 2025, **about €1,410/t** ([Constellium](https://www.constellium.com/news/constellium-reports-strong-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-2025-results)).

**Prices.** I found no published price for armour plate (EN 16914 standard, alloys 7017/7020/5083). My estimate is **€9–11k/t [EST]**.

**Qualification.** No NATO or national timeline is published. Constellium's heavy 5083 qualification was a two-year DoD/Army Research Laboratory project ([Constellium](https://www.constellium.com/news/aluminium-on-the-front-lines)). I plan on **18–36 months**.

**Demand caveat.** Most EU combat vehicles have steel hulls. Germany's plan for 3,000 Boxer and 3,500 Patria vehicles ([grosswald](https://www.grosswald.org/safe-defence-loans-tracker/)) mainly needs steel. Aluminium demand is in liners, logistics vehicles, bridges and naval 5083 plate. Frameworks are small: the UK Warrior aluminium armour framework was £4.8m over 4 years ([UK tender](https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/061717-2025)). **No published Rheinmetall, KNDS or Naval Group framework for aluminium plate exists.**

The precedents that do exist are tier-2:
- Metlen supplies KNDS with 200 Leopard 2A8 assemblies through 2031 ([AJ Bell](https://www.ajbell.co.uk/news/articles/metlen-defence-equipment-arm-signs-battle-tank-supply-deal-knds)).
- SAG supplies Rheinmetall's HX2 vehicle.
- Elval supplies Mevaco, which supplies Naval Group's FDI frigates ([ElvalHalcor](https://www.elvalhalcor.com/media-center/news-events/newsevent-elvalhalcor-elval-strengthening-the-defence-industry-with-cutting-edge-aluminium-technology-solutions-for-next-generation-naval-ships)).

**Business case**

| Case | Capex | Revenue at maturity | EBITDA | IRR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Greenfield 15 kt line | €120m | €135–165m | €21m (about 14%) | **7%**: reject |
| Brownfield debottleneck, 6 kt (heat-treat furnace and stretcher) | €35m | €55–65m | €8.4–12m (15–19%) | **13–19%** |

**Counterparties.** KNDS Deutschland, Rheinmetall Landsysteme, Naval Group through Mevaco, Patria, and tier-2 fabricators such as SAG.

**12-month milestones.** M3: EN 16914 trial plates. M6: start the ballistic test campaign. M9: two letters of intent from primes or tier-2s for at least 1 kt/yr. M12: FID on the brownfield debottleneck, conditional on a 5-year offtake.
