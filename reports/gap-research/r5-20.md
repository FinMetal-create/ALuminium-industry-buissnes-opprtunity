- **Premium measured in points:** a US$550/t premium on US$3,055 LME equals **18 points of LME**. This matters because a buyer who substitutes scrap for primary metal saves LME plus the premium, not LME alone.

## 3. Estimated discount to LME by grade, 2025–26 [est.]

I derived each scrap value as **recovery × value of the remelted product − conversion cost**. The assumptions are:

- Secondary alloy (DIN 226 type) worth about 100–105% of LME delivered in the EU **[est.]**.
- Wrought remelt worth about LME plus part of the premium.
- Conversion and melt cost of €200–350/t **[est.]**.

| Grade | Recoverable Al | Derived value, % of LME | Discount to LME (points) | €/t at Oct 2026 LME (€2,620) | Discount vs LME + DDP premium (points) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mixed shredded / Zorba (90–92%) | 88–91% | 75–82% | **18–25** | €1,965–2,150 | 36–43 |
| Twitch (floated, 95–98%) | 93–96% | 82–88% | **12–18** | €2,150–2,305 | 30–36 |
| Taint/Tabor (clean old sheet) | 95–97% | 85–91% | **9–15** | €2,230–2,385 | 27–33 |
| Sorted 6xxx extrusion (hub output) | 97–99% | 92–96% | **4–8** | €2,410–2,515 | 22–26 |

Three cross-checks support the band:

1. **The old Argus anchor.** A gap of about €100/t between castings scrap and taint/tabor equals about 4–5 points at today's LME. That fits the gap between neighbouring grades in the table.
2. **Export competition.** EU scrap exports hit a record 1.27 Mt in 2025, 50% above 2019 ([AL Circle / European Aluminium](https://www.alcircle.com/news/eu-delays-aluminium-scrap-export-curbs-till-september-will-india-seize-the-opportunity-120070)). EU remelters were squeezed in 2025 because Asian buyers outbid them. Both facts point to the **tight end** of each range in 2025, meaning smaller discounts.
3. **The 2026 price spike.** Scrap prices usually lag a fast LME rise, so the discount in points **widens temporarily** in a spike like the Hormuz shock of March–May 2026 **[est., standard market behaviour]**.

## 4. Testing the 12-point base and the 14-point gate

The memo does not say which spread "points" refers to, so I test both readings.

### Reading A: points are the sorting uplift (mixed shredded input to sorted wrought output)

This reading fits the hub's €150–400/t uplift case.

- **Calculation:** the uplift is the Zorba discount minus the sorted-6xxx discount, which is (18–25) − (4–8) = **10–21 points**, with a midpoint of about **15**.
- **In euros:** 10–21 points is €260–550/t at October 2026 LME, or €230–480/t on the 2025 average.
- **Against the existing case:** the €150–400/t assumption equals **6–15 points** at €2,620/t.

Verdict:
- **The 12-point base is defensible and slightly conservative.** It sits below the derived midpoint of about 15.
- **The 14-point gate sits roughly at the midpoint.** It passes in a normal or tight market. It fails in a tight 2025-type market if the feed is Twitch rather than Zorba, because the Twitch-to-sorted uplift is only (12–18) − (4–8) = 4–14 points.
- **Feed mix decides the gate. Contract Zorba or ELV shred, not Twitch.**

### Reading B: points are the discount of the purchased input to LME

- **Zorba**, at 18–25 points, clears both 12 and 14.
- **Twitch**, at 12–18 points, straddles the gate.
- **Taint/Tabor**, at 9–15 points, often fails it.

If the input is measured against **LME plus the duty-paid premium**, the true opportunity cost of primary metal, every grade clears 14 comfortably in 2026. That headroom depends on the premium, though. If the premium falls back to the 2024 level of about US$250/t, the extra points drop from about 18 to about 9.

### What the hub earns at each spread (125 kt/yr hub, €2,620/t LME) [est.]

| Spread (points) | Gross uplift | Less sorting opex (~€50–80/t ≈ 2–3 pts) | Net/yr |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 (band floor) | €26m | −€6–10m | ~€16–20m |
| 12 (base) | €39m | −€6–10m | ~€29–33m |
| 14 (gate) | €46m | −€6–10m | ~€36–40m |
| 20 (band top) | €65m | −€6–10m | ~€55–59m |

**Payback on €50m capex:**
- At the 8-point floor: about **2.5–3 years**.
- At the 12-point base: about **1.5–1.7 years**.

Even the floor of the band clears a typical hurdle rate. **The real risk is not the size of the spread but whether we can get the feed at all**, given that 1.27 Mt a year is being exported. Each point of LME is worth about **€3.3m a year** on 125 kt.
