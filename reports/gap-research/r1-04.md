# Gap task (round 1)

> Quantify EU competitiveness year by year. Power: EEX German and French baseload forwards for Cal-27 to Cal-31; Pexapark/LevelTen EU PPA price indices for Q3 2026; the EDF-Aluminium Dunkerque and Hydro 2031-40 PPA terms; the status of CISAF and indirect-cost compensation. Set a base, low and high EUR/MWh and the resulting EUR/t cash cost for our plants. CBAM: the default values and benchmarks for aluminium (CN 7601-7616) in the CBAM implementing regulations, plus EUA forward curves, to give EUR/t on imports and the net effect on our own ETS cost for each year 2026-2034. China: China customs and Eurostat data on Chinese semis and foil exports to the EU, existing EU anti-dumping duties (extrusions Reg. 2021/546, foil), the Indonesian smelter pipeline, and the China-EU price gap. For each lever, state the counter-move with its EUR impact.

# EU competitiveness, 2026–2034: power, CBAM and China, quantified

*As of 21 Oct 2026. "(e)" marks my own estimates. The agent proxy blocked direct fetches of EEX, LevelTen and EUR-Lex, so the market figures below come from secondary sources found by search. Check them against EEX/ICE settlements and the Official Journal before board use.*

- Sources: DE from [Tacto](https://www.tacto.ai/en/energy/electricity-price) and [MacroMicro](https://en.macromicro.me/series/24100/germany-baseload-power-price-1-year-forward). - I found no quotes for German Cal-29 to Cal-31. Those values are extrapolated by assuming the DE–FR spread narrows from about €37 to about €22.

**PPA prices, Q3 2026**
- Pexapark's EU composite was €45.0 in July, €46.0 in August and €48.3/MWh in September, so Q3 averaged about €46.5 ([PV Tech](https://www.pv-tech.org/pexapark-september-2026-ppa-prices-rise-5-across-europe/), [ESS News](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/08/24/european-ppa-prices-rise-2-3-in-july-spain-falls-3-9/)).
- LevelTen's Q3 index is due on 22 Oct. Its Q2 wind average was €84/MWh, and hybrid solar-plus-storage PPAs price 24% above solar ([LevelTen Q2](https://www.leveltenenergy.com/post/levelten-european-ppa-price-index-q2-2026)).
- These are pay-as-produced prices. Turning one into smelter-grade baseload adds about €15–25/MWh for firming (e), which gives roughly €62–72/MWh.

**Benchmark contracts**
- **EDF – Aluminium Dunkerque:** a 10-year CAPN nuclear contract from 1 Jan 2026, with a "significant upfront payment" and risk/benefit sharing. The price is not disclosed ([EDF](https://www.edf.fr/en/the-edf-group/dedicated-sections/journalists/all-press-releases/aluminium-dunkerque-and-edf-sign-a-memorandum-of-understanding-for-electricity-supply), [AFP](https://www.connaissancedesenergies.org/afp/electricite-edf-signe-un-contrat-sur-10-ans-avec-aluminium-dunkerque-250722)). The only public anchor is the State–EDF target of about €70/MWh average, against €42 under the old ARENH tariff. My estimate of the effective price is €60–70.
- **Hydro – Statkraft:**
  - April 2026: 12.3 TWh over 10 years (8.8 TWh in NO2, 3.5 TWh in NO3), covering 2029–2038 ([Hydro](https://hydro.com/en/global/media/news/2026/hydro-signs-long-term-power-contracts-with-statkraft/)).
  - August 2026: 876 GWh a year for 2031–2040 in NO5 ([Statkraft](https://pextcloud.statkraft.co.uk/newsroom/news-and-stories/2026/statkraft-and-hydro-sign-long-term-power-contract/)).
  - Prices are undisclosed and described only as "competitive". I estimate €35–45/MWh (e).
  - For reference, financing a smelter restart reportedly needs about $40/MWh over 10–20 years ([Al Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/europes-aluminium-sector-hit-by-rising-costs-lower-output-and-weak-demand-118732)).

**State aid**
- **CISAF:** Germany's industrial power price scheme was approved on 16 Apr 2026 (SA.120965, €3.8bn, 2026–28). It pays relief to a €50/MWh target price on 50% of consumption, against a 2026 reference price of €87.44. That is worth about €18.7/MWh averaged across a site's consumption.
  - Since 5 June the ministry has said it can be combined with indirect cost compensation (ICC), but advisers disagree on whether the same MWh can be counted twice.
  - Half of the aid must be reinvested in decarbonisation ([KPMG Law](https://kpmg-law.de/der-industriestrompreis-kostenentlastung-mit-neuen-voraussetzungen-und-nachweispflichten/), [Gleiss Lutz](https://www.gleisslutz.com/de/know-how/industriestrompreis-und-strompreiskompensation-kumulierung-bestaetigt-ausweitung-der-strompreiskompensation-genehmigt)).
  - CISAF was loosened temporarily in April 2026 for the Middle East crisis ([Cefic](https://cefic.org/news/state-aid-revision-enables-short-term-energy-crisis-relief-for-industry/)).
- **ICC:** the ETS state-aid guidelines were amended on 23 Dec 2025, raising maximum aid intensity from 75% to 80% ([EU Reporter](https://www.eureporter.co/environment/2025/12/30/commission-amends-ets-state-aid-guidelines-to-tackle-carbon-leakage-for-more-energy-intensive-industries/)). The guidelines expire in 2030. European Aluminium is asking for an extension to 2040.
  - Value per tonne = 0.80 × 0.72 tCO₂/MWh × EUA × 14.256 MWh/t. The emission factor and the 14.256 MWh/t benchmark are my recollection of the 2020 guidelines and need checking.
  - At an EUA of €85 that is about **€700/t**.
  - ICC is paid against a benchmark, so it is the same whatever contract the plant buys power on.

**Power cash cost per tonne** (14.5 MWh/t AC plus €5/MWh for grid and shaping, all (e))

| Scenario | EUR/MWh | Gross EUR/t | Net of ICC (~€700) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low: long-term nuclear/hydro contract | 50 | 725 | ~25 |
| Base: 50% long-term at €60, 50% forward-curve average | 75 | 1,090 | ~390 |
| High: German Cal-27 spot exposure | 130–145 | 1,885–2,100 | 1,185–1,400 |

What the base case means for margins:
- Total cash cost is about €2,550/t before ICC and €1,850/t after. This assumes about €1,450/t for alumina, anodes and fixed costs (e).
- Realised price is about €3,080/t: LME $3,010 plus a Rotterdam duty-paid premium of $555–580 ([LME Insight](https://www.lmeinsight.com/)), converted at 1.16 $/€ (e).
- **A German plant without a hedge loses about €800/t of margin in 2027 compared with one on a French long-term contract.**

## 2. CBAM against our own ETS cost

**Inputs**
- **China default values** (Reg. 2025/2621, Annex I replaced by 2026/1740) are 3.000 tCO₂e/t for CN 7601 (unwrought), 4.881 for 7604 (bars and profiles) and 5.559 for 7607 (foil). Mark-ups are +10% in 2026, +20% in 2027 and +30% from 2028 ([cbamfile](https://cbamfile.com/default-values/china), [SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104069166-eu-corrects-cbam-default-values-selected-aluminium-products-and-unknown-origin-precursors-affectedsmm-analysis)).
- **Benchmarks:** primary aluminium is 1.423 and secondary is 0.091 (IR 2026/1412 ETS benchmarks, [Argus](https://www.argusmedia.com/en/news-and-insights/latest-market-news/2764083-eu-lowers-cbam-benchmarks-for-aluminium)). Route-K benchmarks for 7604 are about 1.49 ([SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104086206-cbam-highlights-classification-and-origin-risks-across-eu-aluminium-bars-rods-and-non-alloy-profiles-smm-analysis)). I applied 1.49 to foil as well (e).
- **Free-allocation (CBAM) factor** under current law. The 17 July 2026 proposal COM(2026)616 would set 91.5% for 2028, 81% for 2029 and 59% for 2030, and push the end of phase-out to 2038 ([Asuene](https://asuene.com/us/blog/eu-ets-reform-2026-what-the-july-17-proposal-means-for-cbam-importers-and-carbon-costs)).
- **EUA prices:** Dec-26 is €85.4, Dec-27 €88.7 and Dec-28 €92.3 ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/symbols/ICEENDEX-ECF1!/contracts/?contract=ECFV2026)). For 2026 I used €80, reflecting the Q1 average of €75.4. Prices after 2028 rise about 4% a year (e).

**Formulas**
- Importer cost = (default value × mark-up − benchmark × factor) × EUA.
- Our ETS cost = (1.55 t direct − 1.423 × factor) × EUA. The 1.55 is an EU-average smelter assumption (e).

**How CBAM nets out on our P&L**
- The benchmark terms cancel, so the net effect on domestic sales is (importer intensity − our intensity) × EUA. This assumes the premium passes CBAM through in full.
- Against the marginal importer (UAE, India or Mozambique on actual data, about 1.8 t), that is only **€20–40/t a year**.
- CRU says the current duty-paid/unpaid spread of $79/t does not yet cover CBAM plus the 3% duty ([FT Mercati](https://www.ftmercati.com/en/european-aluminium-premiums-surge-as-supply-tightness-persists-through-q4-197280.html)).
- **Exports carry the full ETS cost with no rebate:** **€186/t by 2034** on primary-based semis, against about **€15–25/t** on high-scrap product.

**Indirect emissions are the bigger gap.**
- CBAM leaves out indirect emissions for aluminium. ICC covers about 80%, which leaves about **€165–200/t** of indirect carbon cost uncovered (e).
- If ICC is not renewed after 2030, the exposure becomes **€850–1,000/t**.

## 3. China and Indonesia

**Chinese exports**
- China's aluminium exports rose 16.3% year on year in H1 2026, reaching a record 711 kt in June. September was 656 kt ([Al Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/china-s-h1-2026-aluminium-exports-climb-16-3-led-by-711kt-tonnes-record-june-shipments-120334), [SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104156318-chinas-aluminum-exports-hit-3-month-high-in-september-up-259-yoy)).
- Plate, sheet and strip: 1.79 Mt in H1, and 3.2–3.5 Mt expected for the full year ([SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104023488-june-aluminum-platesheet-and-strip-exports-set-a-2026-monthly-high-reclaimed-transferred-orders-may-pressure-h2)).
- Foil runs at about 120 kt a month. Extrusions were 1.02 Mt in 2025, down from 1.27 Mt.

**EU imports from China**
- These were $4.51bn in 2025 ([Trading Economics/Comtrade](https://tradingeconomics.com/european-union/imports/china/aluminum)). That is roughly 1.1–1.3 Mt (e). I could not access Eurostat Comext, so this is not an official tonnage.
- Where anti-dumping duties apply, the flows have collapsed. Non-alloy extrusion imports from China fell from 3.0 kt in 2024 to 2.25 kt in 2025, and were down a further 41% in H1 2026.

**China–EU price gap**
- SHFE traded at 23,040 CNY/t including 13% VAT in mid-October. Excluding VAT that is 20,390 CNY/t, or about $2,870/t at an assumed 7.1 CNY/USD.
- EU duty-paid metal is about $3,578/t.
- **The metal gap is about $700/t (€600/t).** Add the conversion-cost gap and semis come in about €700–900/t cheaper before duties and CBAM (e).

**Indonesia**
- Output was about 1.1 Mt in 2025 and is heading for about 1.98 Mt in 2026 (Fastmarkets), with a pipeline of 13 Mt that I could not verify.
- Projects include Tsingshan's planned 800 kt at Weda Bay ($3bn), Adaro/Lygend's 500 kt and Huafon's ~500 kt ([LME Insight](https://www.lmeinsight.com/lme-insight-indepth-indonesias-aluminium-expansion-set-to-reshape-global-primary-supply/), [Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/insights/chinas-tsingshan-plans-to-build-another-aluminium-smelter-in-indonesia/)).
- These smelters run on coal power. Because CBAM prices only direct emissions, they will declare actual values of about 1.7–1.8 t and pay **€30–200/t over 2026–34**, compared with €150–470 for Chinese metal on default values.

## 4. Each lever, the counter-move and its EUR impact

| Lever | Counter-move | EUR/t impact |
|---|---|---|
| Power at €75 base | Asian rivals run captive coal power at about $40/MWh | Gross disadvantage of about €500/t (e); about €0–200 net of ICC |
| Our move: 10–15-year nuclear/hydro contract or firmed hybrid PPA at €60–65 | — | Saves about €360–500/t compared with the German forward curve for 2027–29 |
| CBAM on China defaults (€240–470 on 7601, €460–870 on semis from 2028) | Chinese exporters declare actual values (Yunnan hydro metal about 1.7 t) and shuffle resources | Cuts their CBAM bill by €190–260/t (e) |
| Our move: push the downstream scope extension and anti-circumvention rules ([EA paper](https://european-aluminium.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-02-17-EA-Position-Paper-on-CBAM-Downstream-scope-extension.pdf)) | — | Protects €300–700/t on products made from aluminium (e) |
| Anti-dumping duties at about 20–30% of a ~$3,900 semis price | Shipments through Vietnam, Thailand or Turkey, or via Chinese-owned Indonesian/Thai plants | Erodes about €700–1,000/t of protection |
| Our move: file the converter-foil expiry review now; pursue anti-circumvention cases | — | Avoids €300–600/t foil price pressure from 2027 (e) |
| Free-allocation phase-out | European Aluminium lobbies to pause it; the Commission proposes stretching it to 2038 | Moves €11/t in 2030; worth up to €186/t a year by 2034 |
| Our move: raise recycled content | — | Cuts export ETS cost by about €160/t at 2034 prices |

## 5. What this means for our company
1. **Fix the power price first.** It outweighs every other lever by three to five times. I recommend 60–70% of smelter load on 10–15-year contracts below €65/MWh: CAPN-style nuclear, Nordic hydro, or firmed hybrid PPAs at Pexapark €48 plus about €20 firming. Do not run German smelter volume unhedged into Cal-27.
2. **ICC after 2030 is a cliff worth about €850–1,000/t.** Make extending ICC to 2040, alongside CISAF, the main public-affairs objective. CBAM gives our domestic sales only €20–40/t of net benefit.
3. **Move exports toward high-scrap product.** By 2034 that saves about €160/t of ETS cost on exports, and those exports get no CBAM rebate.
4. **File for the converter-foil expiry review before 9 Dec 2026, and watch the flat-rolled and extrusion reviews.** Without these duties, Chinese semis arrive about €700–900/t below EU prices.
5. **Model Indonesia as the long-run competitor, not China.** It is outside the AD duties and pays little CBAM, with a 2–5 Mt pipeline to 2030 (e).
