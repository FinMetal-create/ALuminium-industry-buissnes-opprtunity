## 2. Sorter data: wider Fe/Cu/Zn windows and the upgraded share

- **What is published.** These sources give purity and throughput only:
  - TOMRA AUTOSORT PULSE at Gerhard Lang: 4–7 t/h, purity above 95% ([TOMRA](https://www.tomra.com/waste-metal-recycling/media-center/customer-stories/gerhard-lang)).
  - Steinert PLASMAX at STEMIN: up to 11 t/h, purity above 99% ([Steinert](https://steinertglobal.com/stemin-plasmax/)).
  - Hydro Nowa Sól: a 120 kt sorting hub with 2 HySort units ([Hydro](https://integration.hydro.com/en/global/about-hydro/hydro-worldwide/europe/poland/nowa-sol/hydro-aluminium-nowa-sol/)).
  - Constellium Neuf-Brisach: LIBS-sorted scrap above 95% purity.
- **What is missing.** I know of no public curve linking alloy window width to upgraded share from TOMRA, Steinert, Novelis or Hydro. Raabe et al. (2022, *Progress in Materials Science*), on impurity-tolerant "dirty" alloys, makes the argument but gives no yield curve. I recalled that paper and did not fetch it.
- **Estimate [est.].** Widening the windows from EN AW-6063 strict (Fe ≤0.35, Cu ≤0.10, Zn ≤0.10) to scrap-tolerant 6xxx (Fe about 0.6, Cu about 0.3, Zn about 0.25) lifts the upgraded share from about 75% to about 90% of input, with a range of +10 to +20 points.
- **What this means for the board's hub base.** Its 90% upgraded share only holds with wide windows.
- **Who closes it.** The Head of Recycling can run the M3 LIBS trial twice on the same Zorba lot, once with strict windows and once with wide windows. The extra cost is about €20–50k [est.].

## 3. A2 restated

A2 today is 100 kt × 10 points × €21.55 = **€21.55m gross**, which is 57.6% of Case A's €37.41m.

**R1: A2 as a higher upgraded share inside the hub.** The hub's uplift per input tonne is upgraded share × spread × €21.55.

| Spread | 75% share | 90% share | Gate €270 needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 points | €194 | €233 | 90% share at 13.9 points |
| 15 points (ladder) | €242 | **€291** | share of at least 83.5% |

- Wide windows are worth **+€3.9m/yr of EBITDA and +€19.2m of hub NPV** at 12 points, moving the hub from €−24.9m to €−5.7m.
- At the ladder spread of 15 points, wide windows are what lets the hub pass its own €270 gate.
- This value belongs to the hub, and the board base already counts it. Case A cannot claim it as well.

**R2: A2 as a casthouse-side saving, net of the hub.** The casthouse swaps purchased sorted or clean scrap for a lower rung of the ladder, such as taint/tabor or mixed wrought.

- **Spread:** 6–17 points on the ladder, less about 2 points of quality cost (dross, chemistry trim), gives **8 points net, range 6–10** [est.].
- **Tonnes:** min(100, C − 90 × hub load), where C is the casthouse's scrap absorption capacity.
- **Central case, C = 130 kt [est.]:** 40 kt × 8 points = **€6.9m/yr gross from 2030, down 68%**. Case A NPV falls from €99.7m to **€52.5m**.
- **Range:** C = 100 kt at 6 points gives €33.5m. C = 190 kt at 10 points gives €99.7m.

## 4. Case A plus the hub as one NPV

Basis: NPV10 at 1 Jan 2027. Hub FID at M12 (Oct 2027). Capex is re-phased to post-FID spend of €5m, €40m and €5m in 2027–29 [est.]. The ramp is the board's: Q4 2028 start-up at 12.5% load, 70% in 2029, 100% from 2030. Upgraded share is 90%.

The memo's implied total was 99.7 − 7.9 = €91.8m. That double-counts the Zorba-to-sorted spread.

## 5. What this means

1. **When casthouse scrap capacity binds, the hub and A2 compete for the same capacity.**
   - At the central case, the combination is worth €46.8–75.5m, against €85.8m for Case A on its own.
   - At a 50% chance of FID, the expected value is €66–81m.
   - Inside our casthouse, the hub only adds value above about **16 points**. With external offtake, the threshold is about **14 points**.
   - Below those spreads, its value is feed security for option C, not NPV.
2. **Case A should be approved on its own terms.**
   - Use Case A without A2 (€27.0m) plus the R2 residual.
   - Do not count a hub that has no FID.
   - Move the window value (R1) into the hub's M3 trial and its gate.
