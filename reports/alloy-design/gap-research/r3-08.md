### 1. Post-2030 ICC: the timeline problem

- **Current scheme [S/recall].** The 2021–30 ETS State-aid Guidelines are OJ C 317, 25 Sep 2020 (https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52020XC0925(01)).
  - Aid intensity is 75%. Inputs are the 14.256 MWh/t aluminium benchmark and 0.72 tCO₂/MWh.
  - Aid is paid in year t+1, so the last guaranteed receipt is in 2031, for 2030 costs.
- **Dec 2025 amendment [S].** It widened eligibility to more sectors (https://www.eureporter.co/environment/2025/12/30/commission-amends-ets-state-aid-guidelines-to-tackle-carbon-leakage-for-more-energy-intensive-industries/).
  - Nothing in the repo record says it extends the scheme beyond 2030.
  - The board paper's 80% high-case aid intensity has not been verified. It should go to the day-45 legal opinion.
- **Precedent for lead time [recall]:**
  - The 2013–20 guidelines were adopted in May 2012, 7 months before the period began.
  - The 2021–30 guidelines were adopted on 21 Sep 2020, 3 months before the period began and about 30 months after the ETS Directive amendment (EU) 2018/410.
  - The likely sequence now: ETS review proposal in 2026, co-decision in 2027–28, then guidelines between mid-2029 and Q3 2030. Germany then has to notify its national scheme (SPK), which adds 3–9 months.
- **Estimate.** There is a 25–40% chance [est.] that a final post-2030 guideline exists by **31 Dec 2029**, the board's closure-decision date. The Board will most likely decide on a draft text.
- **Further risk [recall].** CBAM for aluminium covers direct emissions only. If the Commission adds indirect emissions to CBAM, it may argue that ICC is less needed.
- **Who closes this:** Regulatory Affairs with European Aluminium. They should track the ETS-review proposal and the DG COMP consultation dates.

### 2. Curtailment evidence for 2022–26: how the casthouses ran

| Site | Potlines | What the casthouse did afterwards | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speira Rheinwerk (Neuss) | Cut by 50% in Oct 2022 [recall]; fully curtailed since 2023 [S, repo 06] | Kept feeding the rolling mill on purchased primary and scrap [recall]. A €40m, about 60 kt/yr UBC recycling furnace was commissioned in Oct 2026, which works out to **€480–670 per t/yr of melt capacity** [S: https://www.alcircle.com/news/60kt-a-year-red-bull-backs-speiras-40m-aluminium-recycling-furnace-at-rheinwerk-121519; repo r1-02] | The casthouse became a recycling hub |
| Slovalco (Hydro) | Primary production stopped by about Sep 2022 [recall] | The casthouse kept running on scrap and purchased metal [recall, verify]. A 75 kt restart costing €100m is planned for Q4 2026, **conditional on Slovak ICC approval and a 10-year hydro power contract** [S: https://www.hydro.com/en/global/media/news/2026/slovalco-to-restart-75000-tonnes-of-curtailed-aluminium-capacity-supplying-a-critical-raw-material-for-europe/] | ICC is the gate for the restart |
| Aldel (Delfzijl) | Electrolysis halted in late 2021 [recall] | Continued as a remelt and casting operation [recall] | Not verified |
| Trimet (Essen, Hamburg, Voerde) | Partial curtailments in 2021–23 [recall] | Rod, ingot and foundry-alloy lines kept running on purchased and recycled metal [recall]. Essen added a 120-cell flex retrofit for €36m [S] | Not verified |

**What the cases show.** In every case the casthouse outlived the potlines and moved toward scrap. That makes sense because purchased P1020 is the most expensive input: it carries the duty-paid premium of about €276/t. No operator published a remelt margin.

**Implication for us.** EC-grade conductor rod (1350/1370) and Al-Zr rod cannot be made from post-consumer scrap; they need P1020 or clean 1xxx metal [recall, metallurgy]. B-rod is therefore our line most exposed to the P1020 premium.

### 4. Ledger scenario: potlines curtailed from 2030

From 2030:
- Spare primary falls from 45 kt to **0**.
- Rod needs 57.6 kt of purchased P1020. NHT needs 10.8 kt of purchased P1020, i.e. 0.36 t per t of alloy.
- The 8.3 kt of hub wrought scrap no longer has to be backfilled in billet.
- Total purchases are **68.4 kt/yr × €2,431 ≈ €166m/yr**. That adds an estimated €7m of working capital, about **−€3m of NPV** that is not in the runs below.

| NPV10 in €m (+10% contingency) | Ledger, no curtailment | Curtailed 2030, penalty 70 / 115 / 160 | Curtailed 2031, penalty 115 |
|---|---|---|---|
| B-rod, central adders | −0.5 | +9.2 / **−1.8** / −10.2 | +1.6 |
| B-rod, LOW / HIGH adders | −15.2 / — | — / −21.0 (LOW) / +16.3 (HIGH) | −19.2 / +19.1 |
| B-rod without Al-Zr | −14.9 | — / −17.6 / — | −14.0 |
| B-rod plus a €10m solid-feed melter | — | — / **−9.4** / — | — |
| NHT, 30 kt | −4.1 | −7.0 / **−8.8** / −10.7 | −8.4 |

**What this means:**
- **B-rod is barely worse off when curtailed (−1.8 against −0.5).** In the base ledger, 55% of rod displaces billet at a poor margin. Curtailment swaps that constraint for the remelt penalty.
- **But B-rod is very sensitive to the remelt penalty.** Each €45/t moves NPV by about €10m, and the break-even penalty is about **€108/t**.
- **If B-rod has to fund its own melting capacity, it fails clearly.**
- **At mid-cycle, rod margin from purchased ingot is €40/t.** It is 230 − 75 − 115, against €155/t from liquid metal. That gives steady-state EBITDA of €6.0m/yr including Al-Zr.

### 5. Matching gate tests

Both gates must be passed at FID, because the guideline will probably not exist before both lines are built.

**B-rod: curtailment gate (in addition to the existing 45.5 kt spare-primary gate).** B-rod needs NPV10 ≥ 0 with curtailment from 2030, a €115/t remelt penalty and central adders. Today it is −€1.8m, so it **fails**. It passes on any one of these:
1. Offtakes covering at least 50% of 57 kt with a fixed adder floor of **US$313/t or more over P1020 duty-paid**.
   - Mid-cycle today is US$267/t.
   - The floor rises to US$350/t if a melter is charged to the case, and to US$384/t without Al-Zr.
2. A measured remelt and logistics penalty of **€108/t or less**, from Controlling's cost data plus a quote, **and** solid-melt capacity supplied by the option-C casthouse at no charge to B-rod.
3. Al-Zr volume of 7.5 kt at €500/t contracted. This is a precondition for routes 1 and 2.

**NHT: curtailment gate.** NHT needs NPV10 ≥ 0 when curtailed from 2030. Today it is −€8.8m, so it **fails**. It passes on either of these:
1. A signed premium of **€343/t or more over P1020 duty-paid at 30 kt**, against €303/t without curtailment.
2. Volume of **43 kt or more** at the base contribution, or 40 kt at a premium of €283/t or more.
   - The €6.3m grant on its own is not enough: −8.8 + about 5.5 = −3.3.

**Combined.** If both lines are built and the potlines close in 2030, the two lines together go from −€4.6m to −€10.6m NPV, before the roughly €3m working-capital effect.
