# EU mega-casting alloy demand and a revised NHT gate

## Bottom line
- **The demand estimate is restated.** EU structural mega-casting alloy demand in 2030 is about **128 kt/yr, range 50–256 kt** [est.]. I built it up from named OEM programmes. The memo's 250–400 kt matches the whole structural HPDC pool, both heat-treated and NHT. Mega-casting is only part of that pool.
- **The margin is restated.** Measured against P1020/billet, NHT contribution is **€231/t on a P1020 basis and €177/t on a billet basis**, not €280/t. At 30 kt with contingency, that gives **NPV −€1.8m (IRR 8.9%) and −€9.1m (IRR 3.9%)**.
- **The 28 kt gate fails.** With contingency, break-even is **31.9 kt (P1020 basis) or 43.3 kt (billet basis)**. I propose a joint volume × contribution gate of **at least €7.6m/yr contracted contribution, with volume at least 25 kt**.
- **The demand figure is still not sourced.** I could not reach any published Ducker, CRU or European Aluminium forecast in this session. The web search budget was used up and the egress proxy refused connections. So it rests on figures earlier rounds cited plus a reasoned estimate.

**Check against machine count.** The central case needs about 2.2m castings a year. At 98–162k castings per cell a year (100–120 s cycle, 65–75% OEE, 5,000–6,000 h), that is about **22–28 cells of 6,000 t or more**. This is consistent with an EU installed and ordered base of roughly 25–40 cells by 2030 [est.].

**Check against value.** The global gigacasting market is put at $1.2bn in 2030 ([GII](https://giikorea.co.kr/report/tsci1764226-gigacasting-market-global-industry-size-share.html)). At about $3,000/t that is about 400 kt. A 20–30% EU share gives **80–120 kt**, consistent with the central case.

**Where 250–400 kt comes from.** Ducker puts castings at 145 kg/car in 2030 ([Ducker/European Aluminium](https://european-aluminium.eu/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/23-05-02Aluminum-Content-in-Cars_Public-Summary.pdf)). Times 11.5m cars, that is 1.67 Mt. A 15–25% structural share gives **250–420 kt**, which is all structural HPDC. The memo's range therefore overstates the mega-casting NHT market by about 2–3 times.

**Share test.** A 30 kt plan is **23% of the central market**, 60% of the low case and 12% of the high case. The other credible low-Fe suppliers have primary-metal access: Hydro/Alumetal, Trimet, Speira, Rheinfelden licensees, Rio Tinto and Alcoa (C611). Against them, **10–20% (13–26 kt)** is a realistic entrant share [est.].

## 2. NHT contribution restated against P1020/billet

Inputs per tonne of alloy:
- LME €2,155/t.
- Rotterdam duty-paid premium US$320/t, which is €276/t. So **P1020 DP = €2,431/t** (memo basis).
- NHT price is the memo's €2,700/t, which is **P1020 DP + €269/t**.

Recipe (Al units 0.89 t, of which 40% primary and 60% scrap):

| Cost item | Value |
|---|---|
| Primary metal | 0.36 t (1% melt loss) |
| Scrap | 0.55 t (3% melt loss) |
| Silicon | 0.10 t at €2,100/t [est.] |
| Mn/Mo/Zr/Sr additions | €55/t [est.] |
| Variable conversion | €150/t [est.] |
| Freight | €35/t [est.] |
| Cast scrap from the hub | 85% of LME [est.] |
| Clean wrought scrap (internal alternative: substitutes primary in billet) | P1020 DP − €100 remelt cost [est.] |
| Billet margin over P1020 | €150/t [est.] |

| Basis | Primary valued at | Scrap valued at | Contribution €/t (range) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Memo | AlSi9Cu3 benchmark | — | 280 |
| A: market scrap | P1020 DP | 85% of LME | 368 (149–649) |
| **B: P1020** | P1020 DP | half market, half billet substitution | **231 (24–532)** |
| **C: billet** | P1020 DP + €150 | as in B | **177 (−66 to +505)** |

**Example, basis C:**
- Costs: 928 (primary) + 1,145 (scrap) + 210 (silicon) + 240 (additions, conversion and freight) = €2,523/t.
- Contribution: €2,700 − €2,523 = **€177/t**.

**Biggest sensitivities (basis C):**
- Price premium over P1020 of €180 to €400/t: contribution €88 to €308/t.
- Share of clean wrought scrap in the mix from 0% to 100%: contribution €314 to €40/t.

## 3. Capex gate on the contingency-inclusive break-even

**Model.** I used the swarm's NHT model and added 10% contingency:
- capex €19.8m (memo base €18m), with a €3.6m grant;
- qualification opex €1.65m, €2.2m and €0.55m over the first three years;
- fixed cost €2.0m/yr;
- 15-year life, 10% discount rate, 25% tax.

**Calibration.** At €280/t and 28 kt this model gives 11.5% and +€2.5m. The memo's figures are 11.7% and +€2.6m. The memo's low case is −€13.9m; this model's comparable case (20 kt at €177/t) gives −€16.1m.

| Contribution €/t | NPV at 30 kt | IRR at 30 kt | NPV at 28 kt | Break-even volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 280 (memo) | +4.8 | 12.8% | +2.5 | 25.8 kt |
| 231 (P1020 basis) | −1.8 | 8.9% | −3.7 | **31.9 kt** |
| 177 (billet basis) | −9.1 | 3.9% | −10.5 | **43.3 kt** |

**Break-even curve.**

| Volume | Break-even contribution | Price premium over P1020 DP needed |
|---|---|---|
| 20 kt | €354/t | €446/t |
| 25 kt | €288/t | €380/t |
| 28 kt | €260/t | €352/t |
| 30 kt | €244/t | €336/t |
| 35 kt | €213/t | €305/t |
| 40 kt | €189/t | €281/t |

Volume × break-even contribution comes out at about **€7.1–7.6m/yr** across this range.

**Proposed FID gate, replacing "28 kt":**
1. Signed OEM or Tier-1 nominations of **at least 25 kt/yr**.
2. Contracted **volume × billet-basis contribution of at least €7.6m/yr**. For example, 30 kt at a price of at least P1020 DP + €336/t.
3. Both tested on capex including contingency.

The current price of P1020 + €269 fails at any volume below 43 kt, and 43 kt is above a realistic share of the market.

## Implications
- **NHT moves from marginal GO to WATCH.** The €0.8m qualification-only spend continues. Capex is released only when the joint gate is met.
- **Supply contracts should bring in OEM returns.** Every 10 percentage points of the scrap mix shifted from wrought scrap to OEM returns adds about €27/t [calc].
