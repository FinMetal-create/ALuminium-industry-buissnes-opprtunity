## 2. Copper-substitution figures, re-run

- **Equal conductance:** Al/Cu mass = (1/0.61) × 2.70 / 8.96 = **0.494**. Aluminium is cheaper on metal at any ratio above 0.49x.
- **Saving on metal cost per unit of conductance** = 1 − 0.494 / R:
  - Spot 4.90x: **89.9%**
  - 2026 average 4.45x: **88.9%**
  - Normalised 3.80x: **87.0%**
- **Product level at spot**, adding a Rotterdam Al premium of $567.5 and an EU Cu cathode premium of $150–350 [est.]: ratio 4.15–4.21x, saving **88.1–88.3%**.
- **The "50–60%" claim is withdrawn.** It is neither the metal saving (~87–90%) nor a sourced installed saving. Installed-system savings are 20–40% [est.], because aluminium needs 1.64x the cross-section and needs joints, terminations and insulation. Engineering (busbar and transformer OEM data) should confirm this.
- **Customer saving per tonne of copper replaced:**
  - Spot: (14,400 − 0.494 × 2,940) / 1.1215 = **€11,545**
  - Normalised: (9,500 − 0.494 × 2,500) / 1.16 = **€7,125**
- **Our share of that saving:** our conversion premium of €1,200/t Al × 0.494 = **€593 per t of Cu replaced**, about 5–8% of the customer's saving. That premium is defensible at any of the three bases.
- **Demand:**
  - Formula: 27 Mt global refined Cu × displacement share × 0.494
  - 2026 at 2% displacement: **0.27 Mt Al**
  - 2030 at 6% displacement (JPMorgan, [Reuters/TimesLive](https://www.timeslive.co.za/motoring/2026-06-30-ferrari-and-bmw-follow-tesla-and-chinese-brands-in-shift-to-cheaper-aluminium-wiring)): **0.80 Mt Al**
  - EU at a 10–15% share: **27–40 kt (2026)** and **80–120 kt (2030)** [est.]
  - Both endpoints now reproduce.

## 3. Case A: Cu→Al conductor line (busbars, plated extrusions, winding strip)

**Inputs** [est. unless noted]:
- Capex €25m (€20–32m), spent 60% in 2027 and 40% in 2028
- Grant 15%: €3.75m (national or JTF aid)
- Pre-revenue application engineering €1.5m/yr in 2027–28
- Volume 15 kt at normalised prices (2028/29/30 ramp 30/60/100%), 18 kt at the 2026 average, 20 kt at spot
- Contribution €500/t (conversion premium €1,200 minus €700 variable cost)
- **Fixed opex €2.5m/yr** (12 FTE at €120k plus €1m for lab and certification)
- Working capital 12% of revenue, on revenue of €3,630/t
- Board basis: tax 25%, 15-year life, straight-line depreciation, sustaining capex 2% from year 3 of operation, hurdle 10%

**Steady-state EBITDA** = 15 × 500 / 1,000 − 2.5 = **€5.0m/yr**

| Run | NPV@10% | IRR |
|---|---|---|
| **Normalised 3.8x** | **−€5.7m** | **7.0%** |
| Normalised, no grant | −€8.9m | 5.7% |
| Normalised, plus 10% contingency | −€8.1m | 5.9% |
| 2026 average 4.45x (18 kt) | +€0.5m | 10.3% |
| Spot 4.9x (20 kt) | +€4.7m | 12.2% |
| A range, low (€32m, 12 kt, €400/t) | −€28.0m | −5.5% |
| A range, high (€20m, 20 kt, €600/t) | +€22.0m | 21.5% |

**Break-even** is about **18 kt at €500/t**. Case A clears the hurdle only above normalised prices. FID should therefore be gated on contracted offtake of ≥18 kt, not on the price ratio. Sales and Engineering close this with OEM LOIs (Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy, Schneider).

## 4. Case B: non-heat-treatable (NHT) structural HPDC alloy (casthouse, ≥70% recycled content)

**Inputs** [est.]:
- Capex €18m (€14–24m), spent 40% in 2027 and 60% in 2028
- Grant 20%: €3.6m
- OEM qualification €4m over 2027–29
- 30 kt (20–40 kt), ramping 20/60/100%
- Contribution €280/t (€200–350), from the scrap-versus-primary spread and the alloy premium
- Fixed opex €1.5m/yr
- Working capital 8% of revenue

**Results:**
- EBITDA **€6.9m/yr**
- **NPV +€9.5m, IRR 15.9%**; with contingency +€7.8m and 14.6%
- **B range:** NPV −€19.1m to +€39.6m, IRR −3.0% to 34.8%

The B case does not depend on the Cu/Al ratio. Its key driver is the scrap spread of 8–20 points (board paper).
