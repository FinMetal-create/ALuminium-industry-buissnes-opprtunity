- **The key structure [est.]:** conductor rod is priced roughly at cost-plus over ingot, at P1020 plus about $350–500/t. Its premium over ingot is therefore fairly stable. The billet premium over ingot is the volatile one: it ran $220–630/t in 2019–25 and about $750/t now.

### 2. The billet opportunity cost

Both products start from the same liquid metal, so I measure each one's margin over selling that metal as P1020 ingot. EUR/USD is 0.862, from the board deck (US$2,500 = €2,155).

- **Rod (B1):** net margin over ingot is **€333/t**, taken from the rejected memo. That implies the rod sells at about P1020 + $450, after about €55/t of extra conversion cost.
- **Billet:** margin over ingot = (billet premium − P1020 premium) × 0.862 − €115/t of extra conversion [est. €90–150]. The €115 covers DC casting, homogenising, sawing and Mg/Si additions.
- **Breakeven:** rod beats billet only when billet minus P1020 is below (333 + 115) / 0.862 = **$520/t**. At today's P1020 premium of about $450, that means a billet premium below about $970/t.

| Case | Billet minus P1020 | Billet margin over ingot | Rod advantage over billet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mid-cycle (2019–25 average) | $350 | €187/t | **+€146/t** |
| Peak, 2022 | $630 | €428/t | −€95/t |
| Current, 2026 | $750 | €532/t | **−€198/t** |

- In the recalled history, rod would have beaten billet in **6 of 8 years**. It lost only in 2022 and 2026, the two peak years.
- So at today's $1,175–1,250/t billet premium, **B destroys about €11m a year** (57 kt × €198/t) *if every rod tonne displaces a billet tonne*.

### 3. Cash table for Case B

Assumptions:
- **Line:** 60 kt/yr nameplate; output 15 kt in 2028, 45 kt in 2029, then 57 kt a year.
- **Capex:** €36m, split 60% in 2027 and 40% in 2028.
- **Running costs:** €2m of working capital in 2028, released in 2042; €0.5m a year of sustaining capex from 2030.
- **Opex:** inside the €333/t net margin.
- **Tax and timing:** 25% tax, 15-year straight-line depreciation, no loss carry-forward (conservative), horizon 2027–2042.
- **Valuation:** NPV at the 10% hurdle.

### 4. Conclusions for the memo

1. **The first condition depends on what rod replaces, more than on the premium level.** If our casthouse still sells P1020, sow or T-bar outside, rod upgrades that metal and clears every case, including the peak (S1, 33%). If billet capacity is the marginal product, B only clears the hurdle at mid-cycle (S2, 13.5%). Through a replayed cycle it misses (S4, 7.0%), and at today's spread it destroys value.
2. **I suggest rewording the GO condition.** "Rod beats billet through the cycle" could be replaced by three tests:
   - (a) at least 40 kt/yr of the casthouse's current output is ingot or sow sold outside, or extra liquid metal is available; **or**
   - (b) a rod offtake (Nexans–Hydro template) priced at least P1020 + $520/t, so it beats billet even at peak; **and**
   - (c) firm capex of €40m or less.
3. **Contract structure:** price the rod offtake as an adder over P1020, with no fixed €/t. Keep the billet/rod swing flexible where the contracts allow it. S5 shows that flexibility is worth about 4 IRR points.
