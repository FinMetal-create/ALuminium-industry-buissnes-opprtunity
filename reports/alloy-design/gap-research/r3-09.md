# A1 versus the furnace-robot case: netting the yield claim

## Bottom line

The furnace-robot case keeps its full **EUR 7.3/t** metal-loss saving. A1 (AI charge optimisation) should be restated from **EUR 10/t (EUR 3.0m/yr)** to **EUR 7.8/t (EUR 2.35m/yr)** in the base case, range EUR 3.7–12.3/t. The minimum defensible floor is **EUR 6.4/t (EUR 1.92m/yr)**: the charge-cost component only, with no yield credit at all.

This takes **EUR 0.65m/yr gross** out of the double count (range 0.38–0.81). After Case A's 20% haircut and 25% tax, Case A's NPV10 falls by about **EUR 2.1m** (range 1.2–2.6) [est.].

## 2. Splitting A1 (memo basis: EUR 5–15/t, base 10, on 300 kt)

A1's two stated mechanisms are 2–5 percentage points less sweetening and 0.2–0.5 percentage points better yield. Both are valued on the memo's own unit values:

- **Sweetening:** replacing primary metal with scrap is worth the A3 spread of 20 LME points = **EUR 431/t**.
  - Low: 2% × 431 = **EUR 8.6/t**
  - Base: 3.5% × 431 = **EUR 15.1/t**
  - High: 5% × 431 = **EUR 21.6/t**
- **Yield:** metal is valued at duty-paid P1020 = 2,155 + 320/1.16 = **EUR 2,431/t**, the same basis that gives the robot case its 0.3% × 2,431 = EUR 7.29/t.
  - Low: 0.2% × 2,431 = **EUR 4.9/t**
  - Base: 0.35% × 2,431 = **EUR 8.5/t**
  - High: 0.5% × 2,431 = **EUR 12.2/t**
- **Gross before the cap:** EUR 13.5 / 23.6 / 33.7 per t. The yield share is **36.1%** in all three cases.
- **Applied to A1's capped EUR 5/10/15 per t:**
  - Yield component: **EUR 1.8 / 3.6 / 5.4 per t** (EUR 0.54 / 1.08 / 1.62m/yr)
  - Charge-cost component: **EUR 3.2 / 6.4 / 9.6 per t** (EUR 0.96 / 1.92 / 2.88m/yr)

## 3. Netting against the robot's EUR 7.3/t

**Plausibility test.** Taken together, the two papers claim 0.5–0.8 percentage points of melt-loss reduction on the same 300 kt. Casthouse melt loss that either lever can address (oxidation during charging, door-open time, metal carried out with the dross) is about **1.0–2.5%** of throughput [est.]. Two levers that do not change the melting technology would then remove 25–50% of total loss. That is not credible, so the overlap is real.

**Mechanism split of A1's yield component** [est.]:

1. **Oxidation and dross, 50–70% (base 60%).** This comes from charge sequencing and shorter time at temperature. It is the same pool the robots address through faster charging, shorter door-open time and cleaner skims. **The robot case owns this part. A1 gets zero for it.**
2. **Charge-chemistry yield, 30–50% (base 40%).** This comes from fewer off-spec melts, less dilution and rework, and fewer trims. The robots do not touch it. **A1 keeps this part.**

**A1 yield credit retained:**
- Low: 1.8 × 0.3 = EUR 0.54/t
- Base: 3.6 × 0.4 = EUR 1.44/t (about 0.06 percentage points)
- High: 5.4 × 0.5 = EUR 2.7/t

**A1 net (charge-cost component plus retained yield):**

| Case | EUR/t | EUR m/yr | Double count removed (EUR m/yr gross) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 3.2 + 0.5 = **3.7** | 1.12 | 0.38 |
| Base | 6.4 + 1.4 = **7.8** | 2.35 | 0.65 |
| High | 9.6 + 2.7 = **12.3** | 3.69 | 0.81 |

**Combined Board view, base case:** robot metal loss EUR 7.3/t + A1 EUR 7.8/t = **EUR 15.1/t**, not 17.3. The implied total metal-loss gain is about **0.36 percentage points**, not 0.65, which is plausible.

**Effect on Case A:** EUR 0.65m × 0.8 haircut × 0.75 after tax = EUR 0.39m/yr. Discounted over 2030–40 at 10% (annuity factor 5.37 to 1 Jan 2027), that is about **−EUR 2.1m NPV10** (range −1.2 to −2.6). Case A stays at about EUR 97m, so no decision changes.

## 4. Recommended Board rules

1. **One owner per loss pool.** The robot case owns furnace-practice melt loss, which M12 measures (at least 0.25%). A1 is credited only for charge cost and chemistry-driven yield.
2. **Sequenced measurement.** The A1 shadow run (T2 gate of at least EUR 5/t) is measured on furnaces that already have robots, or against a baseline adjusted for robots. Its yield credit is booked only where the melt-by-melt metal balance shows loss reduction **beyond** the robot's measured saving.
3. **A1 gate restated on charge cost alone:** at least EUR 4/t from primary sweetening and alloy additions, verified on the optimiser's cost logs.
4. **Second overlap to fix:** A1's 2–5 points less sweetening is 6–15 kt of primary replaced by scrap at the same EUR 431/t that A3 uses (15 kt). The CFO must confirm that A1 and A3 are not the same tonnes. If they are, a further EUR 2.6–6.5m/yr gross is at risk [est.].
