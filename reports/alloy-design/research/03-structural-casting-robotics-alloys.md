## Structural Casting, Gigacasting & Robot-Grade Alloys

*Research cut-off 2026-10-25. Method note: the agent proxy refused WebFetch for alcircle.com, euroguss.de, crugroup.com, faxiangongchang.com and patents.google.com in this session, so figures from those sites come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages. I could not find any public price for proprietary structural alloys. Every premium below is therefore marked [est.], and I explain how each estimate was built.*

### 1. Why this niche matters

Large high-pressure die castings (HPDC) cannot go through solution heat treatment and quenching (T6/T7): a single-piece rear underbody distorts. The answer was a new class of **non-heat-treatable (NHT) structural alloys**. They reach about 8-12% elongation and about 120-150 MPa yield strength as cast, or after the paint-bake cycle. These alloys depend on alloy design. That makes them the part of the casting value chain where a smelter-casthouse can earn a premium, as opposed to selling commodity AlSi9Cu3 secondary ingot.

Tesla started the move by replacing 171 stamped parts in the Model Y with 2 rear castings, cutting direct manufacturing cost by up to 40% (https://www.lightmetalage.com/news/industry-news/automotive/giga-castings-in-the-automotive-industry-an-updated-review/). The 2026 Cybercab "unboxed" architecture keeps large front and rear castings, combined with hot-stamped door rings, and halves line length (https://teslanorth.com/2026/09/08/tesla-cybercab-unboxed-line-size).

### 3. Market size and tonnage

Published gigacasting **value** forecasts disagree. One puts the market at USD 0.2 bn (2024) rising to 1.2 bn (2030). Another puts it at USD 3.09 bn (2024) rising to 7.4 bn (2033) (https://giikorea.co.kr/report/tsci1764226-gigacasting-market-global-industry-size-share.html; https://www.giikorea.co.kr/report/sky1854244-gigacasting-market-size-share-growth-analysis-by.html). anp puts EV die-cast chassis at USD 751 m, rising to 2.6 bn by 2030 (https://www.euroguss.de/en/euroguss-365/2024/expert-knowledge/gigacasting-and-gigapress-the-future-of-vehicle-manufacturing). The Chinese broker Pacific Securities assumes 50 M NEVs × 300 kg of integrated castings in 2030, which gives an NHT alloy market of CNY 300 bn. I treat that as a ceiling, not a forecast (https://www.cs.com.cn/ssgs/gsxw/202305/t20230531_6347491.html).

**My bottom-up tonnage estimate [est.]:**
- **Per press cell:** about 120 s cycle, 35% utilisation, about 100 kg shot. That gives about 8 kt of metal melted per cell per year, or about 5-6 kt net alloy purchased after in-house returns.
- **China:** about 100 cells gives about 0.5-0.6 Mt/yr of NHT alloy in 2026.
- **Global gigacasting and megacasting:** about 0.7-1.0 Mt in 2026, rising to 1.5-2.5 Mt by 2030.
- **Europe:** gigacasting alone is about 40-80 kt in 2026, rising to 100-200 kt by 2030. Adding the wider NHT structural HPDC market (shock towers, battery frames, subframes) gives about 250-400 kt in 2030.

For scale, China alone produced 7.95 Mt of aluminium die castings in 2023 (https://www.chinabaogao.com/market/202505/752432.html).

**Price and premium [est.].**
- **China:** NHT alloy carries little premium. The Lizhong contract implies about CNY 20,000/t, roughly EUR 2,400/t, which is close to the ADC12 spot price of CNY 21,700-23,400/t (https://news.metal.com/newscontent/103658440/).
- **Europe:** primary-based low-Fe AlSi10MnMg or AlMg5Si2Mn is built up from LME (EUR 2,155) + duty-paid premium (EUR 250-350) + alloying and conversion (EUR 250-450). That gives about **EUR 2,650-2,950/t**, which is **EUR 300-700/t above standard AlSi9Cu3 secondary ingot**.
- **Low-carbon add-on:** a further EUR 50-150/t can be achieved for certified low-CO2 metal.
- **Chinese pricing pressure:** Lizhong's claim of 15-20% below Western alloy prices means Europe's premium is under threat once Chinese alloy or castings arrive in Europe.

### 4. Robot-grade alloys

**Demand:**
- CRU estimates **17-25 kg of aluminium per humanoid**, the largest metal share after steel. Its moderate case is 400 M robots per year by 2050, which would need about 10 Mt of aluminium (https://www.crugroup.com/en/communities/thought-leadership/2025/the-next-commodity-battleground-humanoid-robots/).
- Chinese industry sources project **100-125 kt of humanoid aluminium by 2030**. This is an unverified expo piece (https://aluminiumchina.com/en-gb/news-center/industry-news/2025/4/5.html).
- My check: 1-3 M robots in 2030 × 20-25 kg of finished parts gives 20-75 kt of finished parts. After machining scrap (buy-to-fly ratio of 2-4×), that is **50-200 kt of semi-finished product**, mostly 6061/6082/7075 bar, plate and extrusion plus thin-wall HPDC [est.].

**Applications today:**
- **6xxx:** frames, housings, link arms (https://www.goodfellow.com/usa/resources/advanced-materials-for-humanoid-robots/).
- **7075-T6:** joints, at about 503-572 MPa yield strength.
- **Ti-6Al-4V:** hip and gearbox nodes.
- **PEEK:** reported in Optimus Gen2 (unverified).
- **HPDC:** Samkee won a humanoid upper-body frame die-casting programme in 2026 (https://www.alcircle.com/news/samkee-chosen-to-develop-lightweight-humanoid-robot-aluminium-die-casting-to-play-a-key-role-120046).

**Competing materials:**
- **Magnesium** is the main threat. AgiBot (Zhiyuan) uses a magnesium skeleton (https://news.metal.com/en/newscontent/104033658-zhiyuan-robotics-uses-magnesium-alloy-for-lightweight-strong-skeleton-in-new-humanoid-robot). Chinese Mg ingot is cheap at CNY 15,400-15,500/t, or FOB USD 2,190-2,290/t, which is *below* primary aluminium per tonne and about 35% lighter by volume (https://news.metal.com/en/newscontent/104004424-firm-prices-and-concessions-coexist-for-magnesium-ingot-weak-demand-drags-transactions-smm-magnesium-ingot-spot-brief).
- **Aluminium's counter for EU buyers:** supply security (Mg is an EU critical raw material with China export controls), corrosion resistance, recyclability and machinability.
- **PEEK and CFRP** win in covers and links where weight matters most. Vendor claims of 60-70% weight savings over aluminium housings are unsourced (https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/articles/cut-humanoid-robot-joint-actuator-weight-by-40-50/).

**Thermal-conductivity alloys for motor and actuator housings.** Standard Al-Si HPDC alloys reach 90-140 W/mK, and the market wants at least 160 W/mK (https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9663847). AT237 (41% IACS, about 165-175 W/mK by Wiedemann-Franz [est.]) and Tesla's 46-55% IACS patent family define the frontier. A thin-wall alloy with **≥45% IACS and ≥150 MPa yield strength** that tolerates recycled Fe is not yet commercially available in Europe.

### 5. Where AI-designed alloys can win

- **Fewer experiments needed.**
  - MIT's ML loop found a printable alloy five times stronger than its cast counterpart after evaluating only **40 compositions instead of about 1 M** (https://news.mit.edu/index%2Ephp/2025/printable-aluminum-alloy-sets-strength-records-may-enable-lighter-aircraft-parts-1007).
  - ML-driven NHT Al-Si-Mg design for integrated die casting (Dong et al., J. Alloys Compd. 2025) and Fe-tolerance modelling for Al5.5Mg2Si (Li et al., Adv. Eng. Mater. 2025) (https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/31672).
  - HPDC inverse design aiming at UTS above 300 MPa with elongation above 8% (Choi et al., *Metals* 2026) (https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4701/16/1/99).
- **Faster development.** Development time is falling from the classic 5-10 years to **15-36 months** from concept to a qualified part (RidgeAlloy took 15 months to a full-scale part). OEM qualification still adds **2-4 years** [est.]: trial lots, crash and fatigue testing, an OEM material specification, and PPAP/IATF. The Rusal VW/BMW staged trials show the multi-step pattern (https://www.foundry-planet.com/d/uc-rusal-alloys-to-be-qualified-by-leading-european-automotive-companies/).
- **Development cost.** About **EUR 3-8 m per alloy** including castings trials, versus EUR 10-20 m by conventional methods [est.].
- **Best use: tolerating impurities.** Models that map Fe, Cu, Zn and Mn from *sorted scrap* to as-cast ductility let a producer price its alloy against its own scrap mix. That links directly to our 100 kt sorting hub.

### So what for our company

1. **We have the inputs.** Low-Fe primary metal, a casthouse, and soon a 100 kt AI-sorted scrap stream are exactly what NHT structural alloys need. Most European die-casters lack alloy R&D, and German die-casting margins are 5-7%.
2. **Shift from commodity ingot to designed alloys.** At a EUR 300-700/t premium, 40 kt/yr of structural alloy is worth **EUR 12-28 m/yr** of extra gross margin [est.].
3. **Use the scrap hub for scrap-tolerant grades.** An alloy designed with ML to accept 0.3-0.6% Fe from our own sorted scrap at 30-60% recycled content lets us capture the scrap grade spread of 8-20 LME points (EUR 170-430/t) *and* the low-CO2 premium.
4. **Close the loop with customers.** Taking back gigacasting runners, biscuits and scrap castings secures clean scrap feed.
5. **Robotics is small but high value.** It will not fill a smelter before 2030, but high-strength 6xxx/7xxx bar, plate and extrusion plus high-conductivity casting alloys can earn EUR 800-2,000/t [est.]. Our pitch against Chinese magnesium is EU supply security.
6. **Act within 12-18 months.** Volvo Košice, Hyundai and Toyota programmes are locking in alloy specifications now, and Chinese suppliers are 15-20% cheaper. Missing this qualification cycle means waiting until 2030 or later.
