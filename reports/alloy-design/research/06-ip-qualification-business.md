## Alloy IP, Qualification & Business Models

*Scope: how aluminium companies make money from alloy innovation, and which model suits an EU producer with a 300 kt/yr smelter, a casthouse, a rolling mill, extrusion and a planned 100 kt/yr AI scrap-sorting hub. Research note: the shared web-search budget ran out before the EN 573 and CEN registration checks were done. No source was found for licence royalty terms or proprietary-alloy price lists. Figures marked [est.] are reasoned ranges, not sourced facts.*

### 1. Where the money in alloy innovation actually goes

There are four ways to earn money from a new aluminium alloy. They are very different in size.

1. **Selling the metal at a premium (the main one).** Nearly all of the value is earned by selling the metal itself at a premium over commodity grades. Producers in the West rarely license alloys for cash. They use a patent or trademark to stop rivals copying a product, and then charge for the semi-finished product. Novelis (Advanz), Constellium (HSA6, Airware, Aheadd), Hydro (CIRCAL) and Speira (SOUL) all work this way.
   - Constellium's HSA6 extrusion alloy reaches UTS >400 MPa. Constellium says it allows 15–30% weight savings over conventional alloys and is in series production, for example on the Mini Countryman ([ASM/Constellium](https://www.asminternational.org/?p=67640)).
2. **Licensing to other regions.** IP is licensed for markets the owner cannot easily serve itself.
   - Alcoa gave CSMet (Shanghai) the *exclusive* right to produce and sell its C611 EZCast heat-treatment-free die-casting alloy in China. CSMet supplies it to NIO and other OEMs ([Business Wire, Sept 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220913005251/en/Alcoa-announces-innovations-in-alloy-development-and-deployment); [Asian Metal](https://www.asianmetal.com/news/1550678/CSMET-wins-new-aluminum-alloy-material-patent-authorization-of-Alcoa-China)).
   - Rheinfelden Alloys licensed Silafont, Castasil, Castaduct and Castaman worldwide to Nikkei MC Aluminium. Before that, these alloys could only be bought from the Rheinfelden plant ([AL Circle, 2019](https://www.alcircle.com/news/rheinfelden-alloys-signs-global-licensing-agreement-with-mna-nikkei-mc-aluminium-for-production-and-sale-of-its-patented-alloys-47729)).
   - Rusal has owned Aluminium Rheinfelden since 2021 ([Dry Cargo](https://www.drycargomag.com/rusal-completes-acquisition-of-aluminium-rheinfelden)). EU OEMs may therefore prefer a non-Russian-owned alternative for structural casting alloys. That is our opening (see Section 6).
3. **Pure alloy-design houses.** These firms design alloys and license them, with small revenues.
   - QuesTek reported more than $750k in alloy licence fees and royalties, plus $80k in software royalties (a 2015 figure) ([NETL deck](https://www.netl.doe.gov/File%20Library/Events/2015/utsr/Wednesday/Saal.pdf)). It moved into software subscriptions (the ICMD SaaS platform) in 2024 ([QuesTek](https://questek.com/questek-announces-strategic-2023-results/)). Thermo-Calc bought its European operations in 2025.
   - Alloyed (formerly OxMet) combines licensing with its own manufacturing. It reportedly raised a Series B of about $48M in March 2025 ([Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/note/a-chronological-look-at-alloyed-s-growth-and-investment-milestones)), and it is working with Brunel's BCAST on AI design of recycled aluminium alloys ([Brunel](https://www.brunel.ac.uk/news-and-events/news/articles/%ef%bb%bf-525k-to-accelerate-AI-alloy-design)).
   - **Lesson:** a design house alone earns little. The value sits with whoever owns the melt and the customer.
4. **OEMs owning the IP themselves.** Large customers increasingly file their own alloy patents and then buy toll production.
   - Tesla holds US12378642B2, "Aluminum alloys for die casting" ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12378642)). It also has filings for high-conductivity cast alloys and tough structural die-cast alloys ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2020/02/07/tesla-aluminum-alloys-die-casting-in-electric-car-parts/)).
   - Apple holds the family "Aluminum alloys with high strength and cosmetic appeal" (US10208371, US10544493, US10597762, EP3052668) ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10544493)). It says its enclosures use a "100 percent recycled aluminum alloy designed by Apple" ([Apple Newsroom 2023](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2023/04/apple-will-use-100-percent-recycled-cobalt-in-batteries-by-2025/)).
   - In these cases the producer gets **qualified-supplier status rather than IP**. A role as manufacturing partner is the realistic one.

### 2. Patent landscape 2015–2026 (who files)

- **Western rolling and extrusion majors:** Novelis, Constellium and Alcoa/Arconic/Howmet lead their own product niches.
  - Novelis, Alcan and Aleris dominate filings for some 3xxx/5xxx sheet series ([PatSnap 3102](https://discovery.patsnap.com/company/topic-search/3102-aluminium-alloy), [5086](https://discovery.patsnap.com/company/topic-search/5086-aluminium-alloy)).
  - In Al-Li alloy composition, Constellium Ravenswood ranks second behind Boeing ([PatSnap](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/rd-blog/al-li-alloy-composition-patent-snapshot/)).
- **China dominates volume.** Central South University leads Al-Li heat-treatment patents with 34 records, ahead of AVIC BIAM with 21 ([PatSnap](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/rd-blog/al-li-heat-treatment-patent-landscape/)). Zhongwang and other mills are the most active new filers in 5xxx. Chinese inventors also hold US patents on heat-treatment-free die-casting alloys, for example US12110575B2 ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12110575)).
  - [est.] China accounts for more than 60% of aluminium-alloy (IPC C22C21) priority filings 2015–2025, but most are domestic-only. A WIPO/Espacenet query was not run because the search budget ran out.
- **Hydro is a light filer.** PatSnap shows only about 21 patents under one Hydro entity ([PatSnap](https://discovery.patsnap.com/company/hydro-aluminum/)). Hydro competes through brand and carbon (CIRCAL, REDUXA) rather than composition IP.
- **Fastest-growing areas.** These are high-recycled-content and scrap-tolerant 5xxx/6xxx alloys, heat-treatment-free structural die-casting alloys (gigacasting), high-conductivity cast alloys for e-motors and busbars, and additive-manufacturing powders.
  - HRL's 7A77 was the first alloy registered under the Aluminum Association's additive-manufacturing system: 7A77.50 for the powder, 7A77.60L for the printed part ([Light Metal Age](https://www.lightmetalage.com/news/industry-news/3d-printing/article-registration-system-for-aluminum-alloys-used-in-additive-manufacturing/)).

**Implication.** Composition patents on 6xxx alloys are weak, because the "composition window" in a patent is easy to design around. Defensible IP lies in **process and scrap-chemistry-control claims combined with trade secrets** (melt correction, homogenisation, AI-controlled chemistry from sorted scrap). Process IP is also the hardest kind for competitors to reverse-engineer.

### 3. Standards and registration (cheap, but needs commercial supply first)

- **Aluminum Association "Teal Sheets"** (international wrought designations):
  - The alloy must be "offered for sale currently and … supplied in the previous 12 months … in commercial quantities", and its composition must differ from every registered alloy ([AA registration form](https://www.aluminum.org/sites/default/files/2021-11/TEMPLATEWROUGHTALLOYREGISTRATIONFORM20180.docx)).
  - Review: the AA Director of Standards and Technology checks it, then the SCATR committee reviews it for 30 days.
  - The latest edition is the 2025 Teal Sheets ([ANSI](https://webstore.ansi.org/standards/aa/aatealsheets2025)).
  - Cost is negligible. The real hurdle is having **12 months of commercial supply first**, so registration usually comes about 1–2 years after first sales [est.].
- **EN 573-3 / CEN TC 132:** European alloys normally get an AA number through European Aluminium, a signatory to the Declaration of Accord, and are then picked up in EN 573 and EN 755/485 product standards. Timing could not be verified (search budget ran out) [est. 2–4 years before an alloy appears in the EN standards].
- **Strategy:** sell under a trademark first, as HSA6, CIRCAL and Castasil were. Register the AA number once volumes exist. A registered number helps purchasing departments, but it does not create a premium; the trademark plus OEM material specification does.

### 5. Premium levels (what a new alloy can earn in EUR/t)

- **Base pricing context.** Baseline is LME EUR 2,155/t.
  - The North German 6063 billet premium jumped to $1,175–1,250/t on 29 May 2026, from $560–600/t in late February ([AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/european-aluminium-premium-reaches-a-record-472-t-in-march-is-it-a-build-up-from-last-year-or-the-hormuz-crisis-117803)). Platts had it at about $500/t in December 2025 ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/metals/121825-commodities-2026-european-aluminum-billet-faces-weak-demand-duty-changes-cbam)).
  - The duty-paid premium then fell 18% in August 2026 ([AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/europe-s-aluminium-duty-paid-premium-falls-18-in-august-as-easing-geopolitical-risks-erase-war-premium-120701)).
- **Carbon premium alone is thin.** The Fastmarkets P1020A low-carbon differential for Europe (≤4 t CO2e/t) was $25–40/t, up from $0–15/t in 2022 ([Fastmarkets](https://fastmarkets.com/insights/low-carbon-aluminium-differentials-in-europe-widen-for-p1020-vaps)).
- **Proprietary-alloy premiums** on top of the standard value-added premium [est., no price list is published]:
  - Scrap-tolerant or high-recycled branded 6xxx billet or sheet (CIRCAL, Advanz RC types): EUR 50–200/t.
  - High-strength crash extrusion alloys (HSA6 type): EUR 150–400/t, but mostly earned as part value.
  - Proprietary structural HPDC foundry alloys (Castasil/C611 type) versus a standard AlSi10Mg: EUR 150–350/t.
  - High-conductivity cast or busbar alloys: EUR 100–300/t.
  - Aerospace Al-Li plate: 2–4 times the price of conventional plate.
- **Royalty benchmark if we license out.** Typical patent royalties run 1–10% of net sales, with an automotive average of about 3.3% ([UpCounsel](https://www.upcounsel.com:443/patent-licensing-royalty-rates); no source specific to metals). On a EUR 3,000–3,500/t product, that is roughly **EUR 60–140/t royalty** [est.].
- **Recycling context.** Hydro CIRCAL 100R is below 0.5 kg CO2e/kg, and 75R is 1.9 kg ([AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/top-10-low-carbon-aluminium-brands-leading-the-sustainable-revolution-104358)). Novelis was at 63% recycled content in FY2024, with a target of 75% by 2030 ([AL Circle](https://www.alcircle.com/news/novelis-secures-german-ecodesign-award-with-a-breakthrough-100-recycled-aluminium-automotive-sheet-116424)).

### 6. AI's role in this business model

AI does not change the qualification clock much. That clock is set by customer testing. AI does reduce the time and cost of design, and it allows alloys designed for *scrap chemistry windows* rather than for primary metal.

- Tiwari, Mendis and Eskin (J. Sustainable Metallurgy, 2025) used machine learning on 292 datasets to reduce 42 6xxx grades to 10 recycling-friendly alloys ([Brunel](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/33188)).
- Novelis is running AI and phase-transformation modelling for high-recycle alloys, including an AI alloy-discovery proof-of-concept with an outside partner ([Novelis careers](https://careers-novelis.icims.com/jobs/47609/ai-%26-advanced-modeling-co-op/job)). It also partners with Sortera on alloy-by-alloy scrap sorting.
- [est.] The design-to-first-trial cycle falls from 2–3 years to 6–12 months. Lab and trial-cast cost per alloy candidate falls by 30–60%.

**Key insight for us:** the defensible asset is the closed loop of sorted-scrap data, AI-chosen chemistry, casthouse correction and certified properties. Rivals without a sorting hub cannot copy it, even if the composition is published.

### So what for our company

**Recommended model: "own the chemistry window, sell the metal, license only abroad."**

1. **Build our own scrap-tolerant alloy families** for 6xxx extrusion billet, 5xxx/6xxx sheet and structural HPDC foundry alloy. Design them with AI against the composition spread the sorting hub produces.
2. **Protect the IP in layers:**
   - process and chemistry-control patents filed in the EP, US and CN;
   - trade secrets on the sorting-to-melt correction models;
   - a trademark;
   - AA and EN registration once 12 months of commercial supply exist.
3. **Run joint development agreements (JDAs) with 2–3 EU OEMs** (automotive, Tier-1 castings, and a grid conductor customer):
   - the OEM owns application-specific IP, and we keep the composition and process IP;
   - in return for co-funding qualification, the OEM gets 2–3 years of supply exclusivity.
4. **Sell value-added metal** at EUR 100–350/t over standard grades [est.]. **License to non-EU producers** (the Alcoa–CSMet template) for a royalty of about EUR 60–140/t.
5. **Fund 30–50% of the programme** through IAM4EU/Processes4Planet Horizon calls and IPCEI-CAM.
6. **Stay out of aerospace qualification.** It takes too long and too much capital for our product mix. Become a qualified manufacturing partner for OEM-owned alloys in consumer electronics.

Indicative economics [est.]: 60 kt/yr of branded alloys at a EUR 200/t premium gives about EUR 12M/yr extra gross margin. Licensing could add EUR 1–3M/yr. The programme would cost EUR 15–25M over 4 years, before grants.
