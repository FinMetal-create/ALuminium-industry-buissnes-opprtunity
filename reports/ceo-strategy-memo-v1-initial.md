**From:** Chief Strategy Officer

## 1. Executive summary

- **Growth will come from value, scrap and power, not from more tonnes of commodity metal.** EU demand is about **13 Mt/yr** [est., EU policy report]. More than half of EU primary needs are imported. EU primary output has fallen to about **1.2 Mt** (Mysteel, 2025), or about 2 Mt by another agent's estimate. Global primary output is about **72–74 Mt**, and China is at its **45 Mt cap** (IAI). New smelting capacity is going up in Indonesia, the Gulf and India, not here. *The swarm found no published total global demand forecast for 2030 or 2040* (see Section 9). The numbers below are by segment.
- **Electrification is the largest global demand engine.**
  - Electrical-sector aluminium is forecast to grow from **10.4 Mt (2020) to 15.6 Mt (2030)** (CRU/IAI).
  - Grid aluminium demand reaches **~21–27 Mt/yr in 2041–2050** (IEA).
  - Global T&D lines go from **4.9 Mt to 5.7–7.2 Mt by 2040** (ING).
  - EU grid investment is **~€1.2tn to 2040**, of which €730bn is distribution (EU Grids Package, Dec 2025).
  - With the copper/aluminium price ratio at **~4.9x** (S&P Global, 15 Oct 2026), aluminium displaces **~6% of copper demand by 2030** (JPMorgan). That is about **+0.8 Mt Al/yr** globally and **80–120 kt in Europe** [est.].
- **European automotive is still the largest tonnage growth pool, but it is slowing.**
  - Demand rises from **~2.5 Mt (2022) to ~4.2–4.5 Mt (2030) and ~4.8–5.0 Mt (2035)** [est.].
  - Aluminium content per car goes from 205 kg to 256 kg by 2030 (Ducker/European Aluminium).
  - The downside case: ICCT says weaker CO2 rules could cut the 2030 BEV share from 61% to 44%.
  - The new specified market is **gigacasting alloy: ~150–300 kt/yr in Europe by 2030–32** [est.].
- **Defence and aerospace have the least tonnage and the best margins.**
  - EU defence spending was **€418bn in 2025**, rising to **up to €547bn by 2029** (EDA).
  - SAFE (€150bn) requires **≥65% EU/EEA/Ukraine content**.
  - Aerospace and defence plate earns about **USD 1,640–2,080/t EBITDA**, against **~USD 325/t** for can and auto sheet (derived from Constellium).
- **Robotics is a value play, not a volume play.**
  - Robotics aluminium demand in the base case is **~70 kt (2030), ~230 kt (2035) and ~0.8 Mt (2040)** [est.]. That is about 1% of global demand.
  - At €25–40/kg finished, it is a **€20–30bn/yr component value pool by 2040** [est.].
  - CRU's long-run humanoid case is ~10 Mt/yr by 2050.
  - Humanoids hold **17–25 kg of aluminium per unit** (CRU).
- **Scrap is now the scarce input.**
  - The EU exported a record **1.27 Mt of scrap in 2025**, about 25% of EU recycling capacity (European Aluminium; FACE).
  - A non-OECD export restriction from **~2027** could cover up to **~950 kt**.
  - EU post-consumer scrap grows from **3.6 Mt (2019) to 8.6 Mt (2050)** (CAAP).
- **Power decides who is still producing primary metal in 2040.**
  - Every **€10/MWh moves cost by €140–150/t Al**.
  - EU smelter cash cost is **~€2,000/t at €50/MWh and ~€2,560/t at €90/MWh** [est.].
  - Every EU smelter must re-contract power before the **2030–31 contract cliff**, while data centres are bidding US$115+/MWh.
  - Inert anodes (ELYSIS, 450 kA cell since Nov 2025) reach licensed commercial lines only in **2030–2035**.
- **Robotics and AI in our own plants pay back now.**
  - EGA reports **USD 100–123m** of impact from 80+ use cases.
  - The biggest lever is AI and sensor scrap sorting, at **€150–400/t uplift** [est.].
  - AI pot control is worth about **€1.6m/yr per 200 kt** [est.].
  - Robotic anode change saves about **€3.5–4.5m/yr per 200 kt** [est.].
- **2026–2028 is a window of opportunity, not a new normal.**
  - The Gulf shock put **3–3.5 Mt of output at risk** (WoodMac).
  - The Rotterdam duty-paid premium reached a record of about **US$595–600/t in May** and was still **~US$574/t on 1 Oct**.
  - Project Blue expects a **~900 kt global deficit in 2026**.
  - We should treat these prices as speeding up payback, never as the base case.
- **My recommendation:**
  - Commit an H1 envelope of **~€250–450m over 2027–29** [CSO] to seven quick wins.
  - The top three are **(1) an AI scrap-sorting and closed-loop platform, (2) a flexible smelter and a 2031–40 power platform, and (3) defence-secure plate**.
  - Robotics becomes a **combined play**: we sell robot-grade metal and housings to robot OEMs and use automation to cut our own cost.
  - In total, this targets roughly **1.5–2.2 Mt/yr of additional addressable EU demand by 2035** [CSO aggregation of swarm segment estimates; some segments overlap].

## 3. How aluminium will be produced in 2040

**The competitive EU producer in 2040 [CSO synthesis]:**
- **Majority recycled.** About 50% of EU demand could come from post-consumer scrap by 2050 (CAAP). Primary metal is used to sweeten scrap melts and to provide flexible load.
- **Power contracted at ≤€50–55/MWh** for 10–15 years, from a hybrid of PPAs, nuclear contracts and CISAF support.
- **Run as a "virtual battery"** for the grid.
- **AI-controlled** from sorting line to potline, with robots doing the hazardous tasks.

**What this means for us:**
1. **Electricity cost and access to scrap will set our competitiveness. Cell technology will matter less before the mid-2030s.**
2. **Recycling costs much less to build.** It needs €400–1,000/t of capacity against more than €5,000/t for a greenfield smelter [est.], and uses about 5% of the energy. Our smelter's job changes. It becomes a source of clean metal to dilute iron and trace elements in recycled melts, which lets us blend in more scrap than a pure remelter can. It also becomes a flexible grid asset.
3. **CBAM gives primary metal weaker protection than it seems.** It does not cover indirect (electricity) emissions for aluminium, and electricity is 75–85% of a coal smelter's footprint. Our protection comes from certification, local content and supply security.
4. **Automation is a set of separate use cases, each with its own payback.** It is not one big programme. Robotic anode-change investments must pay back within 5 years because of the inert-anode risk.

## 5. Deep dives: top 3

### 5.1 #1 AI scrap-sorting and closed-loop platform

**What to do**
1. **Build two sorting hubs** (50–100 kt/yr each) next to our casthouses. Each hub combines shredding, eddy current, XRT pre-sort and 2–3 LIBS units per 50 kt (TOMRA AUTOSORT PULSE or Steinert PLASMAX), plus **our own AI scrap-chemistry and blending layer** feeding wrought slab and billet. Size them on real throughput of **4–7 t/h per unit**, not the 15 t/h nameplate.
2. **Run them as JVs** with one or two large EU shredders, on the Hydro/PADNOS Alusort model (Hydro paid USD 4m for 50% of a 20 kt JV).
3. **Buy and build regional scrap merchants** in CEE, Iberia and Italy, where merchant margins are €20–60/t [est.]. Add a digital trading and traceability platform.
4. **Sign closed-loop contracts with return guarantees, ASI chain-of-custody and digital product passports**:
   - OEM press-shop offcuts and gigacasting returns (15–30% of shot weight)
   - DSO conductor and cable scrap, through a "circular grid" contract
   - DRS operators
   - Aerospace swarf, via a partnership with Constellium Issoire, Novelis Koblenz, AMAG and Airbus Tier-1 machining shops; buy-to-fly runs at 8–10:1
   - Robot and drone machining chips
   - Dross and salt-slag recovery (40–70% of the metal in dross), as a tolling deal with a Befesa-type partner

**Rough investment [CSO]**
- €40–80m for two hubs.
- €50–100m for a merchant roll-up.
- Phase it: hub 1 at final investment decision by Q2 2027.

**Economics**
- A 100–150 kt hub at €150–400/t uplift is worth **€15–60m/yr gross** [est.].
- The larger gain is replacing purchased primary metal and slab in our rolling and extrusion. Recycled metal can earn €300–600/t more contribution than marginal EU primary [est.].
- Payback is 3–5 years [est.].
- **The business case must work without the export restriction.** India (~383 kt/yr) may be exempted as an FTA partner.

**KPIs**
- Post-consumer share of casthouse feed: target +15 pp by 2029 [CSO]
- Tonnes under contracts of three years or more
- Purity >95% on 5xxx/6xxx fractions
- Uplift versus Twitch/Zorba in €/t
- Scrap purchase discount to LME plus premium: target 10–20%
- Product CO2: ≤2 t CO2e/t grades
- Hub utilisation

### 5.2 #2 Flexible smelter and 2031–2040 power platform

**What to do**
1. **Retrofit one or two potlines** with controllable shell heat exchangers, via the Trimet/University of Wuppertal or EnPot route, to run at **±20–25% load for up to 48 h**.
2. **Add AI pot control and a digital twin** covering bath temperature, AlF3 feeding and anode-effect prediction, with an ARRAY Innovation-type partner.
3. **Add a price-driven optimiser** for day-ahead and intraday markets.
4. **Earn revenue from the flexibility** through aFRR/mFRR balancing, interruptibility contracts and cheaper solar/wind-shaped PPAs. ACER expects the EU to need 2.4x today's flexibility by 2030.
5. **Set up an energy-trading and procurement unit.** Re-contract our 2031–40 needs (about 2.9 TWh/yr per 200 kt) as a hybrid:
   - wind/solar PPAs, using Hydro's ~2.3 TWh/yr of 2031–40 PPAs signed in 2026 as the benchmark
   - a nuclear contract, using EDF–Aluminium Dunkerque (10 years, ~450 MW) as the template
   - the CISAF €50/MWh floor
   - minority equity in generation
6. **Lobby:** extend indirect cost compensation beyond 2030, add CfDs for low-carbon aluminium, and include indirect emissions in CBAM.

**Partners:** EnPot, Trimet (licence), ARRAY Innovation, national TSOs, utilities, and our member-state government for CISAF and indirect cost compensation.

**Rough investment:** €30–40m per line, plus a small trading team. Generation equity is optional.

**Value**
- €3–8m/yr per 200 kt [est.].
- The 137 kWh/t saving is worth about €1.6m/yr per 200 kt at €60/MWh.
- Each €10/MWh is worth about €15m/yr per 100 kt.

**KPIs**
- kWh/kg, moving from 13.5–15.0 toward Karmøy's 12.27
- Modulation range achieved
- Flex revenue in €/t
- Share of 2031–40 power contracted, and its weighted price (target ≤€50–55/MWh)
- Anode-effect frequency
- CISAF/compensation secured

### 5.3 #3 Defence-secure armour and marine plate

**What to do**
1. **Upgrade an existing plate or hot mill** with stretching, solution heat treatment and ultrasonic inspection.
2. **Qualify the products** within 12–24 months to national and NATO standards:
   - 5083-H131 and 7020/7017/7039 armour plate
   - 5083/5383 marine plate
   - hard-alloy extrusions for launchers, bridging and vehicle sub-structures
3. **Sell through take-or-pay frameworks** with traceable EU-origin metal from smelter to semi at <4 kg CO2e/kg. Offer strategic stock held on our sites, and push for a European equivalent of the US IDIQ plate contracts.
4. **Add a small drone and C-UAS line** of 7xxx plate and machined kits: motor bells and mounts, launchers, C-UAS turrets and larger airframes. Most small-drone frames are CFRP.

**Partners and customers**
- Land: KNDS, Rheinmetall, BAE Hägglunds, Patria
- Naval: Naval Group, Fincantieri, TKMS, Damen, and the European Combat Vessel programme
- Drones and C-UAS: Helsing, Quantum Systems, Stark, MBDA/Rheinmetall programmes
- Possibly a defence machining house

**Rough investment:** €30–60m placeholder [CSO]. This has to be checked by an engineering study within 90 days.

**Economics**
- Moving 10 kt from can or commodity sheet (~USD 325/t) to defence plate (USD 1,500–2,000/t) adds **USD 12–17m EBITDA**.
- Ambition: 20–30 kt by 2029, worth **USD 25–50m EBITDA** [CSO].

**KPIs**
- Qualifications obtained (number and alloys)
- Signed framework value
- Share of SAFE-funded programmes served
- EBITDA/t
- On-time delivery and surge capacity

**Watch-outs**
- Most defence buying is national; only 24% is collaborative.
- US DFARS rules.
- A ceasefire would not cut budgets that are locked to 2035 NATO targets, but it could slow urgency.

### 6(a) Robotics as a demand market

**What the numbers say**
- **Humanoids drive all the tonnage that matters.** Goldman Sachs (Sep 2026) forecasts 890k units in 2030 and 6.5M in 2035. Morgan Stanley forecasts an installed base of ~134–138M by 2040. Aluminium content is 17–25 kg per unit (CRU), or 20–30 kg including housings and thermal parts [est.].
- **The rest is small.** Industrial robots (542k installed in 2024), cobots (57k to 129k by 2030), AMRs, exoskeletons and drones together add only about 40–70 kt by 2030 [est.].
- **China dominates.** It makes ~90% of humanoids and is moving to **magnesium** skeletons (AgiBot). The EU depends on China for 95–97% of its magnesium, so "EU recyclable aluminium versus China-dependent magnesium" is our sales pitch.

**Our moves**
1. **Robot-grade materials line (#6).** Certified tubes, billet, plate and thermal alloys sold at a premium, with a low-CO2 passport.
2. **Tier-2 actuator housing M&A (#9).** Becomes our route into Schaeffler (humanoid actuators up to 250 Nm; Neura partner; Agility stake), Harmonic Drive SE, Maxon and Bosch Rexroth. As volumes grow, parts shift from machined billet to near-net die casting (Samkee's frame contract), and that shift favours a casthouse-integrated player.
3. **Thermal-function parts.** Structure combined with heat sinking is where aluminium beats Mg, CFRP and PEEK. The same alloys serve data centres and power electronics, which spreads the risk.
4. **Machining-chip take-back.** Semi-finished demand is 1.5–3x net content, so swarf flows straight back into our sorting and remelt loop (#1).
5. **Sovereign drone and C-UAS metal**, folded into #3.

**Gate:** keep robotics capex in the tens of millions of euros until **global humanoid output passes 250k/yr by 2028** and **a Western OEM signs a multi-year housing contract**.

### The combined play: "Robotics in, robotics out"

1. **Our plants become reference sites for European robotics.** We buy automation from the same EU OEMs we sell to (KUKA, ABB, UR, Schaeffler-linked actuator chains, Neura). That gives us reciprocal leverage in commercial talks and proves our robot-grade metal in our own robots.
2. **A closed material loop with robot makers.** We supply robot-grade semis and housings, take back their machining chips, sort them in our AI hubs and remelt them into low-CO2 robot-passport metal. That gives OEMs Scope-3 and CBAM data, and gives us cheap, clean feed.
3. **Robotics in recycling.** Robotic EV-pack dismantling and alloy-specific picking (options shelf) raise the purity of wrought scrap. That links the robotics theme straight to our most valuable opportunity.
4. **An external technology business later.** After 2–3 years of proven use, package the sorting recipes, alloy digital twin, flex optimiser and casthouse vision as a Hycast-style business. Hycast precedents: NOK 200m with Innovation Group; €9.7m with Alu Iberica. EU Apply AI and Horizon calls can co-fund it (€18m AI-robotics call).
5. **Single owner:** a "Robotics & Automation" programme director reporting to the CEO, with joint targets: external robotics revenue plus internal savings.

## 9. Where the research disagrees or is uncertain

1. **There is no global total demand forecast.** None of the eight agents gave a sourced figure for global aluminium demand in 2030 or 2040. The figures we have are segment forecasts plus primary production of ~72–74 Mt. My EU "1.5–2.2 Mt incremental by 2035" is an aggregation of segment estimates and double-counts some items: data-centre grid demand overlaps with grid demand, and BESS overlaps with auto and enclosures. **We should buy a CRU/IAI demand outlook before the board paper.**
2. **EU primary output.** The figure is ~1.2 Mt in 2025 (Mysteel) versus ~2 Mt [est., policy agent]. The gap may be a matter of definition (EU27 vs EU+EFTA, ~4 Mt incl. Norway and Iceland), but it needs to be resolved.
3. **Low-carbon premium.**
   - Fastmarkets P1020A was US$0–10/t, with value-added products at US$20–30/t (last public data, ~late 2024).
   - The policy agent gives "US$10–40/t, verify".
   - The recycling agent estimates €50–300/t, and another agent estimates €50–150/t bilateral.
   - **Treat the market premium as close to zero. Bilateral premiums are not proven.**
4. **Prices.**
   - The 2026 LME peak is US$3,418/t (ING) versus US$3,545.5/t (Fastmarkets).
   - The Rotterdam premium "US$250–450/t" (policy agent, unverified) conflicts with the sourced records of ~US$595–600/t (May) and US$574/t (1 Oct).
5. **CBAM cost on imports.** Estimates are €15–30/t (AL Circle interview) versus ~€40/t (agent model) for 2026. Both rise steeply to 2034. The methods differ: benchmark versus default values.
6. **Content per car and car volumes.** Ducker gives 283 kg (2022) to ~310 kg (2026) per BEV. The recycling agent gives 200–250 kg [est.] and "11–12m EU vehicles" against S&P's 17 Mu for Europe. I used Ducker and S&P.
7. **Gigacasting alloy market size.** One agent estimates 150–300 kt (2030–32). Another estimates 0.3–0.6 Mt for all EU structural casting alloy by 2035. Both are estimates, and VW's 16-press order is **reported, not confirmed**.
8. **Defence aluminium demand (150–250 kt to 300–450 kt).** This is an author estimate with **no published source**. The opportunity rests on margins and policy, not on this tonnage.
9. **Robotics tonnage spread.** The range is very wide:
   - Humanoid aluminium in 2040 is 0.18–1.8 Mt (agent model).
   - CRU's ~10 Mt by 2050 assumes 400M units/yr.
   - A Chinese trade source claims 100–125 kt by 2030, which would need ~4–5M units.
   - Goldman Sachs raised its 2035 forecast 5x in one revision.
   - **This uncertainty is why the robotics plays are milestone-gated.**
10. **Drone counts.** Ukraine's 2025 production is put at 2.5–4M by one report and >4M by another. Aluminium tonnage is small whichever figure is right.
11. **CO2 footprints.** EU primary is put at ~4 t cradle-to-gate (green-primary agent) versus 6–7 t [est.] (recycling agent). The recycled ~0.5 t figure is an IAI benchmark that was not re-checked.
12. **Verification gaps.**
    - The EU policy, markets and advanced-manufacturing report was written **without live source fetches** because the search budget was used up.
    - The recycling report ran out of search budget partway through and relies mainly on estimates.
    - The robotics report relied on search extracts.
    - Vendor claims are unverified: AlumatIQ, HybrEx, EnPot, LIBS nameplate rates.
    - Most internal economics are [est.]: the sorting uplift of €150–400/t, the €3–8m/yr flex value, and recycling capex of €400–1,000/t.
    - **None of these figures should go to the board until they are checked against primary data.**
13. **Policy outcomes not yet decided.**
    - The final scope of the scrap export restriction, including the FTA exemptions.
    - The final content of the IAA.
    - Whether low-carbon aluminium enters the 2035 car CO2 credits.
    - The status of the CBAM downstream extension.
    - Each of these could move the value of #1, #5 and #7 a lot. The H1 business cases are built to stand without them.
